# Statistical Gates for Adaptive Multi-Agent Strategy Search: A Literature Review and Architectural Audit (2023–2026)

Your statistical gating architecture addresses one of the most demanding problems in quantitative finance: maintaining strict false-discovery control when autonomous Large Language Model (LLM) agents adaptively generate, inspect, and refine trading strategies over dependent, heavy-tailed return streams. Evaluated against the 2023–2026 statistical and financial econometrics literature, your current two-stage design—using White’s Reality Check (WRC), Hansen’s Superior Predictive Ability (SPA), and Romano–Wolf StepM on the search sample, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) as a heuristic, and a predictable plug-in Kelly betting process capped at 1/2 with inverse running-maximum p-values combined via Holm on the held-out sample—is substantially more rigorous than standard industry workflows, yet exhibits **four foundational mathematical vulnerabilities and power inefficiencies**.

First, **on the search sample, WRC, SPA, and Romano–Wolf StepM are static batch procedures whose asymptotic guarantees break under adaptive sequential selection** ([<u>Benhamou et al., 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03384v1); [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051)). Counting every evaluation inside StepM adjusts for the *cardinality* of the tested set, but not for the *adaptivity* of the query tree when multiple agents condition their next proposal on a shared transcript of prior outcomes ([<u>Dwork et al., 2015</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)). Second, **on the held-out sample, a predictable plug-in Kelly bet is only a valid test supermartingale if conditional expected returns are non-positive at every step (\mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 0)** ([<u>Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2024</u>](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Events/Discussion%20meetings/Estimating-means-of-bounded-random-variables-by-betting.pdf)). Under serial correlation (momentum or mean-reversion autocorrelation), a strategy with zero unconditional alpha can exhibit positive conditional drift in specific states, breaking the supermartingale property and invalidating Ville’s inequality unless the return stream is predictably pre-whitened ([<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051)) or evaluated in a coarsened filtration via e-lifting adjusters ([<u>Choe & Ramdas, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09698)). Third, **heavy tails and strategy bankruptcy (R_t \to -1 or R_t \< -1 under leverage or shorting) violate or destabilize linear plug-in Kelly betting**, requiring either volatility-scaled clipped payoff scores ([<u>Benhamou et al., 2026</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf)), Catoni-style influence supermartingales ([<u>Wang & Ramdas, 2023</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01250)), or one-sided sub-exponential \psi_E supermartingales ([<u>Howard et al., 2020</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240)). Fourth, **converting holdout e-processes into running-maximum p-values (1/\sup_t M_t) and applying classical Holm (p-Holm) is provably dominated and sacrifices substantial statistical power** compared to native e-value closed testing (e-Holm, Closed e-BH, or online e-BH) applied to stopped e-values ([<u>Hartog & Lei, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015); [<u>Xu et al., 2025</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02517); [<u>Tavyrikov, Goeman, & de Heide, 2026</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf)).

## 1. State of the Art (2023–2026) in E-Values, E-Processes, and Testing by Betting

Between 2023 and 2026, game-theoretic probability and sequential anytime-valid inference (SAVI) matured from a collection of martingale concentration inequalities into a complete statistical theory ([<u>Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk, & Shafer, 2023</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-38/issue-4/Game-Theoretic-Statistics-and-Safe-Anytime-Valid-Inference/10.1214/23-STS894.pdf); [<u>Grünwald, de Heide, & Koolen, 2024</u>](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/safe-testing/); [<u>Ramdas & Wang, 2025</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23614)). Given a composite null hypothesis \mathcal{P} (a set of probability measures on a filtered space (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}\_t)\_{t \ge 0})), an **e-variable** is a nonnegative random variable E satisfying \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}\_P\[E\] \le 1. An **e-process** (E_t)\_{t \ge 0} is a nonnegative adapted process satisfying \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}\_P\[E\_\tau\] \le 1 for every (potentially infinite, data-dependent) stopping time \tau. Every nonnegative test supermartingale starting at M_0 = 1 (satisfying \mathbb{E}\_P\[M_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le M\_{t-1} for all P \in \mathcal{P}) is an e-process, and by **Ville’s (1939) inequality**, any e-process satisfies the time-uniform Type-I error bound \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} P(\sup\_{t \ge 0} E_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha for all \alpha \in (0,1).

### 1.1 Log-Optimality, the Numeraire, and Lower-Tail Type-II Error Bounds

Unlike Neyman–Pearson testing, which maximizes rejection probability at a fixed sample size and can assign positive probability to E = 0 (causing permanent bankruptcy when evidence is multiplied sequentially), the canonical power criterion for e-values under an alternative Q is **e-power** (expected logarithmic growth rate), \mathbb{E}\_Q\[\log E\], known as the Kelly (1956) or Growth-Rate Optimal (GRO / GROW) criterion ([<u>Grünwald, de Heide, & Koolen, 2024</u>](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Events/Discussion%20meetings/Preprint-Grunwald-24-Jan-2024.pdf); [<u>Vovk & Wang, 2024</u>](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.01659)).

Three foundational theoretical results from 2025–2026 govern log-optimality and Type-II error in sequential betting tests. First, in *The Numeraire E-Variable and Reverse Information Projection* ([<u>NSF PAR 10613062, 2025</u>](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10613062), **Theorem 2.6 and Theorem 4.1**), the authors prove that for *any* composite null hypothesis \mathcal{P} and point alternative Q, under **no regularity, moment, tail, or dominating reference measure conditions whatsoever**, there exists a Q-almost surely strictly positive e-variable X^\*—called the **numeraire**—satisfying \mathbb{E}\_Q\[X / X^\*\] \le 1 for every e-variable X \in \mathcal{E}. By Jensen's inequality, the numeraire is uniquely log-optimal (\mathbb{E}\_Q\[\log(X/X^\*)\] \le 0), and the subprobability measure P^\* defined via dP^\*/dQ = 1/X^\* provides the general **Reverse Information Projection (RIPr)** onto the effective null \mathcal{P}\_{\text{eff}}.

Second, when the alternative Q is composite or unknown in sequential testing, [<u>Waudby-Smith, Sandoval, & Jordan (2025/2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02818v1) (arXiv:2504.02818, **Theorem 2.1**) prove that e-processes constructed using online portfolio selection algorithms with sublinear portfolio regret—such as Cover’s (1991) Universal Portfolio, Cover–Ordentlich (1996), or [<u>Orabona & Jun (2023)</u>](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.02818v1)—achieve **distribution-uniform universal log-optimality**, converging almost surely and in expectation to the oracle constant-betting growth rate across entire classes of alternatives without requiring uniform moment assumptions. Their theoretical and simulation comparisons show that restricted-range plug-in or Online Newton Step (ONS) bettors become suboptimal whenever the true optimal betting fraction falls outside their pre-specified parameter range.

Third, while Ville's inequality guarantees Type-I error control regardless of tail behavior, [<u>Forré (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27766) (arXiv:2609.27766) proves a critical negative result for Type-II error: **strictly positive conditional e-power (\mathbb{E}\_\[span_32\](start_span)\[span_32\](end_span){P_1}\[\log E_n \mid \mathcal{F}\_{n-1}\] \ge c \> 0) does not by itself guarantee finite-horizon or sequential Type-II error control**. If the lower tail of \log E_n carries heavy probability mass, a single **catastrophic bet** can wipe out arbitrary accumulated capital, yielding sequential Type-II error arbitrarily close to 1 at horizon t. **Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 3.8** of [<u>Forré (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27766) prove a lossless master inequality: given a predictable certified drift \sum\_{n \le t} c_n \ge C_t and a predictable conditional cumulant generating function (CGF) majorant \log \mathbb{E}\_{P_1}\[e^{-s(\log E_n - c_n)} \mid \mathcal{F}\_{n-1}\] \le \psi_n(s) with deterministic aggregate \Psi^{(t)}(s), the sequential Type-II error \bar{\gamma}\_t(\alpha) = P_1(\tau\_\alpha \> t) satisfies:

\bar{\gamma}\_t(\alpha) \le \gamma_t(\alpha) \le \exp\left(-(\Psi^{(t)})^\star\left((C_t - \log(1/\alpha))^+\right)\right)

where (\Psi^{(t)})^\star(x) = \sup\_{s \ge 0} \\sx - \Psi^{(t)}(s)\\ is the one-sided Legendre transform. Guaranteeing fast detection therefore requires enforcing a lower-tail wealth floor or moment bound (Rungs 1–5 of Forré's ladder, such as Bennett/Freedman bounded-floor variance bounds or bounded log-increments).

### 1.2 Heavy Tails and Bounded-Below Returns

Trading strategy returns occupy a delicate statistical regime: unlevered long-only or limited-liability strategies are **bounded below** (R_t \ge -1, where -1 represents complete bankruptcy) while exhibiting **heavy upper tails** (and heavy lower tails up to -1, or beyond -1 under leverage or shorting). The 2023–2026 literature establishes sharp boundaries across five distinct tail regimes.

In the **unbounded, unknown-variance regime**, [<u>Martinez-Taboada, Chugg, & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06332) (arXiv:2606.06332) emphasize that for i.i.d. random variables X_1, \dots, X_n with mean \mu and finite variance \sigma^2 \in (0, \infty) that has **no a priori known upper bound**, there is **no known nontrivial nonasymptotic e-value** for H_0: \mu \le \theta, and **conjecture that none exist**. Consequently, exact nonasymptotic e-processes require either bounded support, a known lower bound paired with self-normalization, or an a priori moment upper bound.

In the **two-sided bounded regime** (X_t \in \[a,b\] or \[0,1\]), [<u>Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Events/Discussion%20meetings/Estimating-means-of-bounded-random-variables-by-betting.pdf) (*JRSS Series B* 86(1): 1–27) prove that every test martingale for H_0: \mathbb{E}\[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] = m takes the multiplicative betting form M_t(m) = \prod\_{i=1}^t (1 + \lambda_i(X_i - m)) where \lambda_i is \mathcal{F}\_{i-1}-measurable and restricted so that each factor remains nonnegative. They introduce three primary variance-adaptive constructions: (1) **Predictable Plug-In Empirical Bernstein (PrPl-EB, Theorem 2)**, M_t^{\text{PrPl-EB}}(m) = \prod\_{i=1}^t \exp\\\lambda_i(X_i - m) - v_i \psi_E(\lambda_i)\\ with v_i = 4(X_i - \hat{\mu}\_{i-1})^2, \psi_E(\lambda) = (-\log(1-\lambda) - \lambda)/4, and capped predictable bet \lambda_t = \sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\alpha)}{\hat{b}\_{t-1}^2 t \log(1+t)}} \wedge c for c \in \\1/2, 3/4\\; (2) **Approximate GRAPA (aGRAPA) and ONS-m**, which maximize a Taylor expansion of log-wealth to yield the truncated empirical Kelly ratio; and (3) **GridKelly / Universal Portfolio**, which averages capital processes across a grid of \lambda values to guarantee convex confidence sets and universal log-optimality.

In the **one-sided bounded-below regime with heavy upper tails** (X_t \ge -c for c \> 0, \mathbb{E}\_{t-1}\[X_t^2\] \< \infty), linear betting 1 + \lambda_t X_t remains a valid supermartingale for \lambda_t \in \[0, 1/c\] under H_0: \mathbb{E}\[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 0 ([<u>Proposition 2 of Runtime Barrier Auditing, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27726)). However, heavy upper tails destabilize unnormalized linear bets. [<u>Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, & Sekhon (2020/2021)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240) (*Probability Surveys* 17: 257–347) prove that for X_t \ge -c with \mathbb{E}\_{t-1}\[X_t\] \le 0, **Fan’s one-sided sub-exponential (\psi_E) supermartingale**:

L_t(\lambda) = \exp\left\\\lambda \sum\_{i=1}^t X_i - \psi\_{E,c}(\lambda) \sum\_{i=1}^t X_i^2\right\\, \quad \psi\_{E,c}(\lambda) = \frac{-\log(1 - c\lambda) - c\lambda}{c^2}, \quad \lambda \in \[span_136\](start_span)\[span_137\](start_span)\[span_138\](start_span)\[0, 1/c)

is an exact nonnegative supermartingale requiring **no upper bound on X_t and no exponential moments**—only finite conditional second moments (or using Self-Normalized II: Z_t = \frac{1}{2}\sum\_{i=1}^t ((X_i)\_+^2 + \mathbb{E}\_{i-1}(X_i)\_-^2)).

In the **two-sided heavy-tailed regime** (where leverage or shorting allows X_t to be unbounded below as well as above), linear multiplicative bets 1 + \lambda_t X_t can become negative and are **invalid**. [<u>Wang & Ramdas (2023)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01250) (*Stochastic Processes and their Applications* 163: 168–202, **Theorem 9 and Corollaries 10.1–10.2**) prove that if \mathbb{E}\[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] = \mu and \mathbb{E}\[(X_t - \mu)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le \sigma^2 for a known variance upper bound \sigma^2 (allowing infinite third and fourth moments), applying a **Catoni influence function** \varphi(x) = -\text{sign}(x)\log(1 - \|x\| + x^2/2) for \|x\| \le 1 (and \text{sign}(x)\log 2 for \|x\| \> 1) yields a valid test supermartingale that achieves the Law of the Iterated Logarithm (LIL) shrinkage rate O(\sqrt{\log\log t / t}) and logarithmic confidence scaling O(\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}) instead of Chebyshev’s O(\alpha^{-1/2}). For distributions with only a finite (1+\varepsilon)-moment (\beta = 1+\varepsilon \in (1,2)), fractional Catoni influence functions \psi_2(x) bounded by \pm \log(1 \pm x + \|x\|^\beta/\beta) extend this construction ([<u>Sun, 2025</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11054v1); [<u>Heavy-Tailed Bandits, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10529v1)).

Finally, when no support bound or prior variance bound \sigma^2 is known and large-sample guarantees suffice, [<u>Ignatiadis, Wang, & Ramdas (2025)</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19812v4) defined *asymptotic e-values* (\limsup\_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\_P\[E_n\] \le 1) using the self-normalized t-statistic Z_n(\theta) = \frac{\sum\_{i=1}^n (X_i - \theta)}{\sqrt{\sum\_{i=1}^n (X_i - \theta)^2}} via E_n^\infty(\theta;\lambda) = \exp(\lambda Z_n(\theta) - \lambda^2/2). Because optimizing \lambda requires Z_n(\theta) \ge \sqrt{2\log(1/\delta)} (2.448 at \delta = 0.05) rather than the normal CLT quantile z\_\delta (the "missing factor" discrepancy), [<u>Martinez-Taboada, Chugg, & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06332) (**Theorems 4.1–4.3**) construct **Bentkus-type asymptotic e-values** E_n^\alpha(\theta; \lambda) = \frac{(Z_n(\theta) - \lambda)\_+^\alpha}{\mathbb{E}\[(Z - \lambda)\_+^\alpha\]} (Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1), \alpha \in \\1, 2\\), proving asymptotic validity for any distribution in the domain of attraction of a Gaussian and lowering the \delta = 0.05 threshold to 2.063 (a 15.7% reduction).

### 1.3 Dependent Data (Time Series, Autocorrelation, GARCH, and Filtration Lifting)

E-values and e-processes treat dependence differently across hypotheses versus across time. Across hypotheses (H_1, \dots, H_K), merging via arithmetic averaging and multiple testing via e-BH or e-Holm require **no assumptions** on joint dependence. Across time (t = 1, 2, \dots, T) within a single strategy’s e-process, however, a product process M_t = \prod\_{s=1}^t E_s is a test supermartingale **if and only if each step factor satisfies the conditional expectation inequality \mathbb{E}\_P\[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 1**.

When a strategy's excess return satisfies the martingale difference null \mathbb{E}\_P\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 0, **volatility clustering (ARMA-GARCH, stochastic volatility, and regime shifts) does not violate Type-I error control** ([<u>Wang, Wang, & Ziegel, 2024</u>](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/112/1/asae049/7796539); [<u>Di Leonforte & Deliu, 2025</u>](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/stapro/v226y2025ics0167715225001609.html)). However, [<u>Oestmann & Dickhaus (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05089) (arXiv:2609.05089) and [<u>Xue et al. (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27726) (arXiv:2610.27726) demonstrate that under dynamic volatility, standard infinite-horizon plug-in Kelly bets (\lambda_t estimated from s = 1, \dots, t-1) are diluted by long calm periods, causing severe power loss during active bursts. Replacing the expanding average with a **finite-horizon rolling window bet (T-FH GREM, window T = 50)** ([<u>Oestmann & Dickhaus, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05089)) or **Adam-style exponentially discounted empirical moments** ([<u>Xue et al., 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27726)) preserves exact \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}-predictability (and thus exact Type-I control) while increasing detection power at threshold \tau = 20 (\alpha = 0.05) from 2.78% to 18.80%.

By contrast, **serial correlation in the conditional mean (autocorrelation) directly violates the supermartingale condition**. If strategy returns exhibit serial correlation (\text{Corr}(R_t, R\_{t-1}) = \rho \ne 0 from overlapping holding periods, illiquidity, or momentum), then even when the unconditional mean is zero (\mathbb{E}\[R_t\] = 0), the conditional mean \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] is positive whenever \rho R\_{t-1} \> 0, allowing adaptive bets \lambda_t \> 0 to inflate \mathbb{E}\[M_t\] \> 1. Three proven remedies exist in the 2024–2026 literature:

1.  **Predictable Pre-Whitening:** [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051) (arXiv:2609.27051, Section 4) bet on the predictably whitened stream \tilde{X}\_t = X_t - \hat{\rho}\_t X\_{t-1} where \hat{\rho}\_t \in \mathcal{F}\_{t-1} is a shrunk, capped AR(1) estimate computed from past outcomes X\_{\<t}, adjusting the stake cap to \lambda\_{\max,t} = \frac{1}{1 + \hat{\rho}\_t}.

2.  **Running-Average Weak Null via Empirical Bernstein:** [<u>Choe & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.0792) (*Operations Research* 72(4): 1368–1387) prove that instead of testing the strong null \mathbb{E}\_{t-1}\[\delta_t\] \le 0 \\\forall t via linear betting (which [<u>Waudby-Smith et al., 2024</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377870735_Anytime-valid_off-policy_inference_for_contextual_bandits) show fails to cover time-varying means), centering an exponential empirical Bernstein supermartingale around the cumulative drift tests the weak average null \frac{1}{t}\sum\_{i=1}^t \ma\[span_139\](start_span)\[span_139\](end_span)thbb{E}\_{i-1}\[\delta_i\] \le 0.

3.  **Lag-h Subsampling and Filtration Coarsening (E-Lifting):** For multi-step lag-h dependent series ([<u>Henzi & Ziegel, 2022</u>](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/109/3/647/6375942); [<u>Grob, Daniele, & Ziegel, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23064)), or when an e-process (E_t) is valid only with respect to a coarsened sub-filtration \mathcal{G}\_t \subset \mathcal{F}\_t, [<u>Choe & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09698) (*JRSS Series B*, arXiv:2402.09698v5) prove that (E_t) is **not** valid at \mathcal{F}\_t-stopping times. Their **Lifting Lemma and E-Lifting Theorem** prove that while p-processes lift freely across filtrations (\mathcal{G}\_t \to \mathcal{F}\_t), lifting an e-process to the finer data filtration \mathcal{F}\_t requires applying an **admissible adjuster** A: \[1,\infty\] \to \[0,\infty\] satisfying \int_1^\infty \frac{A(x)}{x^2}dx = 1 (such as A(x) = \frac{(x-1)^2}{x(1+\ln x)}) to the running maximum E_t^\* = \max\_{s \le t} E_s.

### 1.4 Adaptive Selection of Hypotheses

E-values provide three proven mechanisms for adaptive hypothesis selection. First, for **post-selection inference under unknown selection rules**, [<u>Xu, Wang, & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journalArticle/Download?urlId=10.1214/24-EJS2253) (*Electronic Journal of Statistics* 18: 2292–2338, arXiv:2203.12572) prove that if each candidate i \in \[K\] has an e-confidence interval C_i(\alpha) = \\\theta : E_i(\theta) \< 1/\alpha\\ (or confidence sequence), and an agent selects a subset of finalists S = \mathcal{S}(X) \subseteq \[K\] via an **arbitrary, unknown data-dependent selection rule**, the **e-BY procedure** reports C_i(\delta \|S\| / K) for each i \in S. Unlike the p-value Benjamini–Yekutieli (BY) procedure, which must penalize confidence levels by \sum\_{k=1}^K 1/k \approx \ln K when the selection rule or dependence is unknown, e-BY achieves exact False Coverage Rate (\text{FCR} \le \delta) control under arbitrary dependence with **zero logarithmic penalty**. Second, for **post-hoc level selection ("roving \alpha")**, [<u>Grünwald (2024)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Aaditya-Ramdas-2042888522), [<u>Koning (2024/2025)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015), and [<u>Chugg et al. (2026)</u>](https://scholar.google.com.ua/citations?user=FnyNlFAAAAAJ&hl=ca) (arXiv:2603.08002) prove that e-values are necessary and sufficient for risk control under data-dependent significance levels \tilde{\alpha}. Third, for **online adaptive streams (generator–verifier loops)** where LLM agents generate hypotheses H_1, H_2, \dots sequentially, [<u>Xu & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24792v1) (*AISTATS 2024*, e-LOND), [<u>Fischer, Xu, & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2407.20683) (arXiv:2407.20683, online e-BH), [<u>Xu, Fischer, & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v337/main/assets/xu26a/xu26a.pdf) (Donation e-LOND), and [<u>Hegazy, Jordan, & Dieuleveut (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06432) (arXiv:2610.06432) prove that allocating an error budget sequence \sum\_{t=1}^\infty \gamma_t = 1 across generated candidates controls \mathbb{E}\[\sup\_{t \ge 1} \text{FDP}(R_t)\] \le \alpha (or lifetime FWER via weighted Ville bounds in [<u>Benhamou et al., 2026</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf)) even when the generator's t-th hypothesis and stopping time \tau depend arbitrarily on all prior evaluations.

## 2. Multiple Testing with E-Values: e-BH, e-Closure, and Comparison with Holm on E-Processes

In multiple testing across m candidate trading strategies, test statistics are strongly correlated because strategies share underlying market, sector, and style exposures. E-values fundamentally alter the multiple-testing trade-off under such arbitrary dependence.

### 2.1 The e-BH Procedure and Compound E-Values Under Arbitrary Dependence

Introduced by [<u>Wang & Ramdas (2022)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388068094_Family-wise_Error_Rate_Control_with_E-values), the **e-BH procedure** takes m e-values e_1, \dots, e_m, orders them e\_{(1)} \ge e\_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge e\_{(m)}, and rejects the hypotheses corresponding to the k^\* largest e-values, where:

k^\* = \max\left\\k \in \[m\] : e\_{(k)} \ge \frac{m}{\alpha k}\right\\ \quad (\text{with } \max \emptyset = 0)

Equivalently, e-BH applies the classical Benjamini–Hochberg (1995) step-up rule to the reciprocal p-values p_i = 1/e_i. However, whereas classical BH on p-values requires independence or Positive Regression Dependence on a Subset (PRDS)—and otherwise requires the Benjamini–Yekutieli (2001) harmonic correction \sum\_{i=1}^m 1/i \approx \ln m—**[<u>Wang & Ramdas (2022)</u>](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~aramdas/ebook-final.pdf) prove (Theorem 9.13) that e-BH controls the False Discovery Rate at \text{FDR} \le \frac{m_0}{m}\alpha \le \alpha under completely arbitrary dependence with zero harmonic penalty**.

The proof follows directly from the deterministic inequality \mathbf{1}\\X \ge t\\ \le X/t: for any rejection set R = R\_{\text{e-BH}}(e) of size k^\* = \|R\|, every rejected hypothesis satisfies e_j \ge \frac{m}{\alpha(\|R\| \vee 1)}, so:

\text{FDR} = \sum\_{j \in \mathcal{H}\_0} \mathbb{E}\left\[\frac{\mathbf{1}\\j \in R\\}{\|R\| \vee 1}\right\] = \sum\_{j \in \mathcal{H}\_0} \mathbb{E}\left\[\frac{\mathbf{1}\\e_j \ge \frac{m}{\alpha(\|R\| \vee 1)}\\}{\|R\| \vee 1}\right\] \le \frac{\alpha}{m} \sum\_{j \in \mathcal{H}\_0} \mathbb{E}\[e_j\] \le \frac{m_0}{m}\alpha

This derivation only requires the **compound e-value** condition \sum\_{j \in \mathcal{H}\_0} \mathbb{E}\[e_j\] \le m ([<u>Ignatiadis, Wang, & Ramdas, 2025</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19812v4), arXiv:2409.19812v4), who prove (**Theorem 4.4**) that *every* FDR-controlling procedure is equivalent to applying e-BH to a vector of compound e-values.

### 2.2 The E-Closure Principle (2025–2026) and Power Boosting

Although e-BH is valid under arbitrary dependence, it is conservative because self-consistency spends at most m_0 \alpha / m of the FDR budget and Markov's inequality leaves slack when e-values overshoot m/(\alpha k). Between 2024 and 2026, three major techniques were proven to strictly dominate standard e-BH.

First, [<u>Xu, Solari, Fischer, de Heide, Ramdas, & Goeman (2025)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02517) (arXiv:2509.02517, **Theorem 6 and Theorem 48**) establish the **e-Closure Principle**, a necessary and sufficient principle for any expected-loss error metric \mathbb{E}\[f_N(R)\] (including FDR where f_S(R) = \frac{\|S \cap R\|}{\|R\| \vee 1}, and FWER where f_S(R) = \mathbf{1}\\S \cap R \ne \emptyset\\). Given an e-collection (e_S)\_{S \subseteq \[m\]} where e_S is a valid e-value for the intersection null H_S = \bigcap\_{i \in S} H_i (under arbitrary dependence, e_S = \frac{1}{\|S\|}\sum\_{i \in S} e_i), a discovery set R \subseteq \[m\] is valid at level \alpha if and only if e_S \ge f_S(R)/\alpha for all S \subseteq \[m\]. Applying e-closure to e-BH produces **Closed e-BH**, which **provably rejects a superset of standard e-BH** (R\_{\text{Closed-eBH}} \supseteq R\_{\text{e-BH}}) and grants simultaneous post-hoc flexibility over R, \alpha, and the error metric. Moreover, the **Donation Framework** of [<u>Xu, Fischer, & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v337/main/assets/xu26a/xu26a.pdf) (xu26a, **Proposition 7 & Theorem 8**) reduces offline computation to O(m \log m) and online Donation e-LOND computation to O(\log t).

Second, if external randomization is permissible, [<u>Xu & Ramdas (2023/2025)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/417413146_Optimal_Certified_Multiple_Testing_with_E-values) showed that dividing all e-values by a single independent uniform random variable U \sim \text{Unif}(0,1) (U-eBH) preserves exact FDR control under arbitrary dependence. [<u>Bai & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35821) (arXiv:2610.35821) introduce **Recertified Closed e-BH (RC-eBH)**, which explicitly computes and spends the unused conditional risk budget of closed e-BH, proving pathwise nesting: R\_{\text{e-BH}} \subseteq R\_{\text{U-eBH}} \subseteq R\_{\text{RU-eBH}} \subseteq R\_{\text{RC-eBH}} for every realization of U.

Third, when distributional information is available, [<u>Wang & Ramdas (2022)</u>](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~aramdas/ebook-final.pdf) (**Theorem 9.13**) boost e_j \mapsto b_j e_j using the marginal null distribution via \mathbb{E}\[T(\alpha b_j e_j)\] \le \alpha for T(x) = \frac{m}{\lceil m/x \rceil}\mathbf{1}\\x \ge 1\\, while [<u>Lee & Ren (2024)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17562) (arXiv:2404.17562, **Theorems 1 & 2**) prove that conditioning on a sufficient statistic S_j for e \mid S_j under H_j (**conditional calibration, e-BH-CC**) yields BH-sharp boosted e-values that strictly dominate e-BH under arbitrary dependence.

### 2.3 Comparison with Holm on E-Process P-Values (p-Holm vs. e-Holm) and the Running-Maximum Trap

Your system currently converts each finalist's holdout wealth process M\_{i,t} into a p-value via the inverse running maximum p_i = 1 / \tilde{e}\_i (where \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{1 \le t \le T} M\_{i,t}) and applies classical Holm (p-Holm) across the m finalists. Recent theorems expose **two fundamental flaws** in this procedure: a severe loss of power from converting to p-values before applying Holm, and a critical validity trap regarding running maxima versus stopped e-values.

[<u>Wang (2025)</u>](https://sas.uwaterloo.ca/~wang/papers/2025Wang-Biom.pdf) (*Biometrika* 112(2), **Theorem 1**) proved via multi-marginal optimal transport duality that under arbitrary dependence, **the only admissible way to merge e-values is a weighted arithmetic average** M\_\lambda(e) = \lambda_0 + \sum\_{k=1}^m \lambda_k e_k. Building on [<u>Vovk & Wang (2021)</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-49/issue-3/E-values-Calibration-combination-and-applications/10.1214/20-AOS2020.pdf), [<u>Hartog & Lei (2025/2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015) (arXiv:2501.09015v4) formalize **e-Holm**: the closed testing procedure that rejects each intersection hypothesis H_I = \bigcap\_{i \in I} H_i whenever the unweighted arithmetic average of its e-values satisfies e_I = \frac{1}{\|I\|}\sum\_{i \in I} e_i \ge \frac{1}{\alpha}.

[<u>Hartog & Lei (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015) prove two decisive results comparing e-Holm to your current p-Holm on p_i = 1/e_i. First, weighted e-Bonferroni local tests (e_I = \sum\_{i \in I} w_i(I) e_i \ge 1/\alpha) are **provably more powerful** than weighted p-Bonferroni tests on the unique admissible derived p-values p_i = 1/e_i (\min\_{i \in I} \frac{p_i}{w_i(I)} \le \alpha \iff \max\_{i \in I} w_i(I) e_i \ge 1/\alpha), so **the closure (e-Holm) is guaranteed to reject at least as many finalists as p-Holm on every sample path, and strictly more whenever multiple finalists carry positive evidence**. Second, whereas p-Holm requires the maximum e-value e\_{(1)} to exceed \frac{m}{\alpha} before *any* finalist can be rejected, [<u>Hartog & Lei (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015) prove that e-Holm reduces to an O(m)-time threshold rule that rejects H_i if and only if:

e_i \ge \frac{1}{\alpha} + \sum\_{j \in J^\*} \left(\frac{1}{\alpha} - e_j\right), \quad \text{where } J^\* = \left\\j \in \[m\] : e_j \< \frac{1}{\alpha}\right\\

Because \|J^\*\| \le \|H_0\| when non-nulls exceed 1/\alpha, the e-Holm threshold is bounded above by \frac{\|H_0\| + 1}{\alpha}. If m = 10 finalists pass your Stage 1 screen and all 10 are genuine signals achieving e_i = 25 \> 1/\alpha = 20 on the holdout, then J^\* = \emptyset and e-Holm promotes **all 10 finalists** at threshold 20, whereas your current p-Holm requires e\_{(1)} \ge 200 and promotes **zero finalists**. Furthermore, e-Holm achieves **post-hoc strong FWER control** (allowing \alpha to be chosen after viewing the data), whereas p-Holm fails post-hoc FWER control.

Why did your current design use p_i = 1 / \sup_t M\_{i,t}? Because by Ville's inequality, \tilde{p}\_\[span_43\](start_span)\[span_43\](end_span)i = 1 / \tilde{e}\_i (where \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{1 \le t \le T} M\_{i,t}) is a valid p-value, so p-Holm controls FWER via the union bound on tail probabilities. However, **the running maximum \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{1 \le t \le T} M\_{i,t} is a pseudo-e-value, not a valid e-value**, because its expectation under H_0 exceeds 1. In *Carefree multiple testing with e-processes* ([<u>Tavyrikov, Goeman, & de Heide, 2026</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf), *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 20(2): 3178–3189, **Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 3.4**), the authors prove by explicit counterexample that under **arbitrary dependence** across e-processes, applying e-\[span_45\](start_span)\[span_45\](end_span)BH, Closed e-BH, or arithmetic-average e-Holm directly to the unadjusted running maxima \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{t \le s} M\_{i,t} **fails to control the FDR or FWER at level \alpha**. ([<u>Hartog & Lei, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015), Section 3.2, show that e-Holm on unadjusted running maxima controls FWER at \alpha + o(\alpha) *only if the e-processes are mutually independent*, which does not hold for correlated trading strategies).

To unlock the power of e-Holm, Closed e-BH, or online e-BH under arbitrary cross-strategy dependence, you have **two mathematically exact options**:

1.  **Use Stopped E-Values M\_{i, \tau_i} (Zero Calibration Loss):** By the Optional Stopping Theorem, if each finalist's e-process M\_{i,t} is evaluated at any \mathcal{F}\_t-stopping time \tau_i—such as the fixed end of the holdout sample \tau_i = T, or the first time M\_{i,t} crosses a pre-declared stopping boundary where it is frozen ([<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27051v1); [<u>Tavyrikov et al., 2026</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf))—then S_i = M\_{i, \tau_i} is a **bona fide e-value (\mathbb{E}\[S_i\] \le 1)**. Feeding S_i = M\_{i, \tau_i} directly into e-Holm or Closed e-BH gives exact FWER or FDR control under arbitrary dependence with zero calibration penalty.

2.  **Use Admissible Running-Maximum Adjusters (Carefree Testing):** If you want to evaluate the historical peak \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{t \le T} M\_{i,t} without freezing the process at a stopping rule, **Theorem 4.1** of [<u>Tavyrikov, Goeman, & de Heide (2026)</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf) and [<u>Choe & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://yjchoe.github.io/slides/ChoeRamdas2024-CombiningEvidenceAcrossFiltrations-Slides.pdf) prove that applying an **admissible adjuster** A: \[1,\infty\] \to \[0,\infty\] satisfying \int_1^\infty \frac{A(x)}{x^2}dx = 1, such as A_1(\tilde{e}\_i) = \frac{(\tilde{e}\_i - 1)^2}{\tilde{e}\_i(1 + \ln \tilde{e}\_i)}, converts the running maximum back into a valid e-process (\mathbb{E}\[A(\tilde{e}\_i)\] \le 1), restoring exact arbitrary-dependence FDR/FWER control.

*(Note: If you ever test independent strategy streams across uncoupled markets where intersection nulls satisfy conditional independence, [<u>Dubey & Huo, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27465), arXiv:2609.27465, **Theorems 4.5 & 4.10**, prove that **Prior-Matched Elementary Symmetric Polynomial (PEM) mixtures** dominate both arithmetic-mean e-Holm and product closure by achieving the rate-optimal pooled KL growth rate lD whenever l streams are active. For correlated strategies on the same market, however, arithmetic-mean e-Holm or Closed e-BH is required.)*

## 3. Adaptive Data Analysis and Reusable Holdouts: What Survives for Dependent Time Series and Shared Agent History?

### 3.1 Classical Reusable Holdouts (Thresholdout) and Max-Information

In the i.i.d. setting (S \sim P^n), [<u>Dwork, Feldman, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold, & Roth (2015a,b)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629) (*Science* 349(6248): 636–638; *NIPS 2015*, arXiv:1506.02629) proved that adaptive data reuse can be controlled via **Differential Privacy (DP)** and **Approximate Max-Information** (I\_\infty^\beta(S; A(S))). Their **Thresholdout** algorithm splits the data into training S_t and holdout S_h. For each adaptively chosen bounded function \phi_i: \mathcal{X} \to \[0,1\], Thresholdout checks whether \|E\_{S_h}\[\phi_i\] - E\_{S_t}\[\phi_i\]\| \> \hat{T} + \eta (where \hat{T} = T + \text{Lap}(2\sigma) and \eta \sim \text{Lap}(4\sigma)). If the gap is below threshold, it returns the training mean E\_{S_t}\[\phi_i\] without leaking holdout information; if above threshold, it returns E\_{S_h}\[\phi_i\] + \text{Lap}(\sigma) and decrements an "overfitting budget" B by 1. By **Lemma 24 of [<u>Dwork et al. (2015)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)**, Thresholdout guarantees \mathbb{P}\[\|P\[\phi_i\] - a_i\| \ge \tau\] \le \beta across m adaptive queries provided the i.i.d. holdout size satisfies n \ge 80 \frac{\sqrt{B \ln(1/(\tau \beta))}}{\tau \sigma} \sim \tilde{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{B} \ln(m/\beta)}{\tau^2}\right). Thus, an i.i.d. holdout can answer an exponential number of non-overfitting queries m as long as at most B = o(n^2) queries actually overfit the training sample.

### 3.2 What Survives for Dependent Time Series?

When S = (X_1, \dots, X_n) is a **dependent time series** rather than an i.i.d. product measure P^n, the standard DP-to-generalization transfer theorem breaks because conditioning on a single time point X_t alters the distribution of neighboring points X\_{t-1}, X\_{t+1}. Two primary theoretical results govern what survives under temporal dependence.

First, [<u>Bassily & Freund (2016)</u>](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=79GwCnXYej) introduced *typical stability* for correlated data where the analyst is restricted to posing (\varepsilon, \gamma)-concentrated queries (queries whose non-adaptive empirical means concentrate around their true expectation). While noise-addition mechanisms preserve adaptive validity under typical stability, **the number of supported adaptive queries collapses from \tilde{O}(n^2) in the i.i.d. setting to at most \tilde{O}(n)**—even when serial dependencies decay toward zero.

Second, [<u>Kontorovich, Sadigurschi, & Stemmer (2022)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08704) (*Adaptive Data Analysis with Correlated Observations*, arXiv:2201.08704) re-established the baseline for correlated observations by introducing **Gibbs dependence**:

\psi(\mu) = \sup\_{x \in \mathcal{X}^n} \mathbb{E}\_{i \sim \[n\]} \left\\\mu_i(\cdot) - \mu_i(\cdot \mid x\_{-i})\right\\\_{\text{TV}}

Their **Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.6** prove that an (\varepsilon, \delta)-differentially private mechanism on n \ge \tilde{O}(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{\alpha^2}\log\frac{1}{\beta}) dependent observations guarantees **({\alpha + 2\psi(\mu)}, \beta)-statistical accuracy** for k adaptively chosen bounded queries. Quadratic query scaling k = \tilde{O}(n^2) therefore survives **if and only if the Gibbs dependence \psi(\mu) is at most the target tolerance \alpha**; otherwise, an irreducible error floor of 2\psi(\mu) persists. For an undirected Markov chain with potential ratio \bar{R}(\mu) = \frac{R(\mu)^2 - r(\mu)^2}{R(\mu)^2 + r(\mu)^2}, **Lemma 3.8, Theorem 3.11, and Corollary 3.12** prove that \psi(\mu) \le \bar{R}(\mu) and that **skipping (subsampling) every t-th observation** (\mu^{\times t}) reduces Gibbs dependence exponentially (\psi(\mu^{\times t}) \le \psi(\mu)^t), restoring (3\alpha, \b\[span_49\](start_span)\[span_49\](end_span)eta)-accuracy for k = \tilde{O}(n\_{\text{eff}}^2) adaptive queries once t \ge \frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{\log(1/\bar{R})}. For general dependent processes without small Gibbs dependence, **Theorem 1.7** (via transcript compression) bounds the failure probability across k concentrated queries by \delta \cdot k \cdot 2^{k \log(1/\alpha)}, which again restricts k to O(n).

Beyond temporal correlation reducing n to n\_{\text{eff}} = n/t, DP-based reusable holdouts (including Thresholdout and the 2026 Gaussian DP e-value mechanism of [<u>arXiv:2605.29388</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29388v2)) require **bounded worst-case query sensitivity \Delta = \max\_{S \\span_51\](start_span)\[span_51\](end_span)sim S'} \|q(S) - q(S')\| = O(1/n)**. For raw heavy-tailed strategy returns, changing a single day’s return R_t has **unbounded sensitivity** on both the sample mean and sample Sharpe ratio unless returns are clipped to \[-c, c\] or mapped to bounded cross-sectional ranks (rank-IC \in \[-1, 1\]). Moreover, because daily financial alpha is small (2–15 bps/day), injecting Laplace noise of scale \sigma \sim \tau / \ln m directly into holdout Sharpe ratios overwhelms genuine economic signal.

### 3.3 Implications for LLM Agents Sharing a Common History of Evaluations

In your system, multiple LLM agents inspect a shared history of evaluations \Pi\_{t-1} = \\(q_1, a_1), \dots, (q\_{t-1}, a\_{t-1})\\ (both their own and other agents') and choose what to test next. The adaptive data analysis literature establishes three mathematical consequences for this shared-history architecture:

1.  **Multi-Agent Swarm Equivalence to a Single Composed Adaptive Analyst:** By **Theorem 15 (Adaptive Composition of Max-Information) and Lemma 16 (Post-Processing)** of [<u>Dwork et al. (2015)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)—as well as the dynamic data formulation of [<u>Adaptive Data Analysis for Growing Data (2024)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.13375) (arXiv:2405.13375)—a swarm of A agents reading a shared transcript \Pi\_{t-1} is mathematically equivalent to a single adaptive algorithm whose approximate max-information **sums across every evaluation in the shared transcript**: I\_\infty^{\sum\_{i=1}^k \beta_i}(S; \Pi_k) \le \sum\_{i=1}^k k_i. Your practice of **counting every evaluation globally across all agents** is therefore mandatory; maintaining separate per-agent budgets would violate composition.

2.  **Truncating or Summarizing Agent Context Does Not Reduce Overfitting:** Limiting an LLM agent’s context window to the last K evaluations—or distilling the archive into a persistent memory summary as in TradeGrad ([<u>OpenReview:Vt69b8C5dg, 2025</u>](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=Vt69b8C5dg))—does not improve worst-case generalization. **Claim 1 of [<u>arXiv:1901.11143v2 (2019)</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.11143v2)** proves that *in full generality, an adaptive data analyst with a truncated view of the transcript of depth K generalizes no better than an analyst with a full view of the transcript*, because adaptive queries can recursively encode historical state.

3.  **Description-Length Leakage of Search Feedback (Evaluate vs. Assess):** By **Theorem 17 of [<u>Dwork et al. (2015)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)**, a randomized or deterministic mechanism that outputs a response in a finite set Y leaks at most \[span_13\](start_span)\[span_13\](end_span)\log(\|Y\|/\beta) max-information per round. Returning continuous backtest metrics and p-values to the shared agent memory maximizes information leakage. As formalized in the 2026 leakage-prevention type grammar of [<u>arXiv:2603.10742</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10742), an iterative agent zone (evaluate) must either feed into a **strictly terminal, one-shot sealed holdout (assess)** or into a **forward-only anytime-valid e-process ledger** where a newly proposed strategy can never inherit pre-proposal ("pre-birth") return history ([<u>Benhamou et al., 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03384v1); [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051)).

## 4. Backtest Overfitting and Data Snooping Since Bailey–López de Prado and Harvey–Liu–Zhu

Since the foundational works of Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, & Zhu (2014/2017) and Harvey, Liu, & Zhu (2016), the literature on backtest overfitting and data snooping has advanced across four distinct fronts.

### 4.1 Critiques of the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) and Advances in Cross-Validation (CPCV)

While [<u>Bailey & López de Prado (2014)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286121118_The_Deflated_Sharpe_RatioCorrecting_for_Selection_Bias_BacktestOverfitting_and_Non-Normality) and [<u>López de Prado & Bailey (2021)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355949889_The_False_Strategy_Theorem_A_Financial_Application_of_Experimental_Mathematics) (*American Mathematical Monthly* 128(9): 806–817) derived the **False Strategy Theorem (FST)** to approximate the expected maximum of K independent standard Gaussian Sharpe ratios, an analytical critique published on the statistical finance blog sharperat.io ([<u>Do Not Deflate, Part I</u>](http://www.sharperat.io/do-not-deflate-I.html)) and recent agentic factor-mining evaluations ([<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051)) identify three reasons why **DSR lacks a valid frequentist Type-I error guarantee**:

1.  **Breakdown of Gaussian Extreme-Value Asymptotics Under Fat Tails:** Even when underlying returns are i.i.d. Gaussian, sample Sharpe ratios are fat-tailed; when returns themselves are heavy-tailed, the extreme value distribution of \max\_{k \le K} \widehat{\text{SR}}\_k diverges from the normal asymptotics used in the FST numerator.

2.  **Conflation of Cross-Sectional Skill Variance and Estimation Luck:** As noted in the sharperat.io analysis, DSR subtracts \sqrt{\sigma_s^2 + \sigma_l^2} f(K) in the numerator (where \sigma_s^2 is cross-sectional variance across heterogeneous strategies and \sigma_l^2 is estimation noise) but divides only by single-strategy luck \sigma_l, leaving the tested null hypothesis unstated and lacking any proof of uniformity under the null.

3.  **Failure Under Adaptive Sequential Loops:** [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051) (arXiv:2609.27051) note that static DSR deflation assumes a fixed batch of K candidates determined in advance and fails to control sequential error when a learning agent reads each verdict and submits near-duplicates of lucky candidates.

For internal cross-validation during strategy search, [<u>Arian, Norouzi Mobarekeh, & Seco (2024)</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385070377_Backtest_overfitting_in_the_machine_learning_era_A_comparison_of_out-of-sample_testing_methods_in_a_synthetic_controlled_environment) (*Knowledge-Based Systems* 305: 112477, SSRN:4778909) conducted a synthetic controlled benchmark across jump-diffusion, Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) stochastic volatility, and drift-burst regimes. Their simulations show that **Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV)**—which purges overlapping lookbacks and embargoes serial correlation across all combinatorial train/test splits to compute the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)—significantly outperforms K-Fold, Purged K-Fold, and **Walk-Forward testing**, which suffered the highest false-discovery vulnerability due to temporal variability and weaker stationarity along a single historical path.

### 4.2 High-Dimensional Frequentist and Empirical Bayes Methods (2020–2025)

In large-scale frequentist and empirical Bayes testing, [<u>Chordia, Goyal, & Saretto (2020)</u>](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/anomalies-and-false-rejections) (*Review of Financial Studies* 33(5): 2134–2179) examined over 2 million trading strategies built from public signals and estimated the expected proportion of unadjusted false rejections at **45.3%**. ## 5. Published Uses of E-Values and Betting Tests to Validate Trading Strategies and Factors

Between 2024 and November 2026, multiple peer-reviewed studies and preprints applied e-processes and testing-by-betting directly to validate LLM-mined investment factors, adaptive trading strategy promotion, and financial risk/volatility models.

### 5.1 LLM Agent Factor Mining: ***Propose, Don't Judge*** ([**<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051))

In *"Propose, Don't Judge: An Anytime-Valid Referee for LLM Agents That Mine Investment Factors"* (arXiv:2609.27051, September 2026), Qu, Chen, and Wang evaluate an autonomous LLM factor-mining agent (Gemini) alongside bandit and round-robin proposers over a 10-year walk-forward campaign on the CSI 500 equity universe. Their core architectural principle is a strict separation of powers: **proposing hypotheses and authoring diagnostic probes belong to the LLM agent; judging belongs to a frozen statistical referee that the agent cannot touch**.

Each candidate factor c is scored strictly on market outcomes revealed *after* its submission date using its daily rank Information Coefficient (rank-IC) X_t \in \[-1, 1\]. Because daily rank-IC is serially correlated, the referee bets on the predictably whitened stream:

\tilde{X}\_t = X_t - \hat{\rho}\_t X\_{t-1}, \quad \text{testing } H_0: \mathbb{E}\[\tilde{X}\_t \mid \mathcal{G}\_{t-1}\] \le 0

where \hat{\rho}\_t is a shrunk, capped AR(1) autocorrelation estimate computed strictly from past outcomes X\_{\<t}. Capital updates multiplicatively as W_t = \prod\_{s} (1 + \lambda_s \tilde{X}\_s), where the predictable stake \lambda_t (aGRAPA / empirical Kelly) is capped at \lambda\_{\max,t} = \frac{1}{1 + \hat{\rho}\_t} to ensure non-negativity. Candidate c is admitted on day t when its capital clears the online e-BH threshold over a pre-registered 2,000-slot epoch (\gamma_c = w_v / N_v with w_v = 1, N_v = 2000, \alpha = 0.05):

R_t = \left\\c : E_c(t) \ge \frac{1}{k_t^\* \alpha\[span_1\](start_span)\[span_1\](end_span)\[span_3\](start_span)\[span_3\](end_span) \gamma_c}\right\\, \quad k_t^\* = \max\left\\k : \\\left\\c : E_c(t) \ge \frac{1}{k \alpha \gamma_c}\right\\ \ge k\right\\

Once admitted, a candidate's e-value is frozen so admissions are never revoked, controlling FDR at every stopping time. To monitor deployed factors for alpha decay, the referee registers a break-even IC threshold \delta = \frac{2c \cdot \text{TO}}{\kappa} (where c is per-side trading cost, \text{TO} is one-way turnover, and \kappa = 0.018 converts IC to gross return) and runs a nonparametric Shiryaev–Roberts **e-detector** ([<u>Shin, Ramdas, & Rinaldo, 2024</u>](https://nejsds.nestat.org/journal/NEJSDS/article/59/info)) that bets on Y_t = \delta_t - \tilde{X}\_t (\delta_t = \delta(1 - \hat{\rho}\_t)), starting a new e-process W^{(j)} on every day j after admission and summing them.

In synthetic zero-alpha simulations, the frozen referee admits **0 false factors** across all random seeds, whereas leaky referees (Peeking, Adaptive Threshold, No Gate) admit **0.26–0.85 false factors per submission**. On the 10-year CSI 500 walk-forward, pre-whitening holds empirical FDR at **1.8–2.4%**, and the frozen referee reduces false admissions by **5–11x** under scripted proposers and **2.6–4.9x** under the Gemini LLM proposer (11.7 false admissions vs. 37.4 for Peeking, 43.5 for Adaptive Threshold, and 77.2 for No Gate in the 4-family library; 9.8–14.6 vs. 97.0–295.6 in the 9-family library). Because realistic daily factor IC has mean \mu = 0.03 and standard deviation \sigma = 0.12, the unconstrained Kelly stake \lambda^\* = \mu/\sigma^2 = 2.08 exceeds any no-bankruptcy cap, so aGRAPA, Gaussian-efficient bettors ([<u>Martinez-Taboada & Ramdas, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21694v2)), and capped Kelly all ride the cap. At the break-even edge \delta = 0.015, a capped Kelly bettor needs **1,434 trading days** (vs. 1,356 days uncapped), and across all admitted true factors on CSI 500 the average certification wait is **~500 trading days** (408–543 days). Because the certified portfolio waits ~500 trading days before investing in a true factor, its net-of-cost Sharpe ratio trails the ungated portfolio (+0.40 to +0.49 Certified vs. +0.62 to +0.78 Ungated across 2016–2019 cohorts), though the certified portfolio never loses money at the registered cost.

### 5.2 Anytime-Valid Strategy Promotion Ledgers: ***Promote*** ([**<u>Benhamou et al., 2026</u>**](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03384v1))

In *"Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Adaptive Financial Research Agents"* (arXiv:2611.03384, accepted at ICAIF 2026), Benhamou, Ohana, Guez, Jacquot, Setrouk, and Saadi design the **Promote** ledger for autonomous agents proposing trading strategies over equity ETFs, defensive ETFs, and Fama–French industry portfolios (daily data March 2006 – May 2026; out-of-sample ledger January 2015 – May 2026).

Every candidate strategy k = 1, 2, \dots is registered at a predictable birth stopping time \nu_k (modifying any parameter, ticker, or cost rule creates a new candidate with a new birth date) and receives a predictable testing-wealth allocation \pi_k \> 0 from a single lifetime budget satisfying \sum\_{k=1}^\infty \pi_k \le 1 pathwise, with all pre-birth data (t \le \nu_k) excluded. For t \> \nu_k, the period return r\_{k,t}^{\text{net}} (after exact 5-bp one-way turnover costs solved via the self-financing equation against drifted weights) is scaled by a predictable scale factor s\_{k,t} \> 0 and clipped to \[-1, 1\]: \mathca\[span_78\](start_span)\[span_78\](end_span)\[span_85\](start_span)\[span_85\](end_span)l{Y}\_{k,t} = \text{clip}(r\_{k,t}^{\text{net}} / s\_{k,t}, -1, 1), while economic P&L is tracked on the unclipped net return. Instead of a single plug-in Kelly bet, Promote averages five fixed-bet e-processes across \Lambda = \\0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35\\:

\bar{E}\_{k,t} = \frac{1}{5} \sum\_{\lambd\[span_79\](start_span)\[span_79\](end_span)\[span_86\](start_span)\[span_86\](end_span)a \in \Lambda} \prod\_{s=\nu_k + 1}^t (1 + \lambda \mathcal{Y}\_{k,s}), \quad \tau_k = \inf\left\\t \> \nu_k : \bar{E}\_{k,t} \ge (\alpha \pi_k)^{-1}\right\\

By **Theorem 1 of [<u>Benhamou et al. (2026)</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf)**, conditioning on \mathcal{F}\_{\nu_k} and applying Ville's inequality plus the union bound guarantees lifetime FWER control \mathbb{P}(\bigcup\_{k \ge 1} \\N_k, \tau_k \< \infty\\) \le \alpha across arbitrarily dependent, adaptively born strategies. Across 3,000 simulation audits under a 24-rule shared-sign null, repeated nominal testing (p \< 0.05) produces **94.5% FWER**, whereas Promote (\pi_k = 1/24, boundary 480 at \alpha = 0.05) records **0 / 3,000 false promotions (0.0% FWER)**. Under an adaptive-birth 32-stream mean-zero audit, repeated nominal testing suffers **93.07% FWER**, while Promote maintains **1.77% FWER**. At a 15-bp component return shift, Promote achieves **70.1% power** with a median detection delay of **2,327 sessions** (scaling from 0% at 5 bp and 7.7% at 10 bp to 99.3% at 20 bp and 100% at 30 bp with median delay 1,049 sessions). On the 2015–2026 market replay across 24 rules after 5-bp turnover costs, Promote promotes **0 rules** (max e-value 1.93 vs. threshold 480), whereas repeated nominal inspection promotes a reversed industry-January rule on January 13, 2016 that loses **-0.90% per year (Sharpe -0.18)** over its shadow path.

## 6. Architectural Audit: Where Your Current Design Looks Weak or Outdated

Against the 2023–2026 literature reviewed above, your current pipeline exhibits **six specific structural weaknesses** across its search-sample and held-out-sample stages.

### 6.1 Predictability of Bets (\lambda_t \in \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}) Does Not Cure Serial Dependence in Returns

Your holdout test relies on \lambda_t being \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}-predictable. While predictability is *necessary* so that \mathbb{E}\[1 + \lambda_t R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] = 1 + \lambda_t \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\], it is **not sufficient** to make M_t = \prod\_{s=1}^t (1 + \lambda_s R_s) a test supermartingale under the null hypothesis that the strategy has zero unconditional alpha (\mathbb{E}\[R_t\] \le 0). For M_t to be a supermartingale for every nonnegative predictable \lambda_t \ge 0, the return stream must satisfy the **strong martingale difference null**: \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 0 almost surely at *every* time step t ([<u>Choe & Ramdas, 2024</u>](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.0792); [<u>Waudby-Smith et al., 2024</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377870735_Anytime-valid_off-policy_inference_for_contextual_bandits)). Because your returns are **dependent time series**, any strategy with positive autocorrelation \rho \> 0 (e.g., overlapping multi-day signals, illiquid small-caps, or trend exposure) has \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \> 0 following positive return days even when its unconditional mean is zero (\mathbb{E}\[R_t\] = 0). A predictable plug-in Kelly bettor (or an LLM agent crafting a rule that sizes up after positive lagged returns) will set \lambda_t \> 0 when \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \> 0, causing \mathbb{E}\[M_t\] \> 1 and **inflating false discoveries above \alpha**. You must either predictably pre-whiten the return/score stream \tilde{R}\_t = R_t - \hat{\rho}\_t R\_{t-1} using pre-step data R\_{\<t} before betting ([<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051)), use the centered empirical Bernstein exponential supermartingale of [<u>Choe & Ramdas (2024)</u>](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.0792) which tests the running average of conditional means, or coarsen the filtration to lag-h blocks and apply the [<u>Choe & Ramdas (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09698) adjuster A(M_t^\*).

### 6.2 The Lower Bound on Step Returns (R_t \ge -1), Heavy Tails, and Strategy Bankruptcy

Your problem statement notes that *"returns are dependent time series with heavy tails; a strategy can go bankrupt."* In the linear betting process M_t = \prod\_{s=1}^t (1 + \lambda_s R_s) with \lambda_s \in \[0, 1/2\], this creates **two severe vulnerabilities**. First, non-negativity of M_t is an absolute prerequisite for Ville's inequality (\mathbb{P}(\sup_t M_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha). With a cap at \lambda_t \le 1/2, non-negativity 1 + \lambda_t R_t \ge 0 holds **if and only if R_t \ge -2 almost surely** (and for general \lambda_t \in \[span_65\](start_span)\[span_167\](start_span)\[span_169\](start_span)\[span_174\](start_span)\[span_176\](start_span)\[span_341\](start_span)\[span_342\](start_span)\[0,1), requires R_t \ge -1 a.s.). If "a strategy can go bankrupt" means an unlevered limited-liability strategy losing 100% of its equity (R_t = -1), then 1 + \frac{1}{2}(-1) = 0.5 \> 0, keeping M_t positive. **However**, if an LLM-generated strategy uses margin/leverage, trades futures, or shorts single-name assets where a heavy-tailed gap or short squeeze causes a single-period loss exceeding -100% (or -200%), then 1 + \lambda_t R_t can become negative (\< 0), **invalidating Ville’s inequality** ([<u>Waudby-Smith et al., 2024</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377870735_Anytime-valid_off-policy_inference_for_contextual_bandits)). Second, even when R_t \ge -1 is enforced by limited liability, [<u>Forré (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27766) (arXiv:2609.27766) proves that heavy left tails cause catastrophic downward jumps in \log(1 + \lambda_t R_t) that erase accumulated evidence, while heavy right tails cause the plug-in empirical variance \hat{\sigma}\_{t-1}^2 to spike (or fail to converge if the fourth moment is infinite), collapsing the plug-in Kelly bet \lambda_t \to 0. To prevent both failures, follow [<u>Benhamou et al. (2026)</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf) by betting on the predictably volatility-scaled, clipped net score \mathcal{Y}\_t = \text{clip}(R_t^{\text{net}} / s\_{t}, -1, 1) (while tracking economic P&L on the unclipped net return), or use **Fan’s one-sided sub-exponential \psi_E supermartingale** ([<u>Howard et al., 2020</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240)) or **Catoni-style supermartingales** ([<u>Wang & Ramdas, 2023</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01250)).

### 6.3 The Static Cap at 1/2 (\lambda_t \le 1/2) and Expanding-Window Plug-In Kelly

Your cap of 1/2 originates in the bounded-mean betting literature ([<u>Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2024</u>](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Events/Discussion%20meetings/Estimating-means-of-bounded-random-variables-by-betting.pdf); [<u>Wang, Wang, & Ziegel, 2024</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12480v4), Eq. 6), where observations lie in \[-1, 1\] or \[0, 1\] and capping \lambda_t \le 1/2 bounds the worst-case one-step log-loss by \log(1 - 1/2) = -\log 2. In your setting, an expanding-window plug-in Kelly bet capped at 1/2 suffers from three inefficiencies. First, for small t on volatility-standardized returns, an expanding-window plug-in estimator \hat{\mu}\_{t-1}/\hat{\sigma}\_{t-1}^2 is noisy: an early positive streak pushes \lambda_t to the 0.50 cap, causing severe **over-betting and quadratic volatility drag** that hurts power. Second, if \lambda_t is computed as an unweighted expanding average from s = 1 to t-1, [<u>Oestmann & Dickhaus (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05089) and [<u>Xue et al. (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27726) show that quiet or negative early periods dilute the betting fraction during subsequent high-alpha regimes. Third, [<u>Waudby-Smith, Sandoval, & Jordan (2025/2026)</u>](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.02818v1) (arXiv:2504.02818) prove that restricted-range plug-in/ONS bettors fail universal log-optimality when the optimal bet falls outside their range, whereas **mixture / Universal Portfolio e-processes** (such as [<u>Benhamou et al.'s 2026</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf) 5-point grid \Lambda = \\0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35\\) eliminate early plug-in estimation noise and achieve optimal log-growth. Furthermore, when pre-whitening \tilde{X}\_t = X_t - \hat{\rho}\_t X\_{t-1} on \[-1, 1\]-clipped scores, the exact no-bankruptcy cap is \lambda\_{\max,t} = \frac{1}{1 + \hat{\rho}\_t} ([<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051)).

### 6.4 Holm on Inverse Running Maxima Across Finalists & Holdout Reuse Leakage

As established in Section 2.3, converting holdout e-processes to p_i = 1/\tilde{e}\_i and running classical p-Holm across m finalists forces the top finalist to clear m/\alpha, discarding the arithmetic-averaging synergy of e-Holm ([<u>Hartog & Lei, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015)). At the same time, you cannot simply plug unadjusted running maxima \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{t \le T} M\_{i,t} into e-Holm or e-BH when finalists are correlated ([<u>Tavyrikov, Goeman, & de Heide, 2026</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf), Proposition 3.2 & Corollary 3.4); you must use **stopped e-values M\_{i, \tau_i}** or apply a **Choe–Ramdas adjuster A(\tilde{e}\_i)**. Finally, if your LLM agents operate in a continuous loop and periodically send new batches of finalists to the *same* held-out sample, applying Holm only within each batch of m finalists **does not control lifetime FWER or FDR across batches**. Every finalist ever evaluated on the holdout must draw a slot weight \pi_k (for FWER via Promote where \sum\_{k=1}^\infty \pi_k \le 1) or \gamma_k (for FDR via online e-BH / Donation e-LOND where \s\[span_209\](start_span)\[span_209\](end_span)um\_{k=1}^\infty \gamma_k = 1) from a **single global lifetime budget**.

\## 8. Concrete Recommendations Ranked by Value and Risk

We rank our concrete engineering and statistical recommendations from highest value-to-risk ratio (immediate mathematical drop-ins) to deeper architectural upgrades:

1.  **Replace p-Holm on Running Maxima with Closed e-Holm (or Closed e-BH) on Stopped E-Values (Value: Very High \| Risk: Very Low):** Your current final gate converts e-processes to p_i = 1/\max\_{t \le T} M\_{i,t} and runs classical p-Holm\[span_36\](start_span)\[span_36\](end_span), forcing the top finalist to exceed m/\alpha (200 for m=10, \alpha=0.05). Instead of taking the unadjusted running maximum over \[1, T\] (which [<u>Tavyrikov, Goeman, & de Heide, 2026</u>](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-20/issue-2/Carefree-multiple-testing-with-e-processes/10.1214/26-EJS2546.pdf) prove fails inside e-Holm or e-BH under arbitrary dependence), define the stopping rule \tau_i = \min\\T, \inf\\t \ge 1 : M\_{i,t} \ge B\_{\text{stop}}\\\\ (freezing the process upon crossing its target boundary B\_{\text{stop}}, or stopping at terminal T). By Optional Stopping, e_i = M\_{i, \tau_i} is an exact e-value (\mathbb{E}\[e_i\] \le 1); if you must evaluate the unstopped historical peak \tilde{e}\_i = \max\_{t \le T} M\_{i,t}, pass it through the Choe–Ramdas adjuster e_i = A_1(\tilde{e}\_i) = \frac{(\tilde{e}\_i - 1)^2}{\tilde{e}\_i(1 + \ln \tilde{e}\_i)} first. Then, for FWER control, apply e-Holm ([<u>Hartog & Lei, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09015)) by setting J^\* = \\j \in \[m\] : e_j \< 1/\alpha\\ and rejecting every finalist i with e_i \ge \frac{1}{\alpha} + \sum\_{j \in J^\*} (\f\[span_81\](start_span)\[span_81\](end_span)\[span_88\](start_span)\[span_88\](end_span)rac{1}{\alpha} - e_j). If your portfolio nets and combines multiple promoted strategies—which [<u>Preprints 202610.1262</u>](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.1262) shows beats single-winner selection by 165–180 bps/year—switch from FWER to FDR via Closed e-BH ([<u>Xu et al., 2025</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02517); [<u>Xu, Fischer, & Ramdas, 2026</u>](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v337/main/assets/xu26a/xu26a.pdf)) or RC-eBH ([<u>Bai & Ramdas, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35821)).

2.  **Fix the Martingale Difference Violation and Bankruptcy Vulnerability in the Holdout Betting Process (Value: Very High \| Risk: Low):** Raw dependent, heavy-tailed returns with potential bankruptcy violate \mathbb{E}\[R_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}\] \le 0 under autocorrelation and can violate 1 + \lambda_t R_t \ge 0 under levered/short losses exceeding -200% or suffer catastrophic bet drawdowns. At each holdout step t, compute the net return r\_{i,t}^{\text{net}} after exact turnover costs, divide by a strictly predictable volatility scale s\_{i,t} \in \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}, and apply either: (a) the clipped, pre-whitened transformation of [<u>Benhamou et al. (2026)</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf) and [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang (2026)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051), setting X\_{i,t} = \text{clip}(r\_{i,t}^{\text{net}} / s\_{i,t}, -1, 1) and \tilde{X}\_{i,t} = X\_{i,t} - \hat{\rho}\_{i,t} X\_{i,t-1} where \hat{\rho}\_{i,t} is the shrunk AR(1) coefficient estimated on s \< t; or (b) if you prefer not to clip upside returns, **Fan’s one-sided sub-exponential \psi_E supermartingale** ([<u>Howard et al., 2020</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240)) \exp\\\lambda_t \tilde{r}\_{i,t} - \psi\_{E,c}(\lambda_t) \tilde{r}\_{i,t}^2\\ or **Catoni’s influence supermartingale** ([<u>Wang & Ramdas, 2023</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01250)).

3.  **Replace the Expanding-Window Capped-1/2 Plug-In Kelly Bet with a Pre-Declared Mixture E-Process or Finite-Horizon aGRAPA (Value: High \| Risk: Low):** To eliminate early plug-in over-betting, dilution during quiet volatility regimes ([<u>Oestmann & Dickhaus, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05089)), and suboptimality when the cap binds ([<u>Waudby-Smith, Sandoval, & Jordan, 2025</u>](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.02818v1)), replace the single plug-in Kelly bet on \tilde{X}\_{i,t} with the **5-point fixed-grid mixture e-process** of [<u>Benhamou et al. (2026)</u>](https://www.set-science.com/a/df7b9c6a-5a49-49e4-8890-e45b708d95aa/pdf): \bar{M}\_{i,t} = \frac{1}{5} \sum\_{\lambda \in \Lambda} \prod\_{s=1}^t (1 + \lambda \tilde{X}\_{i,s}) over \Lambda = \\0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35\\. If you retain a predictable plug-in bet \lambda_t, estimate moments over a **rolling finite window T = 50 (50-FH GREM, [<u>Oestmann & Dickhaus, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05089))** or via **Adam exponential discounting ([<u>Xue et al., 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27726))**, and set the stake cap to \lambda\_{\max,t} = \frac{1}{1 + \hat{\rho}\_{i,t}}.

4.  **Enforce a Global Lifetime Budget Across All Holdout Evaluations (Promote or Online e-BH) (Value: High \| Risk: Medium):** When LLM agents send multiple waves of finalists to the held-out sample over time, running Holm only within each batch fails to control lifetime FWER or FDR across batches. Assign every finalist k = 1, 2, \dots that ever touches the holdout a strictly positive lifetime budget weight \pi_k \> 0 (for FWER via Promote boundary (\alpha \pi_k)^{-1} with \sum\_{k=1}^\infty \pi_k \le 1) or \gamma_k \> 0 (for FDR via on\[span_364\](start_span)\[span_364\](end_span)\[span_366\](start_span)\[span_366\](end_span)\[span_368\](start_span)\[span_368\](end_span)line e-BH / Donation e-LOND with \sum\_{k=1}^\infty \gamma_k = 1). Additionally, coarsen any holdout feedback returned to the shared agent memory to binary pass/fail decisions to minimize max-information leakage \log(\|Y\|/\beta) per **Theorem 17 of [<u>Dwork et al. (2015)</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)**.

5.  **Harden the Stage 1 Search Gate Against Bootstrap Exploits, Omitted Factors, and Pre-Training Contamination (Value: Medium-High \| Risk: Medium):** In Stage 1, replace the stationary bootstrap—where LLM agents can exploit exact block repeats ([<u>arXiv:2611.04070v1, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04070v1), Section 5.1)—with the **PCA/Matrix-Completion + Wild Bootstrap + Deep-in-the-Null Screening** pipeline of [<u>Giglio, Liao, & Xiu (2021)</u>](https://stefanogiglio.org/papers/giglio-liao-xiu-rfs-2021.pdf), and use **CPCV** ([<u>Arian, Norouzi, & Seco, 2024</u>](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385070377_Backtest_overfitting_in_the_machine_learning_era_A_comparison_of_out-of-sample_testing_methods_in_a_synthetic_controlled_environment)) for internal cross-validation. Ensure your held-out sample lies strictly after the LLM’s pre-training cutoff or apply FINCAD contrastive decoding ([<u>Li & Ma, 2026</u>](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.510.pdf)).

6.  **Deploy a Post-Admission E-Detector for Automatic Strategy Retirement (Value: Medium-High \| Risk: Medium):** Because even true strategies suffer post-deployment alpha decay ([<u>Jensen, Kelly, & Pedersen, 2023</u>](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432); [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051)), attach a nonparametric **Shiryaev–Roberts e-detector** ([<u>Shin, Ramdas, & Rinaldo, 2024</u>](https://nejsds.nestat.org/journal/NEJSDS/article/59/info); [<u>Qu, Chen, & Wang, 2026</u>](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27051), Section 4.4) to every promoted strategy in live/paper trading. Betting on Y_t = \delta\_{\text{cost}} - \tilde{X}\_t against the strategy's break-even transaction cost floor \delta\_{\text{cost}} guarantees a lower-bounded Average Run Length (ARL) to false alarm while automatically retiring decayed strategies.
