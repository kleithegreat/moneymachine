# Review: a possible C4.5 v3

**Recommendation:** separate **false promotions**, **unresolved safety outcomes**, and **whether the swarm was exercised**. A single “counts as promotion” bit cannot measure only false discoveries while also preventing broken agents from passing vacuously.

This is a prospective recommendation, not a reinterpretation of v2. Leave the running series unchanged.

## 1. Preserve v2’s verdict and distinguish it from the scientific finding

**Outcome this could change: whether v2 passes, and whether its result is misreported as a false discovery.**

`swarm-null-2-N2-5` correctly counts under v2. A4 made zero evaluations; the other agents made 11, 9 and 9. All four sessions exited cleanly. The decision record says nothing promoted and the audits held; the absolute-path command was refused exactly as the frozen permission required. This was an agent failure, not evidence that the permission boundary malfunctioned.  
Sources: `swarm-null-2-so-far.jsonl:6`; `audit/decisions.md:503–507`.

Therefore report:

- **v2 accounting:** one counted campaign among six recorded.
- **Actual discoveries:** none in those six. All six search reports have empty screened and finalist sets.
- **Exercise failure:** one of 24 agents evaluated nothing.

The snapshot’s total evaluations are 36, 28, 30, 49, 39 and 29; its filled-trial counts are 34, 26, 26, 46, 39 and 28. This is some real searching, not full-budget searching.  
Source: `swarm-null-2-so-far.jsonl:1–6`.

Neither relabel N2-5 nor replace it. V2 requires all ten fixed campaigns, with promotion, crash, or any zero-evaluation agent triggering its composite count (`audit/criteria.md:472–476`). The November 2 decision explicitly preserves that rule.

## 2. Define the estimand before defining failure handling

**Outcome this could change: whether C4.5 supports a false-discovery claim, an operational-reliability claim, or neither.**

For campaign \(j\), the false-discovery endpoint should be:

\[
Y_j=\mathbf{1}\{\text{the frozen procedure promotes at least one strategy}\}.
\]

Under a genuine complete null, every promotion is false, so \(E[Y_j]\) equals both campaign-level family-wise error and complete-null FDR. It is **not** mixed-market FDR, the number of false strategies, or project-wide error control. The paper makes these distinctions explicitly (`docs/whitepaper.tex:342–362,544–552`).

A swarm-null test checks the **particular self-stopping, history-sharing procedure**, including its actual search behavior. It cannot establish:

- safety of arbitrary 400-evaluation searches;
- power or useful research behavior;
- mixed-market FDR;
- safety on other generators or real markets;
- freedom from the model’s historical knowledge.

Stage 4 already disclaims calibrated swarm rates and power (`audit/criteria.md:427`). The inferential argument instead rests on untouched holdout data, conditional-null assumptions, the enforced loss bound, predictable betting and Holm—not the swarm smoke test (`docs/whitepaper.tex:330–354`).

There is also an existing null-model qualification: bankruptcy absorption can create positive expected benefit. Keep reporting those evaluations and any associated promotions; do not discard them to restore a cleaner null result (`audit/criteria.md:354`).

## 3. Separate observable non-discovery from censored evidence

**Outcome this could change: whether failed campaigns unfairly inflate the discovery count—or disappear and create a false pass.**

Use the following prospective treatment:

| Campaign outcome | False-discovery accounting | Consequence for acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Anything actually promotes | One false-discovery campaign, regardless of other failures | Counts against the discovery allowance |
| Voluntary stop or designed turn/wall limit, complete accounting and gate | Its actual promotion outcome | Low evaluation count alone is not failure |
| Proven failure before **any campaign-wide data-bearing disclosure** | No actual discovery | Does not establish swarm exercise; never silently replace it |
| One agent evaluates nothing while peers search | Keep the campaign’s actual gate outcome | Incomplete four-agent exercise; not a void campaign |
| Unplanned interruption, missing records, or gate failure leaves the registered outcome unresolved after exposure | Unknown, not a demonstrated false discovery | Charge against the conservative safety allowance |
| Isolation or accounting violation | Promotion evidence may be untrustworthy | Independently blocks stage acceptance |

**Exposure is campaign-wide.** An agent with zero evaluations may nevertheless have received peers’ results through history. Conversely, its absence can change what other agents see and propose. N2-5 cannot become a harmless “pre-data void” merely because A4 itself evaluated nothing.

**Why failures still count against safety:** stopping, crashes and missingness can depend on promising results, longer searches, particular strategies or resource consumption. Keeping only clean completions estimates risk among survivors, not risk of the registered procedure. This is informative censoring even without anyone deliberately cherry-picking.

A complete ledger can sometimes establish a terminal outcome despite a failed session. If a prospective protocol explicitly defines that failure as terminal and runs its unchanged gate over every recorded trial, its observed output measures that **failure-prone procedure**. It does not establish what an uninterrupted swarm would have discovered. Do not alternate between those targets according to which yields the nicer result.

The current implementation deliberately fails closed: session crashes count, exceptions count, and started-but-unrecorded campaigns are recovered as crashes (`gate/agent/campaigns.py:125–134,262–283`). A future design must replace that protection explicitly, not merely delete it.

Similarly, never remove missing finalists from Holm’s family. Stage 3’s real-run rule retains them with \(p=1\), return zero and an incomplete-batch label (`audit/criteria.md:402`). The swarm currently raises on a failed holdout process instead (`gate/gate.py:210–223`); changing that behavior would itself need registration.

## 4. A three-way result is warranted

**Outcome this could change: whether “nothing discovered” becomes an undeserved pass or an undeserved discovery failure.**

For a fixed ten-campaign cohort, record separately:

- **F:** campaigns with actual promotions;
- **U:** campaigns whose registered safety outcome remains unresolved;
- **exercise status:** whether each campaign demonstrably exercised the intended swarm.

A concrete prospective rule is:

- **Fail the discovery threshold:** \(F>1\).
- **Pass the smoke test:** all ten fixed records exist, exercise requirements hold, and \(F+U\le1\).
- **Inconclusive / not established:** otherwise.

Unresolved campaigns therefore still consume the conservative allowance; they are not called proven false discoveries. If more than one is unresolved, safety has not been established even with zero observed promotions. A binary stage-exit interface can simply treat **inconclusive as non-pass**. Explicit invariant failures remain independent stage failures.

N2-5 illustrates why this distinction matters: it provides an observed non-promotion from a partially functioning swarm, not a false discovery and not a clean four-agent exercise.

The precedent is C3.2 v2: its numerical tally was only one counted campaign, but **none evaluated anything**, so it established no procedure and was not accepted. V3 then added zero-evaluation protection (`audit/criteria.md:394–399`). Removing that protection without an alternative acceptance guard would repeat the same mistake.

## 5. Guard against vacuity with structural exercise—not search quotas

**Outcome this could change: whether an inactive swarm passes, or a functioning low-volume swarm fails.**

For a clean ten-campaign exercise claim, require direct evidence that:

- all four expected agent identities ran;
- each reached the permitted evaluation path and obtained an actual kernel evaluation result—not merely checks, budget refusals or syntax rejections;
- the campaign included a filled trial;
- actual cross-agent history receipt occurred;
- the gate accounted for every recorded trial.

These are minimal exercise checks, **not claims of meaningful breadth**. A campaign failing them should make the exercise conclusion incomplete, rather than manufacture a false discovery. Keep it in the fixed cohort; do not run until ten successful campaigns accumulate.

Do not resurrect 200 evaluations, require exhausted budgets, pad searches or continue agents after they stop. V1’s two functioning swarms made 33 and 36 evaluations and both counted solely because of that floor (`audit/stage4/null-summary-v1.json:113–139`; `audit/decisions.md:449–453`).

The brief expressly prefers a few distinct hypotheses (`gate/agent/brief.md:12–16`). Moreover, `evaluations_made()` includes non-budget-refused evaluation requests, including rejections and aborts; a positive count alone does not demonstrate productive search (`gate/agent/campaigns.py:59–66,350–361`).

The earlier Claude review’s assertion that fewer evaluations cannot make promotion likelier is too strong (`audit/reviews/2026-11-01-claude-c45-v2.md:8`). The GPT review correctly notes that adaptive proposals, multiplicity penalties and changing finalist families prevent such a monotonicity claim (`audit/reviews/2026-11-01-gpt-c45-v2.md:18–22`).

## 6. Judge correctness separately; report statistical weakness honestly

**Outcome this could change: stage acceptance despite broken accounting, or overstatement of weak null evidence.**

Keep C4.1–C4.4 independently mandatory: exact rewrites, canonicalization checks, sandbox/history integrity, budget enforcement and effective-N reporting. Their failures cannot be compensated by zero promotions (`audit/criteria.md:454–464`).

The final evidence check should verify exact cohort membership, uniqueness, A1–A4 records and every campaign’s audits—not only ten rows and an aggregate count. The present C4.5 test checks row count, counted promotions, history and total budgets, but does not itself enforce those additional conditions (`criteria/test_stages3to5.py:39–42`).

Report, without adding retrospective thresholds:

- actual promotions separately from conservative adverse outcomes;
- crashes, zero-evaluation agents and permission denials;
- attempted, successful, rejected, repeated and zero-fill evaluations;
- distinct filled trials, history use and stopping causes;
- screen, SPA, StepM, mechanism and holdout-stage reach;
- effective-N fragility and bankruptcies;
- the H400 comparison as descriptive, not a matched causal comparison.

`summary()` currently exposes the composite promotion tally, not separate actual-promotion and failure totals (`gate/agent/campaigns.py:535–551`).

For ten independent, fully observed binary campaigns, exact one-sided 95% upper bounds are approximately:

- **0/10:** 25.9%;
- **1/10:** 39.4%.

A procedure promoting noise with probability 20% would still satisfy “at most one of ten” about **37.6%** of the time. These are weak smoke-test outcomes, not validation of 5% control. Do not calculate a reassuring complete-case bound after dropping failed runs.

Power and downstream-path exercise deserve separate, prospectively designed tests. Requiring natural null campaigns to produce finalists would perversely penalize successful rejection of noise. The paper instead relies on cheaper baseline calibration and independently specified holdout checks (`docs/whitepaper.tex:356–360`).

## 7. Do not fork the harness merely to rescue this series

**Outcome this could change: which procedure is being judged and which earlier evidence remains applicable.**

No present brief or permission change is warranted by N2-5. The brief already repeatedly specifies `./mm-agent`, and the denial prevented an unpermitted command (`gate/agent/brief.md:3–4,25–34`; `audit/decisions.md:506–507`).

The existing canary checks that a model can write and reach a synthetic client. It is not proof that every research agent will follow the command syntax (`sandbox/canary.py:1–5,15–19,46–78`).

If repeated failures eventually justify improving the interface, make that an explicit new procedure—not an absolute-path exception quietly added to preserve a result. Both briefs, the daemon, prompts, permissions and shared sandbox participate in the harness identity (`gate/agent/harness.py:26–31`). Even changing `swarm.md` changes the shared hash.

Under `CLAUDE.md:47–49` and `audit/criteria.md:356`, that incurs fresh-null/refreeze obligations; applying a changed research procedure to a real arm requires a new, later holdout. Preserve old results as evidence for their old identity, not validation of its replacement.

## 8. Fresh evidence must follow the new decision—not merely have new filenames

**Outcome this could change: whether v3 is prospective evidence or a rule fitted to v1/v2 outcomes.**

Any v3 needs reviewed, dated, human-authorized criteria, committed before its campaigns begin. Changing a frozen acceptance condition is not automatically a safe amendment just because the promotion gate is unchanged (`CLAUDE.md:53–65`; `audit/reviews/2026-11-01-gpt-c45-v2.md:35–41`).

Require:

- a fresh, fixed campaign-ID list and thus fresh markets, search trajectories and seeds;
- no v1/v2 rows judged under v3, and no repurposing v2’s remaining IDs;
- frozen procedure/input identities and prospective interruption rules;
- fresh per-campaign audits and exercise evidence.

Unchanged, independent component evidence need not all be regenerated: canonicalization results can remain applicable if their implementation is unchanged; holdout-test checks can remain applicable if its algorithm and assumptions are unchanged. Regression and new-run integrity checks still apply.

Fresh N2 draws are fresh **campaign evidence**, not an untouched test of a generator family already inspected repeatedly. The paper explicitly warns against overfitting validation to its simulators (`docs/whitepaper.tex:524–525`). Preserve every earlier failure and version history.

## What could be wrong with this recommendation?

**Outcome this could change: cost, completion probability and the breadth of any eventual claim.**

- **It is stricter about exercise.** Requiring ten fully exercised swarms can leave an expensive cohort inconclusive because of harmless agent behavior. That is intentional conservatism, but may be disproportionate for a smoke test.
- **Exercise is not competence.** One successful evaluation per agent and one filled trial can still be trivial, repetitive searching. This cannot establish useful power or high-budget safety.
- **Classification creates discretion.** “Resolved,” “exposed” and “adequately exercised” could become new opportunities for post-result judgment. Freeze observable definitions and conservative defaults first.
- **The target can drift.** Measuring the actual failure-prone procedure and measuring an adequately functioning four-agent swarm are different questions. Report both; do not use the former’s zeros to validate the latter.
- **Repeated redesign remains selection.** Fresh IDs prevent replay, not continual criterion-shopping. Keep any v3 conditional on a genuine design need, preserve v2’s eventual verdict, and do not turn successive smoke tests into a calibrated-rate claim.
