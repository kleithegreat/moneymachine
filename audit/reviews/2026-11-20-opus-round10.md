## 2. Soundness of the twin (D230)

**What I accept**, after checking each derivation by hand:
- **The sandwich** (`p ≤ q ≤ r`, `nearestBits p = nearestBits r` ⇒ `nearestBits q = nearestBits p`) holds unconditionally:
  - **Signs:** a negative nonzero rational's bits are at least `2^63`, because its magnitude is at most `infBits` and never wraps, and a nonnegative one's are below it. So `p < 0 ≤ r` contradicts the hypothesis. `p = 0` forces `r` to round to `+0`, and then `q` does too.
  - **Magnitude:** `mag` is monotone in `|q|`. Within a binade, `roundHalfEven` is monotone. Across binades, `(e+1022)·2^52 + m` with `m ≤ 2^53` reaches at most `(e+1024)·2^52`, the next binade's least code. The subnormal clamp at `e = -1022` and the `min … infBits` saturation are both monotone.
- **`exp`'s nesting** holds for `n ≥ expStart x`. `s_{n+1} − s_n = t_n/2`, and `t_{n+1} = t_n·a/(n+2) ≤ t_n/2` because `n+2 > 2a`. `s_n − t_n > 0`, since `t_n ≤ term_n < s_n` once `n ≥ 1`, and at `x = 0` `t = 0`. The `x < 0` branch nests because its reciprocals reverse the order.
- **`log`'s nesting** holds for every `n` and every `|w| < 1`, including `w = 0` and `w < 0` (the tail uses `|w|`). `T_n − T_{n+1} − |ΔS_n| = 2|w|^{2n+3}/(1−w²) · w²·(1/(2n+3) − 1/(2n+5)) ≥ 0`. Scaling by `e` of either sign and adding the `z` series keeps the nesting. `logSplit`'s `m` lies in [3/4, 3/2): its `guess` is within one of `log2 y`, and the branch puts `y/2^e` in [1, 2) before the halving. So `z` lies in [−1/7, 1/5).
- **`firstDeciding_of_enclosure`** is correct as stated. A deciding ball at `m` makes `m` itself decide, by the sandwich on `L ≤ lo m ≤ hi m ≤ U`. So the least deciding `n*` is at most `m`, nesting gives `lo n* ≤ lo m ≤ hi n*`, and the sandwich gives equal results. A ball can decide only if X4 decides, so a cap NaN propagates. Theorem 4 by `rfl` (stage 3 is `firstDeciding` itself) is sound, and makes the fallback's speed irrelevant to correctness.
- **The ball enclosures** are sound by floor/ceil monotonicity, whatever their width. Stage 1's `m = expStart + 60` and stage 2's `m = expTerms` both lie in `[first, last]`. Stage 1's `ln2Ball1` (`m = 32`) and stage 2's `ln2Ball2` (`m = 90`) use the same `m` as their `z` series, as they must, since X4's `logLoAt y n` uses one `n` for both. `atanh`'s oddness handles `zneg`. `e < 0` swaps `ln 2`'s ball ends. For `x ≥ 0`, `slo − thi` in `Nat` is guarded by `thi < slo`.
- **Edges I traced through the ball:**
  - `x = ±2^-1074`: the ceil chain grows by one ulp a term, and the result decides `1.0`.
  - `x = −2^-54`: `2^-109` from a midpoint, within stage 1's width.
  - `(709.78, 710)`: both ends round to `+∞`.
  - `(−746, −745.13)`: `e < −1075`, so `+0`, as X4 gives.
  - Subnormal results: `e' = −1022` caps the left shift at 1,074 bits.
  - Overflow: `e ≥ 1024` exits early, and `m = 2^53` at `e = 1023` lands exactly on `infBits`.

**No input found where `expFast` or `logFast` would differ from `Exact.exp`/`Exact.log`.** None can, if the proofs check (see F5 for the one way around them). My transcription of stages 1 and 2 agreed with X4 on all 4,020 `exp` arguments I tried.

### F3. Stage 2's width as stated is impossible, and building to it breaks the proof (text)

- **What it breaks:** step 5x, sub-step 5, which says stage 2 at `(260, 520)` has "relative width `< 2^-512`, strictly tighter than X4's own cap tail `2^-479` on every input". Part G says the same.

That is about `2^-481` of `e^a` near `a = 200` (my evaluation of `ln(2a^{n+1}/(n+1)!) − a` at `n = 2a + 260` gives −334 nats there, and −496 at `a = 745`). The ball's rounding term, by the report's own bound `2^{23−P}`, is `2^-497`, not below `2^-512`.
- **The outcome it could change:** none on outputs. A builder who took the sentence literally and tightened stage 2's tail below X4's could not prove `expFast_eq`, and would hit a wall. The claim that "stage 3 = 0" still holds, since `2^-480 ≪ 2^-120`.

## 3. Can a wrong twin still pass?

In `~/.cache/mm-r10-review/cs/Cs.lean`, `ops.f := slowId` with `@[csimp] @slowId = @fastId` compiles `ops`'s closure to `fastId`'s body. ### F5. `implemented_by` and `extern` pass every planned check (design; an existing gap, X3's too)

- **What it breaks:** the claim that `checks.engine-proofs`, with three admitted axioms only, mechanically backs X6.
- **Construction:**
  1. Write `def expFast := exp` with `@[implemented_by expBallUnproved]`, or put `@[implemented_by]` on `exp` itself.
  2. X6's `@exp = @expFast` is then `rfl`, its axiom set is empty, and `check-proofs.sh` passes, since it collects the axioms of theorems only.
  3. The compiled engine nevertheless runs `expBallUnproved`.
  - It passes test 12 and B4 wherever the unproved ball happens to agree with MPFR on the 3.27 million arguments and on B4's streams. It need not agree anywhere else, for example at a midpoint case no corpus holds.
  - `@[extern]` does the same through C.
  - A builder who hits a wall at `@[csimp]` (wall 1's last resort, or a proof that will not close) has exactly this shortcut.
- **The outcome it could change:** a kernel output that differs from X4 while every check passes.
- **Defect:** of the design's mechanical guard. **Fix at the cause:** the proof check also fails on any constant in an `Engine` module that carries `implemented_by` or `extern`, and on any `unsafe` definition. (`partial` cannot be proved through, so it needs nothing.) It is one more loop in `check-proofs.sh`'s `#eval`, and it guards X3 and every later twin too.
