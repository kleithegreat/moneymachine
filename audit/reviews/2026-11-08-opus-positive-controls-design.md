## Verdict

**Redesign the matrix before freezing; the question, the four-stage attribution and the sentinel idea stand.** The design answers the human's literal ask, but most of what its 48 campaigns would measure is already known or computable: confirmation at 360 steps is ~0 by the paper's own S4a algebra and by stage 2 r2 (0 of 100 promotions in N2-s1, s2, s3 at 1,080 steps), and the oracle and baseline arms re-run r2's mixed sweep at 8 instead of 100 markets per cell. The only quantity that needs model usage, the agent's discovery and selection, gets the least precision. Four of the seven criteria fail while the claim holds, with probabilities from about 24% to near certainty (F3–F7). The cost cap rests on linear turn scaling that the recorded sessions contradict (F6). Nothing reuses spent evidence or protected data, and the harness hash is still the frozen one (`6a335714…`, recomputed).

## Findings, ranked

**F1. Most outcomes are predetermined or already recorded (changes: what the study can teach, and whether its cost is justified).** PC2 itself derives that even the oracle almost never confirms (log K₃₆₀ ≈ 0.034 and 0.072 against 3.0). Stage 2 r2 already ran this positive control for the baseline on N2 with the same 550-day search: G400 at oracle net Sharpe 1/2/3 had campaigns with a screened candidate 24/71/100%, finalists 13/25/76%, promotions 0/0/0 of 100 each (`r2-finalists.json`, `sweeps.json`). Every selected campaign in the proposed matrix is therefore "selected, not confirmed" before it runs, and PC4's "if expression/discovery succeeds but selection/confirmation does not, redesign" has already fired for confirmation. The design never cites r2's planted cells. What is unknown: whether the agent evaluates a recovering program (discovery) and whether the gate keeps it (selection). Spend the study on that, and state confirmation as derived, adding for each recovered finalist the holdout length its truth mean would need (an input to the queued better-powered B = 1 test).

**F2. The oracle is not an upper bound, and PC2's selection prediction is contradicted by the record (changes: the cells' plausibility labels, the "upper bound" claim, and how misses are read).** PC2 predicts 0.002/0.043 screen survival for a true candidate at S = 1/2. r2 observed 24%/71% of campaigns with a screened candidate, at a higher hurdle (N ≈ 393), and 0 of 300 in every null cell. The reason is that the oracle (`0.1·a` on all 12 coins) is a poor implementation of the plant. It holds ten noise coins under a common factor with λ = 0.83, and it rebalances every step. In my rough model of the draft's synthetic-funding N2 (not the kernel), the oracle at net S ≈ 1.4 sits beside an `xs_demean`-plus-smoothing variant at ≈ 3.3, and the oracle loses about 2.6 bps a step to turnover (β = 0 gives oracle S ≈ −2.3). "S = 1" therefore labels the oracle, not the edge: the expressible family in that cell reaches about 2–3. Fix: pre-register a small reference family (raw, demeaned, smoothed), pick by development net Sharpe, and calibrate on it, or keep the oracle and report the best reference beside it. Drop "upper bound" except for "knows the rule's form, N = 1".

**F3. PC.C4 fails almost surely while attribution works (fail while true).** Recovery needs both lower endpoints above 0 at t₁₉ = 6.51 (checked), and non-recovery needs an upper endpoint at or below 0. Any program with a true net Sharpe in about (−0.46, 0.46) over 200 years is unresolved. That band includes every low-turnover unrelated program an agent tries (slow trend, small constant tilts), whose paired gain and net mean are both ~0. In any campaign with no certified recovery, such a program decides between "did not discover" and "discovered, not selected", so it can change the outcome. The criterion then requires zero unresolved, and fails. Fix: define recovery by materiality on the paired gain, a lower endpoint above a stated fraction (say half) of the reference's paired gain, and non-recovery by the gain's upper endpoint falling below that fraction. Because the matched β = 0 markets share innovations, unrelated programs have a tiny paired-gain SE and resolve. Keep net mean > 0 as a separate condition for recovery. Derive the fraction from the claim ("captures the plant materially").

**F4. PC.C6 pre-fails on a known defect (changes: C6 and therefore the study's exit, as written).** I ran `test_reporting_trace_includes_boundary_fill_costs` unmasked on a fresh build. Holdout returns, `final_equity` and `total_return` are correct. The holdout trace's fills and the summary's fees, slippage, turnover and fill count omit the boundary fill. The oracle rebalances at the report boundary in effect always, so a witness on "boundary fills, fees/funding" fails on the frozen kernel by construction, and the draft says that failure fails the criterion. Fix: check what the gate consumes (returns, total return, start-of-window normalization, claim and result counts) exactly against the witness. Name the finding-6 fields as a recorded qualification (`audit/reviews/2026-11-03-gpt-hunt-kernel.md`, finding 6), not a judged condition.

**F5. PC.C2's ±10% tolerance blocks a correct calibration about a third of the time (fail while true; a number without a derivation).** β̂ makes a fixed 20-market dev bank hit the target exactly. Its true Sharpe is off by the bank's error, SE ≈ √(2190/438,000) = 0.071 annualized, and validation adds an independent 0.071. Validated minus target is about N(0, 0.10), so it falls outside ±0.1 with probability 0.32 at S = 1 and 0.05 at S = 2 (iid; t₅, GARCH and the shared funding source make it worse). The draft's response is "uncalibratable, start nothing". Fix: drop the tolerance gate. Report β̂ and the validation Sharpe with its CI, or pool both banks. Any check must be sized from the SE.

**F6. The cost estimate is not an upper bound (changes: the budget request, C7, and C3/C4 through mid-session crashes).** GPT's figures reproduce exactly from `null-v3.jsonl`: 1,318 turns, $26.44, 1.72 five-hour windows, 0.14 weekly. But usage grows superlinearly in turns, because each turn rereads the whole context. Over the 20 sessions, a log-log fit of five-hour use against turns gives an exponent of 1.8, and cost 1.85. A quadratic fit predicts 0.50 of a five-hour window for one 200-turn session, against the linear 0.26 (and 0.58 from the log-log fit). Sixteen full sessions are then 8.1–9.3 windows. At the measured weekly-to-five-hour ratio (0.081 in stage 3, 0.091 over stage 4's larger deltas), that is about **65–75 weekly points, not 34**, so the 40-point cap binds once sessions average about 140 turns. Expected use is lower: at the null sessions' 66 turns it is about 12 points, at 100 turns about 22. The admission rule's 4.18/16 = 0.26 per session is also too small. Under the 0.8 ceiling, a session admitted at 0.54 can hit the five-hour limit mid-run, and that session is a crash, which makes its campaign unresolved. Admit on the 200-turn projection (≈0.5–0.6), and give the human the worst case honestly. Weekly deltas are recorded at 0.01 resolution (14 sessions at 0.01, 6 at 0.00), so derive weekly use from the five-hour deltas.

**F7. PC.C3 fails on a benign denial (fail while true; precedent C4.5 v3 N2-8).** `audit_holds` fails when a tool name outside the permitted set appears at all, even if it was denied (`session.tool_use`). In stage 3 v3 and stage 4 v3, 1 of 60 agent sessions did this, so at least one of 16 does with probability about 24%, and "true for all 16 sessions" then fails the criterion although isolation held. Fix: separate a denied call (isolation working, evidence intact) from an executed out-of-set call. Only the second is unresolved.

**F8. Use paired markets, as stage 3 did (changes: interpretability of each miss; compute).** The independent-arm rationale does not hold. Stage 3 ran arms A and B on one market with one joint batch and per-arm Holm, and an oracle evaluated in its own ledger after the searchers freeze discloses nothing. With 550 search days, a market's realized plant Sharpe has SE ≈ 0.8, so whether the agent "should" have kept something depends heavily on the market. With pairing, the oracle on the same market conditions each agent miss: if the gate drops even the oracle there, the market and gate explain the miss, not the agent. Sixteen markets instead of 48.

**F9. The model-free arms need no austerity (changes: the baselines' precision).** Oracle selection at N = 1 is about Φ(S·√(3300/2190) − 1.645) = 0.34 and 0.79. At n = 8 those give "inconclusive" with probability 0.74 and 0.63. These arms cost compute only. Run them at r2's 100 markets per cell, or simulate the oracle directly, and cite r2 for G400.

**F10. n = 8 labels only extreme rates (changes: whether the agent result is conclusive).** With "usually" at 7 or more of 8 and "not usually" at 1 or fewer, a true rate of 0.2 is inconclusive with probability 0.50, 0.3 with 0.74, 0.5 with 0.93 and 0.8 with 0.50 (`prec.py`). The arithmetic checks (half-widths 0.3585/0.3430; 0.3123, 0.5293, 0.4707). But the 0.35 target is tied to no decision, and the plausible agent selection rates are intermediate. At the same 16 sessions, one effect cell gives a half-width of 0.25 (n = 16) instead of two cells at 0.34. I suggest the S = 1 oracle cell, whose best expressible variants already reach about 2–3 (F2).

**F11. The sentinel duplicates an existing test and exercises less of the stage 3 path than it (changes: what C6 certifies).** `gate/tests/test_agent_real.py` already runs `campaigns.real()` end to end with nonempty finalists: stubbed model, real kernel, daemon, ledger and claim. It covers promotions committed before diagnostics, an aborted finalist at p = 1 in its family, a kill after the claim, and recovery after a crash at each step. PC5/PC6's new driver composes `gate.freeze`, `gate.release` and `commit_promotions`, but not `campaigns.report` or `real`, so "exercises the nonempty stage-3 path" overstates it. Q3: it spends no real holdout (synthetic ids, fresh markets) and does exercise freeze, claim, single-release refusal and committed-row accounting. Simpler and stronger: extend that test with a full-geometry market (730/180/670, 360 returns), the oracle as forced finalist, an independent witness for returns (refmm replay of the full market sliced at the boundary, or a spec-first reconstruction), and a real SIGKILL of `mm holdout` after its claim. The existing test fakes that kill by writing `spent.jsonl`.

**F12. The synthetic funding source is not required (changes: comparability, calibration work, plausibility).** N2's frozen funding blob covers search-period data [2024-09-30, 2026-04-03), before stage 3's embargo, and C3.2 and C4.5 already used it. No holdout rule forbids it. Replacing it gives up comparability with r2 and with the stage 3/4 null markets, forces a new calibration (F5), and has every market resample one frozen 550-day path. If the real reason is something else, such as the oracle prediction's 10–90% share under mostly-floor real funding, state it and derive it. Otherwise reuse `campaigns.series_inputs`/`null_market` unchanged.

**F13. Truth-assay compute as specified is about 1,470 core-hours (changes: schedule; the "reuse" claim).** `sweep.net_returns` generates and writes a market for every call (41 s per ten-year N2 market measured, `audit/stage2/campaign-profile/memory/calibration-N2-ten-year.json`), so 128,640 replays cost about 1.5M s. Streaming every program through one `mm eval -` per shared market (as `search.Evaluator` does) costs about 25 core-hours. The paper's "shared generated markets" needs that code, and should say so.

**F14. Smaller points (outcome: none judged, unless noted).** (a) `campaigns.null_market` plus `REPORT_FROM_DAY["agent"] = 670` already gives the 730/180/670 → 360 geometry, so no "geometry adapter" or 60-day caveat is needed, and the contrast with `reference/differential.py` is beside the point. (b) `brief.md` and `research.md` point the agent at funding dislocations, so the planted family is hinted. The claim must say "a hinted family", or a positive agent result will be over-read. (c) Agent selection is easier than G100's partly because N is smaller: the hurdle is 3.27 at N = 11, the null sessions' median, against 4.18 at N = 100. Report N beside every selection rate. (d) Name the kernel pin, `3a732069…` (C3.2/C4.5), and how it is reproduced: my build under another `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` hashed `b88c15e6…`. (e) Erasure: §17 and the draft restate each other almost line for line. The criteria should state what and cite the paper for why. Stage 7's roadmap rewrite and the deferral sentence correctly follow decision (a). (f) The 40-point request is the human's to approve (spend beyond an approved budget). The draft says so.

## Checked and sound

The numbers check: the log-wealth and horizon algebra, the DSR hurdle 4.18 at N = 100, t₁₉ = 6.51, the Clopper–Pearson figures, and the usage sums. The ids are disjoint from every existing family, and market seeds derive from them. No input comes from a holdout, an embargo, the forward window or live data. The forced finalist bypasses only search selection and leaves the betting and Holm thresholds alone. Attribution substeps are decidable from what the machinery records: `search_decision`'s screened list, SPA p-value and finalists, `mechanism_check` per finalist, and `holdout_decision`'s return, p-value and Holm. `harness_hash()` today equals `audit/stage3/harness.json`, so no fork is needed, unless a flake update moves the `claude` store path.

## Simpler design answering the same question

Use N2 with its frozen funding through `campaigns.null_market` (pilot geometry and costs). Pre-register a reference family (F2) and one effect cell (F10). On 16 paired markets, run the agent, G100 and the reference, with one joint holdout batch per market and per-arm Holm, exactly stage 3's design. Run the reference and G100 at 100 or more extra markets, compute only. Truth by materiality on the paired gain (F3), streamed (F13). State confirmation as derived, plus the holdout each recovered finalist would need (F1). Make the sentinel a test (F11) and scope C6 per F4. Expected cost is about 12–25 weekly points, with an honest worst case near 70 (F6). This keeps the human's three arms and four-way attribution, and puts the model budget where the answer is actually unknown.
