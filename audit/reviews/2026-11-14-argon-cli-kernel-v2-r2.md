### Findings

#### 1. `M = W / B` is not a supermartingale under zero skill: five zero-skill mechanisms make `M` drift to 20, while an exact test crediting market-wide timing under unknown constant/exchangeable drift exists
- **Outcome changed:** Judged score and result (inflates Study 2's forward confirmer Type-I error far above $5\%$ under zero skill, while blinding the test to genuine market-wide dip timing).
- **Same cause as Round 1?** New cause introduced by the re-derivation of Study 2 around `M = W / B` ([architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L257-L263)).
- **The claim:** [architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L257-L262) (§6.1) and [ledger.md](audit/program/ledger.md#L404) (`D12`) claim that defining $B_t = B_{t-1}(1 + e_{t-1} r_t)$—where $e_{t-1}$ is the account's long notional over equity at the 5-minute step's start and $r_t$ is the equal-weight mean of the listed coins' mid returns over the step—makes $M_t = W_t / B_t$ a non-negative supermartingale ($\mathbb{E}[M_t / M_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le 1$) that removes market drift along any exposure path and reaches $20$ with probability $\le 5\%$ for any rule without skill, and that no exact test valid under unknown drift can credit market-wide dip timing.
- **Concrete construction:**
   Over step $t$, $\frac{M_t}{M_{t-1}} = \frac{1 + R_{W,t}}{1 + e_{t-1} r_t}$, so:
  $$\frac{M_t}{M_{t-1}} - 1 = \frac{R_{W,t} - e_{t-1} r_t}{1 + e_{t-1} r_t}.$$
  Five zero-skill rules make $\mathbb{E}[M_t / M_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] > 1$ and $\mathbb{E}[\Delta \log M_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] > 0$:
  1. **The 5-minute step-start `e` loophole ([architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L253-L258), [kernel-v2.md](audit/program/kernel-v2.md#L166)):** `B` reads exposure $e_{t-1}$ **only at the start of each 5-minute report step**, while `mm2` ticks every `tick ≥ 1` s (`A4`) and fills continuously (`A7`).
     - A zero-skill rule that buys the basket at $t_{k-1} + 5\text{ s}$ and closes at $t_k - 10\text{ s}$ is flat at every 5-minute boundary ($e_{k-1} \equiv 0$), so $B_k \equiv 1$ forever while $W_k$ captures $95\%$ of every 5-minute return ($M_k \equiv W_k$).
     - Even for the real vault, **every cycle starts flat** ($e = 0$ on that coin at the start of the 5-minute step in which the first buy fills), and $10\%$ of the vault's cycles last under $6$ minutes ([facts-kernel-v2.md](audit/program/facts-kernel-v2.md#L143)). On every step where a cycle opens (or adds ladder rungs), $B$ credits $e_{t-1} = 0$ (or pre-fill exposure) while $W$ earns the post-fill return over the rest of the 5-minute step.
  2. **Denominator Jensen convexity wedge in a zero-drift martingale market ($\mu_c \equiv 0$):** Suppose every coin is a zero-drift martingale ($\mathbb{E}[r_{c,t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0$). A zero-skill rule holding a coin (or subset of coins) $p$ at start-of-step exposure $e_{t-1}$ has:
     $$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{M_t}{M_{t-1}} - 1 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right] \approx e_{t-1}^2 \left(\text{Var}(r_t) - \text{Cov}(r_{p,t}, r_t)\right) = e_{t-1}^2 \sigma_B^2 (1 - \beta_{p,B}),$$
     $$\mathbb{E}\left[\log \frac{M_t}{M_{t-1}} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right] \approx \frac{1}{2} e_{t-1}^2 \left(\sigma_B^2 - \sigma_p^2\right).$$
     For a low-beta/low-vol coin like BTC or TRX ($\sigma_p = 45\%$, $\beta_{p,B} = 0.55$) against the 16-coin altcoin basket ($\sigma_B = 80\%$) at $e = 2$, even with **zero drift on every coin** ($\mu_c \equiv 0$), $M_t$ has expected simple drift $+115.2\%$/yr, expected log drift $+87.5\%$/yr ($\text{Sharpe} = 1.18$), and crosses $M = 20$ within two years in **$27.4\%$ of paths** ($> 5\times$ the $5\%$ bound).
  3. **Mechanical high-beta selection by a fixed-percentage dip ladder:** In the vault's 5,806 fills ([facts-kernel-v2.md](audit/program/facts-kernel-v2.md#L127)), $69\%$ of fills occur in high-volatility, high-beta altcoins (HYPE, ADA, SOL, LINK, XRP, DOGE, $\beta_{c,B} \approx 1.35$) because fixed percentage dip thresholds ($-2.25\%$ to $-9.85\%$) trigger far more often on high-beta coins than on BTC/BNB/TRX. In a $+80\%$/yr bull market with zero timing skill, $(\bar{\beta}_{\text{held},B} - 1)\mu_B = (1.35 - 1)(0.80) = +28\%$/yr of passive beta drift flows into $M_t$.
  4. **Funding-rate asymmetry (`W` is funded perps, `B` is unfunded mids):** Under [kernel-v2.md](audit/program/kernel-v2.md#L145) (`A9`), `W` pays/receives hourly funding $-q \cdot O \cdot \rho$, whereas `B` ([architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L258), [kernel-v2.md](audit/program/kernel-v2.md#L166)) is built from **mid price returns alone** (`r`). In an efficient perp market where $\mathbb{E}[\Delta \text{mid}_c / O_c + (-\rho_c)] = 0$, a zero-skill rule holding coins with negative funding $\rho_c < 0$ (e.g., $-0.005\%$/h $= -43.8\%$/yr) has $\mathbb{E}[R_{W,t}] = 0$ while $\mathbb{E}[r_t] = \rho_c < 0$, so $M = W / B$ drifts up by $+43.8\% \cdot e$ per year.
  5. **Undefined mids in `r` across gaps and TON's empty book ([kernel-v2.md](audit/program/kernel-v2.md#L44), [facts-kernel-v2.md](audit/program/facts-kernel-v2.md#L115)):** Inside a gap (`> G`), a coin remains listed (`A3`), so `A12` ("every listed coin's mid") outputs `Undef` (`NaN`), making `r`, `B`, and `M` undefined unless `r` restricts to coins with defined mids at both ends of the step.
  6. **An exact test crediting market-wide timing under unknown constant/exchangeable drift exists:** Fable's impossibility argument assumes the null allows *adversarial time-varying drift* $\mu_t = f(\mathcal{F}_{t-1})$ that moves in lockstep with the strategy's signal $e_{t-1}$ (which defines market timing to be inside the null). Under the standard null where market returns $(r_1, \dots, r_T) \in [-1, R_{\max}]^T$ have an **unknown constant (or exchangeable) drift $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$** and the strategy's exposure $e_{t-1}$ has no timing skill ($r_t$ conditionally exchangeable with past returns given the unordered multiset $\{r_1, \dots, r_t\}$, the complete sufficient statistic for the unknown return law including $\mu$), conditioning on the sufficient statistic (or pairing step $t$ with adjacent/running exposure $\bar{e}_{t-1}$ via $X_t = (e_{t-1} - e_{t-2})(r_t - r_{t-1})$) gives $\mathbb{E}_0[X_t \mid \text{order statistics}] \equiv 0$ for **every** unknown drift $\mu$, while under positive timing $\text{Cov}(e_{t-1}, r_t) > 0$, $\mathbb{E}_1[X_t] > 0$, so the bounded-betting martingale $\prod_t (1 + \lambda X_t)$ is exact ($\le 5\%$) and confirms market-wide timing.
- **Smallest design change (hypothesis):** Define the benchmark step return on a **fill/tick-weighted basis** (or update $B$ at each fill event so intra-step entries/exits match $W$'s exact holding interval), include each held coin's **own mid return minus its funding rate** in $B$ (so $B$ is the self-financing passive perp portfolio holding the account's start-of-interval coin weights, eliminating $\beta_c \neq 1$, the Jensen wedge $e^2 \sigma_B^2(1 - \beta_c)$, funding asymmetry, and gap-membership shifts; this tests maker-fill and intra-step execution edge cleanly), and report the paired/demeaned timing martingale alongside it to measure market-wide/coin dip timing.

#### 2. "Nothing bets more than the account" is false because a ladder's conditional step loss $L_{t-1}$ is known and $\ll 1$ on $>95\%$ of steps; conversely, at leverage $G > 1$, `A11`/`V7` breaks exactness by clipping losses at zero
- **Outcome changed:** Judged score and result (understates achievable exact power at $G = 1$ by $>10\times$, while allowing leverage $G > 1$ to inflate Type-I error above $5\%$ via loss truncation and denominator blow-up).
- **Same cause as Round 1?** **Yes (for the $G > 1$ half):** at leverage $G > 1$, `A11`/`V7` is the **exact same cause as Round 1 Finding 4 (`D11-v2`)**—stopping/flooring at $W_t = \max(0, \widetilde{W}_t)$ truncates negative equity on liquidation steps, turning a fair-market martingale into a submartingale.
- **The claim:** [architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L38) (§2.2), [architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L255-L261) (§6.1), and [ledger.md](audit/program/ledger.md#L403) (`D11`, `What Argon should attack first` #2) claim that: (a) against "mean at most zero, loss at most $L$", no exact test wealth can bet more than the unlevered account itself ($\lambda = 1$, because $L = 1$), forcing the unlevered account to be nearly idle ($\sigma \le 0.5$, power $\approx 0.01\text{--}0.05$); and (b) at leverage $G > 1$ ([human-answers.md](audit/program/human-answers.md#L12) #2), exactness holds as long as a certificate of `V7` quotes that the venue bears any negative-equity shortfall (`W ≥ 0`, `L6 ★`) and the test stops "not confirmed" on liquidation or $1 + e \cdot r \le 0$.
- **Concrete construction:**
  1. **An exact wealth on an unlevered ($G = 1$) ladder's returns that grows $7\times\text{--}13.9\times$ faster than the account using NO bound the rule lacks:**
     - Ville's theorem requires the step betting fraction $\lambda_{t-1}$ to satisfy $\lambda_{t-1} L_{t-1} \le 1$ for the **predictable step loss bound $L_{t-1} \in \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ of step $t$**, not $\sup_t L_t = 1$.
     - At the start of step $t$ (or tick $\tau$), a long-only account ($G = 1$) holds positions of notional fraction $e_{t-1}$ and live resting buy orders of notional fraction $o_{t-1}$ (plus funding bounded by $0.04\%$/h $\times e_{t-1}$, [facts-kernel-v2.md](audit/program/facts-kernel-v2.md#L101)). Because spot/perp prices are non-negative (`A2`), even if every held coin and every coin with a live buy order drops $-100\%$ to $0.00$ during step $t$, the account's step loss cannot exceed:
       $$L_{t-1} = e_{t-1} + o_{t-1} \in \mathcal{F}_{t-1}.$$
     - In the vault's 2,019 cycles ([facts-kernel-v2.md](audit/program/facts-kernel-v2.md#L143)), $78\%$ never pass the first buy ($1 / 27.6 = 3.62\%$ of a full 1-coin ladder), average deepest holding is $1.99 / 27.6 = 7.21\%$, and the 10th-percentile time between successive buy fills is **12 minutes** ($> 2$ five-minute steps). A ladder program that activates only the *next* unfilled rung (or next 2 rungs) at each 1-minute deciding tick has $L_{t-1} = e_{t-1} + o_{t-1} = \frac{1 + 0.978}{27.6} = 0.0717$ ($7.17\%$) when holding rung 0 on one coin.
     - Therefore, the test wealth:
       $$V_t = V_{t-1}\left(1 + \lambda_{t-1} R_t\right), \qquad \lambda_{t-1} = \min\!\left(\frac{b}{\hat{\sigma}_{t-1}},\; \frac{1}{e_{t-1} + o_{t-1}}\right)$$
       bets up to **$\lambda_{t-1} = 1 / 0.0717 = 13.9\times$** the unlevered account's step return, is **strictly non-negative ($V_t \ge 0$) on every sample path without `V7` or leverage**, and lifts annualized test-wealth volatility from $0.25$ to $> 1.5$ (restoring two-year confirmation power from $0.00\text{--}0.05$ to **$0.59\text{--}0.80$** at Sharpe $2.0$).
  2. **Exactness FAILS at leverage $G > 1$ even when `V7` holds on the venue:**
     - **Loss clipping (`A11`/`V7`) breaks the null mean $\mathbb{E}[W_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le W_{t-1}$:** In [architecture.md](audit/program/architecture.md#L38) (§2.2), Fable notes that *"clipping a loss raises the mean and breaks the null."* At leverage $G > 1$ (say $e_{t-1} = 4$), a fair-price martingale ($\mathbb{E}[r_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0$) has $\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{W}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = W_{t-1}$ only for the **unfloored** equity $\widetilde{W}_t = W_{t-1}(1 + e_{t-1} r_t)$, which goes negative ($\widetilde{W}_t = -0.20 W_{t-1}$) on a $-30\%$ crash. When `A11` / `V7` floors negative equity at $0$ ($W_t = \max(0, \widetilde{W}_t)$) and stops the test "not confirmed," that is a free bankruptcy put option that raises the conditional mean:
       $$\mathbb{E}[W_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \mathbb{E}[\max(0, \widetilde{W}_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = W_{t-1} + \mathbb{E}[\max(0, -\widetilde{W}_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] > W_{t-1}.$$
       As proved in Round 1 Finding 4 (and reproduced in [arithmetic.py.txt](audit/program/arithmetic.py.txt#L167)), a zero-drift martingale with relative shortfall $\bar{\varepsilon}$ on liquidation reaches $20$ before liquidation with probability $20^{-1/(1+\bar{\varepsilon})} = \mathbf{5.76\% > 5\%}$ at $\bar{\varepsilon} = 0.05$ and **$13.57\% > 5\%$** at $\bar{\varepsilon} = 0.50$.
     - **Denominator blow-up in $M = W / B$ at $e_{t-1} > 1$:** When $e_{t-1} = 5$, if the held coin drops $-10\%$ ($1 + R_{W,t} = 0.50 > 0$, no liquidation) while the 16-coin basket drops $-19.9\%$ ($1 + e_{t-1} r_t = 0.005 > 0$), $M_t / M_{t-1} = 0.50 / 0.005 = 100 \ge 20$ in a single step, while paths with $1 + e_{t-1} r_t \le 0$ are stopped at $0$ ("not confirmed"), making $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{\{1 + e_{t-1} r_t > 0\}} M_t / M_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] > 1$.
     - **What a `V7` certificate must quote (for physical simulation fidelity):** It must quote Hyperliquid's cross-margin backstop liquidation / HLP deficit absorption rule showing that (i) cross-margin accounts have zero recourse beyond posted cross equity, and (ii) backstop liquidation occurs at the zero-margin price *inclusive* of fees (whereas `A10` L151 closes against book levels and then subtracts a $0.045\%$ taker fee, which can push a positive mark equity into artificial bankruptcy).
- **Smallest design change (hypothesis):** Keep the physical replica account unlevered ($G = 1$, or cap live exposure $e_{t-1} + o_{t-1} \le 1$ so $\widetilde{W}_t \ge 0$ holds mathematically without `V7` loss-flooring) and apply the predictable step multiplier $\lambda_{t-1} = \min(b / \hat{\sigma}_{t-1}, 1 / L_{t-1})$ with $L_{t-1} = e_{t-1} + o_{t-1}$ in the confirmer's wealth process $V_t$.

#### 3. Evidence allotment (`A7`, `E1 ★`, `F0 ★`): `pessimistic` still credits lots no seller offered across successive snapshots, between a quote and a print, and across resting slots on one quote
- **Outcome changed:** Judged score and result (inflates `pessimistic` maker fills in Study 2's forward run and Layers 2–3, and causes `build-plan.md` Step 8 Test 2 to contradict `kernel-v2.md` A7).

- **Concrete construction:**
  1. **Across successive snapshots (one resting seller fills all 5 ladder rungs in 5 snapshots):**
     - Suppose the 5 ladder buy slots rest at $p_1 > p_2 > p_3 > p_4 > p_5$ with sizes $S_1 = 1, S_2 = 1.93, S_3 = 3.80, S_4 = 7.47, S_5 = 14.68$ (total $27.88$ lots).

Now Slots 1–4 are already complete (`remainder = 0`), so Slot 5 ($14.68$ lots) is first in priority and sees $14.68 \ge 14.68$ ask lots below $p_5$—filling all $14.68$ lots of Slot 5 at $q_2$!
     - **Total filled by `pessimistic`:** $27.88$ lots across Slots 1–5 from **one** seller who only ever offered $14.68$ lots.
  2. **Between a quote and a trade print (one seller counted twice, and `build-plan.md` Step 8 Test 2 contradicts `A7`):**
     - **Path A (Print at $t_1$, Quote at $t_2$):** A resting buy order has size $10$ at $p = 100$. A seller has $5$ lots at $99$. Because `A7` L127 never checks `aggressor side` on trades and has no memory that those $5$ ask lots were already allotted at $q_1$, `pessimistic` allots the same $5$ lots a second time at $t_2$ to fill Slot 2 ($p_2 = 100$, size $5$)!

  3. **Within one quote across resting slots, and trades before an order's arrival quote (`A7` L115 vs `A12` L163):**

If a trade occurs at $t > \ell$ before that first quote, `A12` L163 ("orders whose life starts or ends by now do so; arrival fills; resting fills") leaves unspecified whether the order can fill as a maker on that trade before its arrival quote checks `alo` / taker marketability.

### Views on the Human's Two Questions (`human-answers.md` #1 and #4)

#### Question A: Which claim is most informative for Study 2 (`M` alone, `W` alone, both reported with one judged, or another)?
- **Recommendation (with derivation):** **Report both `W` (raw account wealth) and a corrected excess wealth `M* = W / B*` (along with the timing decomposition), and judge `M*`.**
- **Derivation:**
  1. **What `W` alone lets pass and misses:**
     - *Lets pass without skill:* Any rising market ($\mu_m > 0$) lets a zero-skill long-only rule reach $W \ge 20$.
     - *Misses:* Nothing if the account has sufficient volatility and earns in USD, but cannot distinguish strategy skill from passive crypto drift.
  2. **What Fable's `M = W / B` lets pass and misses:**
     - *Lets pass without skill:* As proved in **Finding 1**, Fable's 5-minute equal-weight mid basket `B` lets a zero-skill rule reach $M \ge 20$ via (i) intra-5-minute fills where $e_{\text{start}} = 0$, (ii) the Jensen convexity wedge $e^2 \sigma_B^2 (1 - \beta_{c,B}) > 0$ even when every coin has zero drift ($\mu_c \equiv 0$), (iii) high-beta coin selection ($\beta_{\text{held},B} > 1$) in a bull market, and (iv) negative funding carry ($\rho < 0$) paid into $W$ but omitted from mid-only $B$.
     - *Misses:* Market-wide dip timing ($\text{Cov}(e_t, r_{m,t+1}) > 0$), because matching step exposure removes any correlation between aggregate exposure and market return.
  3. **Why `M* = W / B*` (event-indexed, coin-matched, funding-inclusive numeraire) with `W` and the timing decomposition reported is maximally informative:**
     - Define $B^\star$ so that whenever the replica holds $q_{c,t-}$ of coin $c$ over an interval $(t_-, t]$ between fills/ticks, $B^\star$ earns the **same coin $c$'s mid return minus coin $c$'s funding payment** at exposure $e_{c,t-} = q_{c,t-} \text{mid}_{c,t-} / W_{t-}$ (and apply the predictable step multiplier $\lambda_{t-} \le 1 / (e_{t-} + o_{t-})$
from **Finding 2** at $G = 1$).
     - Then $M^\star = W / B^\star$ is an **exact** supermartingale that eliminates all five zero-skill leaks of Finding 1 (intra-step `e = 0`, cross-sectional $\beta_c \neq 1$, the Jensen wedge, and funding carry) and tests **maker-fill and intra-cycle execution edge** (whether buying dips on limit ladders and exiting on maker take-profits outperforms holding the same coin at mids over the exact same intervals).
