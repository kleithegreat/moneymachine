# Fact-check of the two Argon Hyperliquid reports (Claude Sonnet, fresh session), 2026-11-08 16:10 UTC (11:10 EST)

Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5.5. Checked `docs/research/2026-11-07-argon-hyperliquid-rate-limits.md` ("R1") and `docs/research/2026-11-07-argon-hip3-growth.md` ("R2") against hyperliquid.gitbook.io and the official Python SDK (raw GitHub), fetched 2026-11-08 between 16:00 and 16:10 UTC (11:00 to 11:10 EST). No Hyperliquid API call was made and no price, return or forward-window datum was read. Code was read on the `thesis-refreeze` branch (`thesis/market.py`, whitepaper T3 at `docs/whitepaper.tex` lines 705-711); `main` does not hold it. WebFetch summarises pages with a small model, so each quote is as returned by it and under 125 characters; the Terms page (`app.hyperliquid.xyz/terms`) is a JavaScript app and returned nothing, so Terms claims are checked only against the project's own certificate.

**Verdict.** The rate-limit mechanics R1 rests on are right and verified against the official docs: 1200 weight/min/IP, 20 base, the per-20-item and per-60-item surcharges, 500 rows per time-range response, 5000 retained candles, and the separate WebSocket limits (10 connections, 30 new/min, 1000 subscriptions, 2000 messages/min, 100 in-flight posts). Its arithmetic reproduces the code's reservations (45, 103, 20; 8,968 x 45 = 403,560). But three load-bearing claims for option (c) are not in the official docs: (1) that subscriptions are exempt from REST weight is only implied by the page's structure, never stated; (2) that a candle subscription "pushes updates until the bar closes" has no official text, and the documented `Candle` schema (`t T s i o c h l v n`) has no field marking a bar final; (3) that WS `post` requests cost REST weight is a guess (the docs are silent). R1 also never addresses the real constraint: the snapshot is a 524-request burst due at a fixed time, and its amortised plans (8.7 wt/min and so on) say nothing about it. R2's "35-DEX limit will not bind" answers the wrong question: the code's limit at B=120 is d <= 5 (T3: 20(1+d) <= 120), already exceeded by 10 registered dexes. R2 does, in passing, document the best fix (`allPerpMetas`, flat 20, returns every dex's contexts including `markPx`) without noticing it. Citations of "last updated N days ago" are unverifiable and look inferred; discount them.

## Arithmetic against the code and T3

- Reservations in `thesis/market.py` (`MAX_ITEMS = {"fundingHistory": 500, "candleSnapshot": 5000}`, floor): full candle page 20 + 83 = 103, full funding page 20 + 25 = 45, a 25-row funding tail 21, a short candle tail 20. R1 produces the same numbers, because it uses the same floor assumption (item 4). Neither the code nor R1 has a measured charge.
- T3: 348 x 21 + 278 x 45 = 19,818 per day = 13.76/min, as GPT checked; 8,968 x 45 = 403,560; 524 x 20 = 10,480 = 87.3 min at 120/min. All reproduce. R1's 4-cold-market figure (12,172 weight, 101 min) also reproduces and matches the "over 100 minutes" in the prompt.
- R1's steady-state 8.7 wt/min (daily 48-bar candle sweep + 24-bar funding sweep + hourly `allPerpMetas`) is correct arithmetic: (300 x 20 + 300 x 21 + 480) / 1440 = 8.9. But it spreads the work over a day; the thesis snapshot needs 524 requests each of whose newest daily bar becomes final only at 00:00 + 120 s, i.e. a burst, and a tail request cannot be fetched earlier. R1 proves the average fits and does not touch the wall.
- R1's "average 120 wt/min budget for the forecasting track" takes the 600 courtesy half as given. The 120 is 600 - 480 (recorder), and `venue/sources.toml` still says the recorder runs at 400 and the backfill at 200 (stale; `CLUSTER.md` line 50 shows 480). Raising the thesis share above 120 means spending beyond the approved 600, which only the human may do.
- R2's 720 comes from a different budget than the code: T3 gives `20(1+d) <= B` at B=120, so d <= 5, not 35. `thesis/market.py` computes `slots = limiter.budget // 20 - 1` = 5 and already prints SAMPLING CAPACITY EXCEEDED for 10 registered dexes. The "35" appears only as the 720-weight reading of a different allowance; the limit at stake is 5 and it is already crossed.

## Decision consequences

**Option (c), WebSocket candle closes.** What a certificate would honestly quote today: the endpoint `wss://api.hyperliquid.xyz/ws`; the candle subscription and `Candle` schema above; the six WS limits as a separate list; "Use websockets for lowest latency realtime data."; "You can use the API to record additional historical data sets yourself." It could not honestly claim that subscriptions are free of REST weight (item 11, only implied), that WS posts are or are not charged (item 12), that a candle's last update is final (items 14, 15), or the Terms: the human's 2026-10-23 decision names the read-only public info API, so a WS transport needs a new operator decision, not a quote. The outcome at stake is the equality of the stored close with the REST close: the REST close is accepted when `t + interval <= fetchedAt` plus 120 s, with no official finality field either way, so what must be shown (before any adoption, without touching the forward window) is that the last WS frame for a bar equals the REST bar after the delta; the docs cannot supply that. Budget: 348 daily + 176 hourly = 524 of 1000 subscriptions on the one IP; reconnecting resubscribes all 524 messages, so 2000 messages/min allows three full reconnects a minute, and the 30 new connections/min cap is not the binding one. Nothing else in `ingest/` or `thesis/` opens a WebSocket (grep), so the 1000 is currently unshared, but listings grow. Missed bars still need REST, so the wall is gone only in normal operation (agrees with GPT).

**Option (a), later session.** Unaffected by anything in the reports, except that all of its capacity maths hinge on the rounding rule (item 4): a ceil rule makes every tail 21 and the 87-minute figure 91, the 524-request plan 11,004 weight. The T3 derivation should say that the surcharge rounding is unmeasured, or measure it with a bounded probe (a human decision, since it is an API call).

**T3 and the recorder's share.** T3's 14/min and 45/min sums are exact for floor and about 5% low under ceil on the tails; the conclusion that the snapshot does not fit stands under both. The 600 courtesy ceiling and the 480/120 split are project choices, not Hyperliquid limits; the true limit is 1200 and anything above 600 is the human's. The stale 400/200 figures in the certificate should be corrected on the next certificate edit.

**The 35-DEX design limit (really the 5-dex initial wave).** R2's growth forecast (10 to 12 registered by 2027-01-31, 4 to 5 live) is a third-party-sourced estimate and is not needed: today's 10 already exceed 5. Round-3 finding B (spend the first cohort on all-delisted dexes) stands. The fix R2 names and does not name as one is `allPerpMetas`: one 20-weight call returns every dex's `markPx`, which would lift the d <= 5 wave limit entirely. It changes the mark source frozen into the manifest's sampling convention (per-dex `metaAndAssetCtxs` at H+5 s), so equality of the two responses' `markPx` is unestablished and the change is a refreeze-class decision with two reviewers.

## Material the reports missed

1. `allPerpMetas` as the single-call mark sampler (above), and the obvious conclusion that the dex-count limit is a request-count artefact.
2. The time-critical burst: no plan is offered for 524 requests due at a fixed hour, nor for a prewarmable subset (hourly tails prewarmable, daily not).
3. The operator is a US person; clause 1.6 of the Terms, which R1 quotes, bears directly on any new transport, and the certificate's decision is scoped to `/info`.
4. Rounding of the surcharge is undocumented, which every figure here depends on.
5. The recorder and thesis may run on different egress IPs (CLUSTER co-egress is the "shared-IP coordination" qualification); neither report tells whether the limit is on the IP as seen by Hyperliquid or per node.

## Ranked recommendations

1. Do not write a source-certificate quote for WebSocket "zero REST weight". Quote the separate limit list and the "lowest latency" sentence, state the exemption as an inference, and take a new dated operator decision for the WS transport (the 2026-10-23 decision names `/info` only).
2. Treat the final-candle question as open and require an empirical equality test (WS last frame versus REST after the 120 s delta) on data outside the forward window, such as testnet, before option (c) is adopted; use the existing `t + interval <= fetchedAt` plus delta rule on both transports and reconcile disagreements through REST without overwriting a used value.
3. Pick option (a) or (c) on their merits, not on R1: R1 supplies no evidence that bears on the 524-request burst. If (a), state in the dated criteria revision that capacity assumes the floor rule and name the measurement that would test it.
4. Add the measured surcharge rounding to the open items (one bounded probe, for the human to approve), because 20 versus 21 and 103 versus 104 change every reservation in T3.
5. Rewrite the "35 dex" statement wherever it lives as "5 dexes in the initial wave at B=120", note that it is already exceeded, and evaluate `allPerpMetas` for marks as a separate, reviewed refreeze item; do not cite R2's growth forecast as a reason to leave it.
6. Correct the certificate's stale recorder and backfill shares (400 and 200 against 480 deployed) at the next certificate edit.
7. Keep R1's practitioner 429 and S3 material out of any rule or paper statement (items 20 and 23); both reports remain leads.
