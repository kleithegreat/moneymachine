## The six requested attacks

### 1. D87 — shortened horizon versus longer replay

**Outcome tested:** rung-2 registration and the reported guard maximum.

Take the existing five-minute pair construction and put either an unaffordable charge or a price change exactly at midnight \(T\). The revised fold:

1. processes events and ticks strictly before \(T\);
2. values the guard using that day’s report-row prices;
3. only then processes the actions stamped \(T\).

The midnight charge therefore cannot erase the preceding day’s crossing. The shortened run also has a valuation without requiring a tick at its excluded endpoint. A final-span row after the day rows cannot enter that valuation.

**No remaining boundary counterexample found.**

This equality is for the **same guard rule and supplied mixes**. Recomputing the hindsight mix from a different record endpoint can legitimately produce another rule and another earlier wealth figure. That is not the midnight-order fault R1 identified.

### 2. D88 — slicing across spans and first quotes

**Outcome tested:** the synthetic bar, forecast and selected `(φ,d)`.

Within a span, the contributions telescope:
\[
x_1+(x_2-x_1)+\cdots +(x_n-x_{n-1})=x_n.
\]
Starting the next span from zero then adds its own terminal figure, rather than subtracting an unrelated previous-span value.

I tried the requested edges:

- **Two span endings inside one day:** both completed-span contributions enter that day’s accumulator before its final `day` row.
- **A span ending at midnight:** the day snapshot precedes the midnight actions. The old span’s subsequent duplicate endpoint contributes zero; events actually stamped midnight enter the following day.
- **The artificial final span:** its post-day duplicate contributes zero.
- **First quote inside the first day:** the first row contributes its whole cumulative figure, and the first price ratio uses the first defined price—not an undefined price at the horizon’s start.
- **No price in a day’s rows:** that coin is excluded from the synthetic bar and named, rather than silently dividing by an undefined starting price.

**No residual R2 construction found.** The added fixture checks the transformation, although its execution through the eventual engine remains a build obligation.

### 3. D89 — endpoint haircuts and forecast accuracy

**Outcomes tested:** rung forecasts and potentially the selected stake/depth.

The bound is now algebraic. Eligible pairs have positive daily `W`; every sampled haircut lies in \([0,1]\). Consequently,
\[
W_t^b
=\left(\prod_{s\le t}f_s\right)h_t
\le \prod_{s\le t}f_s=W_t.
\]
Neither block boundaries nor repeated recoveries can break it. The constant-price pulled-bid construction no longer manufactures wealth.

However, **using historical haircuts is not guaranteed to forecast better than using `W` alone**.

For example, let replay price wealth grow approximately 0.45% daily, with endpoint haircut \(h=1/2\) throughout. After 730 synthetic days, price wealth is about 26.5, but bid wealth is about 13.3: the corrected bootstrap forecasts no rung-1 crossing. If forward price growth remains the same but bids stand at prices, the forward ledger crosses 20; the price-only forecast was better. Grid-compatible approximations preserve the inequalities.

That construction changes the forecast and can change selection when another pair competes. **It does not reopen R3:** the forecast makes no guarantee of accuracy under a changed bid regime. Applying historical haircuts is a specified forecasting choice, not a theorem of superiority. Manufacturing wealth from a haircut recovery was a different, structural error, now removed.

### 4. D90 — replay return below minus one

**Outcome tested:** whether a registration proceeds, and with which forecast and pair.

Stopping for the architect is appropriate. Once the replay contains such a return, the specified bar’s domain has failed. Continuing by dropping that day, clipping the return, substituting a bar or allowing the builder to decide which paths survive would introduce an unspecified, result-changing choice.

Rejecting that replay from bootstrapping is conservative but defined. It does not erase an earlier completed forward crossing or add a new forward stopping rule.

**No blocker found.** The wall must remain a wall: any architect-approved replacement would need its own review before registration, not an undocumented fallback.

### 5. CS2.2’s sentence and readers

**Outcome tested:** “held,” “did not hold,” or “not judged.”

The sentence now names exactly the measured object: **daily volatility of price-valued test-ledger wealth**. The corresponding definition, `held` interface and forward-test requirements all use `W`.

The old bid-oscillation fixture remains useful as a *counter-test*: it demonstrates why substituting bid returns would change the answer. Its calculation of a bid-based diagnostic is not a surviving bid read by the judge.

The day-213 ending is also repaired at the right point. `held` reads ending information only within its selected lists; a later ending cannot retroactively turn the first 212 days into “not judged.”

**No residual R4 or ending-window defect found.** Equal price volatility need not imply equal bid-based crossing power; CS2.2 now explicitly disclaims that implication.

### 6. D92/O9 — what reaches the freeze

**Outcomes tested:** freeze timing and, through the resulting judged windows, CS2.1, CS2.2 and CS2.3.

The forbidden direct routes are closed in the specification:

- no later vault fills or fill counts before freeze decision-makers;
- no later page of the vault’s public record;
- no protected `inspect` output in tracked files, prompts or review bundles;
- no diagnostic output used as a registration input, subject to the expressly permitted recorder-health/gap exception.

This is a process boundary, not an engine-enforced personnel boundary. Registration review must check compliance; merely hashing the engine cannot establish it.

**But Fable’s stronger argument is false:** “every judged day follows registration” does not imply that permitted integrity knowledge cannot change anything judged.

A simple construction is enough. The advisor sees an allowed outage diagnostic and delays freezing by a day while restoring collection. With the same eventual program:

- a crossing on what would have been the first judged day can fall before the new start, changing **CS2.1**;
- shifting the first 212 days can move a high-variance day into or out of the list, changing **CS2.2**;
- shifting the first 61 days can include or exclude unmatched entries, changing **CS2.3**.

No vault-fill count or price disclosure is needed.

That is **result-changing freedom permitted by the human’s rule**, not proof that the prospective criteria are invalid. The windows remain determined before their outcomes are observed. It also does not, by itself, break the conditional supermartingale argument for CS2.1.

I therefore accept rejecting the mandatory separate integrity role, but **not** the assertion that integrity access is outcome-inert. Nor does permission to inspect for integrity authorize using those observations to tune the trigger or selector; the stated restrictions still prohibit that use.
