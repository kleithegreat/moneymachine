## Verdict

The report is a useful bibliography and a poor audit. Its "four foundational vulnerabilities" are three misreadings of what our gate claims and one power point that is overstated but partly right. (1) Search-period WRC/SPA/StepM breaking under adaptivity is true, and the paper already says so: everything on the search period is a selection rule with no error claim, and the only inferential step is the holdout, conditional on the search information (whitepaper.tex:313). (2) Autocorrelation "breaking" the supermartingale attacks an unconditional null we do not use: our null is the conditional one, E[r'_t | F_{t-1}] ≤ 0 at every step (whitepaper.tex:325–328; criteria.md:248 states that positive conditional means are deliberately outside it). (3) Bankruptcy and heavy tails: the report's own arithmetic (λ ≤ 1/2 needs r ≥ −2) is the paper's stated scope assumption, already flagged as assumed and owed in real markets (S3). (4) e-Holm "provably dominates" our Holm only on the same e-values. Ours uses Ville's running maximum, which e-Holm cannot use. Even so, the simulation below finds e-Holm on the terminal wealth K_T valid and no worse than the current rule in any cell, and much stronger when several finalists are real. It wins only at effects the pilot cannot reach (S4a), so it is a candidate for the queued better-powered test, not a defect. The adjuster the report prescribes for the running maximum is invalid as printed: its defining integral diverges. Two of the report's concrete fixes would add false discoveries under our null: clipping the score at −1 lets a negative-mean short-volatility stream pass almost surely, and AR(1) pre-whitening tests a different null that our H0 does not imply. Citations: no load-bearing paper was fabricated. The 2026 arXiv ids all exist and their titles and authors match. But several numbers and claims are attached to the wrong paper, and two citations are used to say something their sources do not say. Nothing here touches a frozen identity. The cheapest correct response is a few sentences in the paper, plus inputs to the already-queued "better-powered B = 1 betting test" (PROGRESS, Next; decisions.md:261), held to that work's own fresh criteria.

## Findings

Each finding states whether the claim holds for our design, the outcome it could change, whether it touches a frozen identity, and the response.

**1. StepM/SPA under adaptive search: true, already handled.** Counting every evaluation fixes cardinality, not adaptivity: an agent that reads earlier results can construct a candidate that is a function of the realized search data, and no bootstrap over the final family accounts for that. The paper concedes exactly this. "Everything computed on the search period … is a selection rule … it supports no error claim on its own. Romano–Wolf's guarantees … do not survive applying it to a family that was itself chosen on the same data" (whitepaper.tex:313). StepM's p-values are "used for selection only" (line 318). The holdout's guarantee is conditional on F_search (S3: P(p_i ≤ u | F_search, H_{0,i}) ≤ u), so search adaptivity cannot affect it, provided the finalists are F_search-measurable. Small correction to the report's description: the gate's N is distinct canonical strategies that filled at least once, not every evaluation (S5, whitepaper.tex:605). The budget is what counts every evaluation. Outcome: none. Action: none.

**2. Autocorrelation and the supermartingale: misreading.** The report treats our null as "zero unconditional alpha". Ours is H_{0,i}: E[r'_{i,t} | F_{t-1}] ≤ 0 at every t (whitepaper.tex:325–328). A zero-mean AR(1) return stream is not in H0, because it has positive conditional means after gains. Rejecting it is not a type I error by the stated claim, and criteria.md:248 says so explicitly: such returns "are predictable, so a strategy could exploit them, and they are not the gate's no-edge null". The null markets satisfy H0 by construction: the price is generated as c = o(1+f₁+f₂+f₃+ε)/(1−f₄) with E[ε | F_o] = 0 (NULLS.md, No edge). Any predictable position therefore has a conditional-mean-zero gross PnL, and costs make the net mean negative. Simulation A ran the frozen `holdout_pvalues` on 20,000 zero-mean Gaussian AR(1) streams (n = 546, sd 2%, ρ = 0, 0.3, 0.8). It produced zero rejections at 0.05 in every cell. The plug-in bet follows the running mean, not the lagged return, so it cannot harvest the autocorrelation at these lengths, even where H0 is false. Pre-whitening, the report's remedy, is not valid for our null: X̃_t = r_t − ρ̂_t r_{t−1} has conditional mean E[r_t|F] − ρ̂_t r_{t−1}, which is positive whenever ρ̂_t r_{t−1} < 0, even under H0. It tests Qu et al.'s different null. What survives is a sentence. The paper says what the null is, but nowhere says in words what a rejection does *not* certify: that the strategy, unscaled, has a positive unconditional mean. The net-return > 0 condition (whitepaper.tex:321) is the only guard on that reading. Outcome: the paper's claim (interpretation). No frozen identity. Action: one sentence in §8's holdout-test paragraph.

**3. Bankruptcy, leverage, heavy tails: already handled; the report adds nothing.** The report derives the paper's own condition: with λ ≤ 1/2, nonnegativity needs r ≥ −2. That is the paper's scope assumption, stated three times (whitepaper.tex:313, 329; S3, lines 578–586), and it is flagged "assumed, owed" for real markets and the frozen pilot. Upper heavy tails do not affect validity: λ ≥ 0, and a large positive r only raises a factor. The report's §1.2 concedes linear bets are valid for X ≥ −c. Heavy tails cost power, which the paper already reports as very low (S4a). Catoni and ψ_E alternatives need a known variance bound, or remain one-sided linear-type bets with no validity gain over ours. Outcome: power only. Action: none now. They are candidates for the queued better-powered test.

**4. e-Holm versus Holm on Ville p-values: the dominance claim does not apply; power depends on regime.** Hartog–Lei's dominance holds when both procedures see the same e-values, i.e. Holm on p = 1/e. Our p is 1/max_t K_t, smaller than 1/K_T. The running maximum cannot enter e-Holm (Hartog–Lei §3.2; Tavyrikov et al.). The valid e-Holm alternatives are therefore the terminal K_T, K stopped at a preset boundary, or an adjusted maximum, and each gives up Ville's free maximum. Holm on Ville p-values is valid under arbitrary dependence: it needs only marginal validity, which S3 gives, and the report agrees. Simulation C used m finalists, k of them with per-step mean μ and the rest null, iid normal returns with sd 2%, n = 546, and 2,000 replications. It compares Holm on 1/max K (current), e-Holm on K_T, and e-Holm on K stopped at m/α. No procedure rejected a null in any cell. Mean true rejections were as follows. At m = 3, k = 1, μ = 1.6%: current 0.83, e-Holm 0.86. At m = 10, k = 1 or 3: all 0 up to μ = 1.6%. At m = 10, k = 10, μ = 1.2%: current 0, e-Holm 6.36. At μ = 1.6%: current 0, e-Holm 10.0. At μ ≤ 0.8% every procedure rejected nothing. At the capped bet, wealth grows almost deterministically (log K ≈ n(μ/2 − E r²/8)), so max K ≈ K_T and Ville's maximum buys little. e-Holm's threshold, 1/α plus the nulls' shortfalls, never exceeds Holm's first hurdle m/α. My prior, that the running maximum would favour the current rule, was wrong. The report's direction is right, but its recommendation 1 is not: the adjuster it gives for the running maximum, A(x) = (x−1)²/(x(1+ln x)), fails ∫₁^∞ A/x² ≤ 1 (the integral diverges; citation table). Stopping is unnecessary on a fixed horizon, since K_T is already an e-value. So the sound variant is e-Holm on K_T. The report's 10-finalists-all-real example (each e = 25) is correct arithmetic. The regime where the gain appears is unreachable at pilot lengths: stage 3's real campaign had zero finalists, and PROGRESS's qualifications say zero promotions is the expected outcome. Outcome: power only, never false discoveries in either direction. A switch would change the frozen holdout test (criteria.md:244–247, calibration v3), so it would need new criteria, fresh campaign ids and a later holdout. Action: make e-Holm on K_T the first candidate for the better-powered test. No change now.

**5. Clipped scores and bet grids: harmful under our null.** Promote clips r/s to [−1, 1] and declares the clipped score's conditional mean to be the estimand (Benhamou et al., HTML). Clipping the lower tail raises the mean. A short-volatility stream with frequent small gains and rare large losses then has a negative mean return and a positive clipped mean. Simulation B: +0.3% with probability 0.996 and −80% with probability 0.004 (mean −0.0212% per step), n = 546, 20,000 series. The frozen test rejected at 0.05 in 0 of 20,000. Promote's statistic (clip(r/0.5%, ±1), 5-point grid mixture) crossed 20 in 100% of series, with median terminal e ≈ 10⁴⁴. This is exactly the Bahadur–Savage shape that calibration v3 was written to survive (criteria.md:241). Adopting recommendation 2(a) or 3 as written would promote a money-losing strategy as a discovery. Only upper-tail clipping is conservative. The grid mixture alone, on raw returns with λ ≤ 1/2, is valid and is a power question. Outcome: false discoveries, if adopted. Action: record in the paper's S3, or wherever the better-powered test is specified, that any bounded score must clip only the upper tail, or must state the score itself as the null, with its relation to net return derived.

**6. Lifetime budget and holdout reuse: already handled.** Holdouts are spent per campaign and never recycled (whitepaper.tex:322). Error control is per campaign, with a summable budget for a project-wide claim (whitepaper.tex:333; S3, line 590), and the two stage-3 arms share a holdout under a stated union bound. Agents never see holdout data (line 315). Coarsening holdout feedback is moot: the holdout is queried once, after the freeze. Outcome: none. Action: none.

**7. Stationary-bootstrap exploit: does not apply to our nulls; worth one line in NULLS.md's rationale.** Liu's exploit works because the bootstrap resamples the return-generating shocks from a finite pool. Our null markets bootstrap only exogenous inputs (funding, premium, event counts), and ε is fresh symmetric noise with funding compensated in price (NULLS.md, No edge and Common pieces). An agent that memorizes funding blocks therefore earns no net edge. SPA's and StepM's bootstrap draws are internal to the gate, and agents never see them. Outcome: none for false discoveries. Action: none. Optionally note in NULLS.md why bootstrapped inputs cannot leak edge.

**8. DSR as a heuristic: agreed, already stated.** whitepaper.tex:354 and S2 already list costs, heavy tails, adaptivity and negative trial correlations as ruling out a conservative reading. The sharperat.io critique adds nothing for us. Action: none.

**9. LLM pre-training look-ahead: handled.** Stage 3's historical holdout is one month past the model's cutoff and is qualified as a pipeline validation run, and the forward window confirms (whitepaper.tex:426, 664). The report's recommendation 5 (cutoff) is already the design. Its other parts, swapping SPA's bootstrap for Giglio–Liao–Xiu and adding CPCV, change selection only. They could change power, but not the holdout's error claim. Noted.

**10. Post-admission e-detector: noted, out of scope.** It could matter for paper trading or a live stage, and it does not affect any judged result. It belongs in `docs/future-work.md` if anywhere.

## What the report missed

- **Its premise.** The design already separates selection from inference, so most search-side "vulnerabilities" cannot change a false-discovery outcome. A useful audit of our gate would attack the conditioning instead: are the finalists truly F_search-measurable? That depends on the freeze (whitepaper.tex:320–321, the kernel's batch check) and on the model cutoff, and the report never examines it.
- **The cap is coupled to the reported floor in code, not to L.** `gate/gate.py:149` caps λ at `0.5 / LOSS_BOUND` with `LOSS_BOUND = 1.0`, matching the frozen formula 1/(2B) (criteria.md:244). The paper derives the cap as 1/L, with L = 2 the pre-absorption bound, a different quantity from B_rep (S3, whitepaper.tex:588). The two coincide only at B = 1. Under O1's provisional B_rep = 0.2 the formula would give λ ≤ 2.5, and a pre-absorption loss of −2 would then make a factor negative. S3 already says O1 owes a new conditional null, so this is noted. Whoever builds O1 must not reuse the formula. Likewise, the docstring at `gate/gate.py:139–141` states validity under reported r_t ≥ −B, the superseded criteria reading. The paper corrected this (S3: "Reported returns' floor of −1 is not a substitute"). The docstring should cite S3 rather than restate the old condition. Neither point touches a judged result.
- **The power gap is the real weakness, and the report gives no number for our setting.** S4a's hurdle (about 1.1% per step at n = 546) dwarfs anything its proposed alternatives recover. Whatever the queued better-powered test chooses, its gain should be measured on planted markets under fresh criteria, not taken from these citations.
