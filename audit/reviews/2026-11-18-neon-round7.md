## 1. Attack on D205 (One Reading a Month, Failure Isolation, and Operational Levers)

### Finding 1.1 — A failed or delayed computation in month $m$ changes month $m+1$'s funding pull range and captured record

- **What it breaks:** D205's central claim that *"no failure after the capture moves the reading"* and that *"a run's timeout... changes no reading and no output"* (`architect-r7-report.md` §§1c, 2a; `4-study-2.md` step 7, wall 3).
- **Construction:** Let `w_start = 2027-02-01 00:00 UTC`. Month 1 publishes horizon $T_1 = \text{2027-03-01 00:00 UTC}$ (day 28). March has 31 days. On `2027-04-01 12:00 UTC` (month 2), the firing pulls `[2027-03-01, 2027-04-01 12:00)` and captures $I_{\text{Apr}}$ (horizon $T_2 = \text{2027-04-01}$, day 59). From `2027-04-01` onward, `2027-03-01` is outside the 30-day window of `mmvault funding`'s daily `00:17 UTC` pull (`2027-04-01` minus 30 days is `2027-03-02`). On `2027-04-02`, the venue serves a late funding row for an unheld coin—or restates a funding rate—for `2027-03-01 01:00 UTC`. No daily pull ever queries `2027-03-01` again.
  - **Without computation failure:** Hosts 1 and 2 compute $I_{\text{Apr}}$ cleanly and publish $T_2 = \text{2027-04-01}$ before May 1. On `2027-05-01 12:00 UTC`, May's firing pulls from *"the last published replay's horizon"* (`4-study-2.md` step 7, wall 3), which is $T_2 = \text{2027-04-01}$. It never queries `2027-03-01`, never writes the late/restated row to `<root>/settled/funding/`, publishes day 89 (judging CS2.3), and runs to day 730.
  - **With computation failure (or timeout delay) on $I_{\text{Apr}}$:** Hosts fail, disagree, or time out past `2027-05-01 12:00 UTC` (e.g., two 7-day timeouts + Host 3 timeout + architect triage, or an April capture delayed to April 25 by a REST outage followed by 7-day run timeouts crossing May 1—no architect delay required). Because *"no firing waits on a run"*, May's `2027-05-01 12:00 UTC` firing sees May has no identity and pulls from the **last published** horizon—which is still $T_1 = \text{2027-03-01}$! That pull fetches `[2027-03-01, 2027-05-01 12:00)`, writes the late/restated `2027-03-01 01:00` row to `<root>/settled/funding/`, and pins it inside $I_{\text{May}}$. When $I_{\text{Apr}}$ eventually resolves and publishes $T_2$, $I_{\text{May}}$ runs next, reads the `2027-03-01 01:00` row, and either rejects via A18 (`R_MARKET`, if restated) or fails the prefix-reproduction check on `2027-03-01` (if a late unheld row adds a `funding` trace row), ending the test at `2027-03-01` (day 28).
  - The same mechanism breaks the claim that the 7-day run timeout is an unpinned operator parameter that *"moves nothing"*: changing the timeout determines whether $I_{\text{Apr}}$ publishes before May 1 and therefore changes May's pull range and captured bytes.
- **Outcome it could change:** Turns CS2.1 from `"Earned"`/`"Outgrew"` into `"Not confirmed"`, CS2.3 from `"met"` into `"not judged"` (28 < 61 days), and CS2.2 from `"held"` into `"not judged"` (28 < 212 days).
- **Classification:** **Design defect.** The spec decouples step 1's capture timing from step 2's publication, yet couples step 1's `--from-ms` to step 2's publication state (`last published horizon`) instead of an invariant bound (e.g., the last captured horizon's cap, or pulling from `w_start` unconditionally).

## 2. Attack on D207 (The Horizon Cap and Pull Range)

### Finding 2.1 — Positive collector-host clock skew (and zero-margin `00:00 UTC` retries) allow a pull to lack a due hour while `T ≤ pull_start`

- **What it breaks:** D207's core proof that *"Every due hour of a horizon `T` settles before `T`, and the pull began at or after `T`, so the pull asked for every one of them after it settled"* (`architect-r7-report.md` §1a; `4-study-2.md` step 7, wall 3), and the claim that *"No event's time comes from it, so the engine still has one clock."*
- **Construction:** In Study 2, `late = 3,599,999 ms` (`4-study-2.md`, venue table). By `KERNEL.md` A18 (*Funding's lateness*), an hour $H$ is **due** for horizon $[w_{\text{start}}, T)$ iff $w_{\text{start}} \le H$ and $H + \text{late} < T$. For any UTC midnight $T$, the hour $H = T - 3{,}600{,}000$ (`23:00:00.000 UTC` of the previous day) has $H + \text{late} = T - 1\text{ ms} < T$, so **`23:00 UTC` is a due hour of $T$**, and any settlement timestamp $t_{\text{settle}} \in [23\text{:00:00.000}, 23\text{:59:59.999}]$ is valid.
  - **Clock-skew construction (zero venue indexing lag):** D207 chains two inequalities across **two different clocks**: $t_{\text{settle}}(\text{venue}) \le H + \text{late} < T \le \text{pull\_start}(\text{host})$. That implication fails whenever the collector host's clock leads the venue's clock by any $\Delta > 0$ (e.g., $\Delta = 5\text{ s}$ from NTP offset/drift, or $\Delta = 15\text{ min}$). Suppose an earlier outage pushes retries to the scheduled `00:00:00.000 UTC` hourly firing on host time:
    1. `mmvault funding` starts at host time `now = T` (`00:00:00.000`), which is **venue time** $T - \Delta$ (`23:59:55.000`, or `23:45:00.000`).
    2. Due hour $H = 23\text{:00}$ settles on the venue at $t_{\text{settle}} \in (T - \Delta, T - 1\text{ ms}]$ (e.g., `23:59:58.000` for $\Delta = 5\text{ s}$, or `23:50:00.000` for $\Delta = 15\text{ min}$—the venue has already stamped 887 s $\approx$ 15 min late on record) and is served with 0 ms indexing lag.
    3. At venue time $T - \Delta < t_{\text{settle}}$, `mmvault funding` requests Coin 1 (`endTime = T`). Because `23:00` has not yet settled on the venue, the venue returns rows only through `22:00`.
    4. Over the ~60 s needed to pull all 17 coins, venue time advances past $T$ to $T - \Delta + 60\text{ s}$, and `mmrec` writes quotes with venue timestamps $\ge T$.
    5. The pull exits 0 (`complete: true` for every coin), captures identity $I$, and both hosts compute `horizon = floor_midnight(min(pull_start = T, last_quote ≥ T)) = T ≤ pull_start`.
    6. Coin 1 is held into `23:00`, so the engine aborts with `R_SETTLEMENT_MISSING` at `23:59:59.999`. (If $\Delta > 1\text{ h}$, the same failure occurs even for an on-time `23:00:00.000` settlement with zero lateness.)
  - **Zero-skew, minimal-indexing-lag corollary:** Even with $\Delta = 0$, the architect's reassurance (*"At the scheduled 12:00 firing the pull begins 13 hours after the last due hour"*) fails at the `00:00:00.000 UTC` hourly retry firing: `pull_start = T` has only **1 ms** of margin past $H + \text{late} = 23\text{:59:59.999}$. A `23:00` settlement stamped at `23:59:59.500` (inside `late`) with just 1 second of REST indexing delay (served at `00:00:00.500`) is missed by Coin 1's query at `00:00:00.100`, even though its serving delay after settlement is 1 second, not an hour.
- **Outcome it could change:** Triggers a false `R_SETTLEMENT_MISSING` abort at `23:59:59.999`, truncating the replay and ending Study 2 early—discarding later CS2.1 `"Earned"`/`"Outgrew"` crossings and potentially leaving CS2.2 or CS2.3 `"not judged"`.
- **Classification:** **Design defect.** Capping the horizon at `floor_midnight(pull_start − M)` with a registered safety margin $M$ (e.g., $M = 1\text{ h}$, so a `00:00` retry caps at $T - 1\text{ d}$ and never races `23:59:59.999`)—plus explicit NTP-sync monitoring—closes both small positive clock skew and zero-margin `00:00` retries.

## 4. Attack on D208 (Cuts in `mmrec cat`, Skipped Files, and Reader Boundaries)

### Finding 4.1 — A flush-boundary cut where `Block₁ + Block₂` exceeds `mmrec cat`'s output buffer can make a local-file host and a streamed-pipe host disagree under `ZSTD_decompressStream`

- **What it breaks:** D208 and `RECORDER.md` test 4 (*"a file cut at any byte yields whole lines only... two readings at one length are byte for byte one"*), and D206's expectation that two hosts reading identical hash-verified bytes `[0..L)` agree.
- **Construction:** Under `architect-r7-report.md` §2c, Host 1 (`prodesk7`) reads `<file>` directly from the local `ws` PVC while Host 2 reads the first $L$ bytes **streamed** over the network from `prodesk7`. Suppose an active zstd stream has two consecutive flushed blocks: `Block₁` (15 KiB compressed $\to$ 40 KiB decompressed) and `Block₂` (15 KiB compressed $\to$ 40 KiB decompressed), and the capture length $L$ cuts at the **exact end of `Block₂`** (a flush boundary). Suppose `mmrec cat`'s C stub uses `IN_SIZE = 64 KiB`, `OUT_SIZE = 64 KiB`, and the textbook `while (in.pos < in.size)` decompression loop without also draining `dctx->outBuff` while `out.pos == out.size` (the exact class of bug that caused `zstd 1.5.7` to drop up to 128 KiB before a cut, `RECORDER.md` line 2744):
  - **Host 1 (local regular file):** A single `read()` returns all 30 KiB (`Block₁ + Block₂`). `ZSTD_decompressStream` decodes `Block₁` (40 KiB) and fills the remaining 24 KiB of `out` with the start of `Block₂` (`out.pos == 64 KiB`), consuming all 30 KiB of input (`in.pos == in.size == 30 KiB`) while leaving the last 16 KiB of `Block₂` buffered inside `dctx->outBuff`. Because `in.pos == in.size` and `remaining == 0`, the loop exits—**silently dropping the last 16 KiB of complete lines from `Block₂`**.
  - **Host 2 (network stream / pipe via `/proc/self/fd/N`):** TCP segmentation delivers `Block₁` and `Block₂` in separate `read()` calls. Read 1 outputs all 40 KiB of `Block₁`; Read 2 starts with an empty `out` buffer and outputs all 40 KiB of `Block₂` (`40 KiB < 64 KiB`)—**emitting the 16 KiB of lines Host 1 dropped**.
  - **Two related cut/stream gaps in `RECORDER.md`:**
    1. If `mmrec cat`'s C stub treats a short `read() < requested` as EOF (untested because Test 4 runs only on local files), Host 2 stops at the first TCP segment at any cut $L > \text{MSS}$.
    2. If capture cuts **inside the 4-byte trailing xxHash32 checksum** right as `mmrec` closes an hour file with `ZSTD_e_end` (all blocks complete with `Last_Block = 1`, 1–3 bytes of checksum present), or at `L = 0` (newly opened hour file before first flush), `RECORDER.md` does not specify whether `mmrec cat` exits 0 with `cut` or exits non-zero as a checksum error; exiting non-zero falsely aborts an honest capture.
  - **Theoretical boundary (what I accept):** Once `mmrec cat` is pinned to one Nix store path (A13), reads strictly $L$ bytes looping over short `read()`s (never `stat()`/`lseek()`), hashes `[0..L)` in the same pass/buffer (preventing TOCTOU on live files), drains `ZSTD_decompressStream` completely (`while (in.pos < in.size || out.pos == out.size)`), and specifies `L = 0` and partial-checksum cuts as `cut` (exit 0), **`ZSTD_decompressStream` is a pure deterministic state machine over the byte sequence `[0..L)`—no cut can then cause two honest x86-64 hosts to differ.**
- **Outcome it could change:** Spurious two-host disagreement on identical hash-verified bytes (blocking publication and triggering Finding 1.1/1.3), silent loss of up to 128 KiB of lines before a flush cut (missing prints/quotes and altering CS2.1/CS2.3), or false run abort on a checksum-boundary cut.
- **Classification:** **Specification/test defect** in `RECORDER.md` (`mmrec cat` interface and Test 4 must mandate stream/pipe chunk-size invariance—testing 1-byte vs. 1 MiB reads on multi-block `> OUT_SIZE` flushes—plus `L = 0` and partial-checksum cuts, and specify how remote hosts pass streamed bytes to `mmrec cat <file> <L>` without TOCTOU).

  1. **Host clock $> 1$ h behind venue clock:** `RECORDER.md` files each frame by host receive hour. If `prodesk7`'s clock lags venue time by $> 1$ hour (e.g., RTC drift after reboot before NTP converges), frames stamped $\ge w_{\text{start}}$ are filed in hour $< w_{\text{start}} - 1\text{ h}$ and silently skipped by D208's reader.
  2. **Freeze-to-midnight `listings` contradiction:** The freeze occurs *before* $w_{\text{start}}$ (*"the first midnight at or after the freeze"*, `criteria.md`), yet `4-study-2.md` step 6 wall 2 requires the registration to hold each coin's rules *"in the listings copy at the first judged midnight"*—which does not exist at commit time. If a coin's `szDecimals` changes (or it delists) between the registration commit (e.g., `2027-01-31 18:00 UTC`) and $w_{\text{start}}$ (`2027-02-01 00:00 UTC`), the first listings copy showing the change has `time < w_start` (e.g., `22:00 UTC`). D208's filter (`w_start ≤ time < w_end`) drops that listing event, and `00:00 UTC` is not *"the first copy that shows it"* (`KERNEL.md` line 2405), so the engine starts at $w_{\text{start}}$ with stale registered rules and rejects Day 1 (`E_DECIMAL`). Additionally, `ledger.md` D208 states generically *"Files before the hour before `w_start` are not read"*, which must explicitly exempt `mmvault` fills (needed back to April 2026 for CS2.3's `chained`) and pre-`w_start` `listings`.
  3. **Placed-decimal equality omitted from D208:** D209 specifies parsed A18 placed-decimal comparison (`"5.0"` == `"5"`) for vault fills, but D208 (`KERNEL.md` line 2404) omits that rule for `fundingHistory` rates, quotes, and trades across pulls/reconnects (where raw string comparison of `"0.0001"` vs. `"0.00010"` would falsely trigger A18 rejection).
- **Outcome it could change:** Drops Day-1 events (CS2.1/CS2.2/CS2.3), falsely rejects Day 1 with `E_DECIMAL` (ending the entire study before a single day is run), or falsely aborts CS2.1 on benign decimal formatting across pulls.
- **Classification:** **Design defect** (2: resolve how rules changing between freeze and $w_{\text{start}}$ initialize the forward market header); **text/specification defect** (1: state the clock-skew assumption for `w_start - 1h` and scope the skip rule to recorder streams only; 3: extend D209's placed-decimal equality to D208).

## 5. Attack on D210 (CS2.1's Drafted Note and Its Proof)

### Core verdict on D210: Can it add a false discovery?

**No—against its stated comparator class, the safety proof holds.** Let $E$ be D210's stopping rule and $A$ be any procedure that agrees with $E$ through $R_{k-1}$ and keeps every published day's end as shown (`criteria.md`: *"what a day's end showed stands in every later replay"*).
- Every `"Outgrew"` named by $E$ was already named by some $R_j$ ($j < k$), so $A$ names `"Outgrew"` too.
- Every `"Earned"` left standing by $E$ occurred on a day $d_1 \le T^* < T_{k-1}$ published by $R_{k-1}$ (with no differing row at or before $d_1$), so $A$ also has $W^b(d_1) \ge 20$ (with ledger alive and no return $< -1$ through $d_1$) and must name either `"Earned"` or `"Outgrew"`—and `"Outgrew"` implies rung 1's rejection since $\bar{B} \ge 1$.
- For CS2.2 and CS2.3, stopping early can only turn an unjudged future failure (`"did not hold"` / `"not shown"`) into `"not judged"` (a property already disclosed in CS2.2, `criteria.md` line 1015), never into `"held"` or `"met"`.

Thus on every world path, $E$'s rejections are a subset of $A$'s, and **D210 adds no false discovery** relative to any committed-text reading that keeps published days as shown.

**Three defects remain in the drafted note and the precision of its claims:**

### Finding 5.1 — The drafted note contradicts the rule's own handling of already-published `"Outgrew"`, CS2.2, and CS2.3

- **What it breaks:** Internal consistency between the drafted CS2.1 note (`architect-r7-report.md` §4, line 188) and both the proof (line 195) and `4-study-2.md` step 7, test 10 (line 1258).
- **Construction:** Month 3 ($R_3$, day 90) judges CS2.3 = `"met"` (days 1–61) and names CS2.1 = `"Outgrew"` on day 80. Because CS2.2 must still run to day 212 (`criteria.md` line 1003; `4-study-2.md` line 1242), Month 4 ($R_4$, day 120) runs and encounters a late row on **day 50**.
  - The drafted note states that $R_4$ *"ends the test at the last midnight before the first row that differs [day 49], **as a record the engine cannot run past ends it**, and publishes nothing."*
  - A record the engine cannot run past on day 50 ends with 49 days run—which cannot hold a day-80 `"Outgrew"` or a day-61 CS2.3 verdict! Yet under `4-study-2.md` line 1258 and the proof (line 195), day 80's `"Outgrew"` and day 61's CS2.3 `"met"` **both stand**, while CS2.2 becomes `"not judged"` (ended at day 49), and had day 80 been `"Earned"` alone it would have been **withdrawn**!
  - Furthermore, the note's sentence *"whatever else a replay did there, keeping every published day as shown, it would name every verdict this end leaves"* is literally false when $E$ ends with `"Earned"` (at $d_1 \le T^*$) and $A$ continues to reach `"Outgrew"` later: $A$ names `"Outgrew"`, not `"Earned"` (`criteria.md`: `"Earned" where rung 1 alone was reached`). What holds is that $A$ confirms every rung $E$ confirms (at least as strongly).
- **Outcome it could change:** An implementer following the note literally would either withdraw a final `"Outgrew"`/CS2.2/CS2.3 verdict or fail to withdraw `"Earned"` after $T^*$; and the final report would state an unexplained contradiction (*"Outgrew at day 80, CS2.3 met on days 1–61, test ended at day 49"*).
- **Classification:** **Text defect** in the drafted CS2.1 note (state explicitly that previously named final `"Outgrew"` and once-judged CS2.2/CS2.3 verdicts stand, while non-final `"Earned"` after that midnight is withdrawn; and replace *"would name every verdict this end leaves"* with *"would confirm every rung this end leaves confirmed"*).

### Finding 5.2 — Midnight boundary ambiguity (`before` vs. `at or before`) on a late quote in `(23:59:59.000, 23:59:59.999]`

- **What it breaks:** The drafted note's stopping boundary (`architect-r7-report.md` line 188) and `4-study-2.md` step 7, wall 3 (line 1315).
- **Construction:** On day $D$ (midnight end $T$), a late quote arrives at `23:59:59.995`—after the last 1-second tick (`23:59:59.000`)—pulling the bid from 100 to 1 (dropping $W^b$ from `20.5` to `0.2`), with no print, fill, or liquidation. Because quotes write **no trace row of their own** (`KERNEL.md` A19), the **first row that differs** in the entire report and trace is day $D$'s `coin` row, whose timestamp is **`time = T`** (midnight itself).
  - Both the note and `4-study-2.md` line 1315 equate this stop with *"as a record the engine cannot run past ends it (CS2.1)"*, where CS2.1's formula is *"the last midnight **at or before** that time"* (`criteria.md` line 960).
  - If implemented as `floor_midnight(time)` (`≤ T`), the test ends at $T$—**keeping day $D$** and its false `"Earned"` even though day $D$'s own `coin` row differed!
- **Outcome it could change:** Fails to withdraw a compromised `"Earned"` on day $D$ when the changed record first appears in day $D$'s midnight `coin`/`account` rows.
- **Classification:** **Text/specification defect** (define the end unambiguously as the last published midnight whose day's-end rows and all prior rows match byte-for-byte—i.e., strictly before `T` when the first differing row is a `day`/`account` row at `T`).

### Note on the 5% bound scope (caveat, not a defect of D210)
The proof validly establishes $\text{Rejections}(E) \subseteq \text{Rejections}(A)$ for every committed-text procedure $A$ that keeps published days as shown; it does **not** establish Ville's 5% bound against the *complete/corrected* record if missing/late events are correlated with adverse price moves (since a final `"Outgrew"` published on an incomplete $R_{k-1}$ survives even after $R_k$ reveals the missing crash tick). Because that gap is already present in the committed text's irreversible `"Outgrew"` rule rather than introduced by D210, D210 itself adds no false discovery, and the proof's wording (*"the 5% bound of any such reading holds for E"*) is accurate.

## 6. Sizing: Reading Only Used Channels/Coins and Hash-Pinned Per-Coin Extracts

### Finding 6.1 — Reading the full 1.2 TB recording across 24 monthly two-host replays threatens B5's 24-hour wall (O22), capture atomicity, and `prodesk7`'s live recorder

- **What it breaks:** B5's 1-day replay wall (`kernel-v2.md` B5; O22), D205's capture atomicity (Finding 1.2), and `4-study-2.md` step 7's unspecified channel scope.
- **Construction:** From `RECORDER.md` (*Storage budget*), one quiet hour is 73 MB compressed = 1.27 GB uncompressed (**17.4× expansion**). At month 24, the architect's 1.2 TB compressed recording (`architect-r7-report.md` §2c) decompresses to **~20.9 TB of JSONL**—covering ~350 perps across `l2Book-fast`, 179 main-dex 20-level `l2Book`, `allDexsAssetCtxs`, `trades`, and `events`.
  - On one core, decompressing ~20.9 TB of zstd (~1–1.5 GB/s $\implies$ 4–5.5 h) plus scanning/JSON-parsing ~20.9 TB just to discard ~333 unused coins and unused channels (~200–500 MB/s $\implies$ 11–28 h) takes **15–33 hours before the Lean engine processes a single event**, blowing B5's 24-hour wall.
  - Meanwhile, Study 2's 1.95 billion 5-level quotes (`kernel-v2.md` B5) at ~450 B/line are only **~0.9 TB uncompressed (~50 GB compressed)**—roughly **20× smaller** than the whole recording.
  - Across 24 monthly replays $\times$ 2 hosts (sum of linearly growing horizons $\approx 12.5 \times$ month-24 size), re-streaming the full recording reads **~30 TB** off `prodesk7`'s single unmirrored live-recorder disk (plus ~15 TB of capture-time SHA-256 reads).
  - Additionally, `4-study-2.md` never specifies which recorder channels are in the forward identity (`l2Book-fast` vs. `l2Book`, excluding `allDexsAssetCtxs` and `events`), nor whether D207's *"last quote the recorder's files hold"* ranges over all ~350 perps or only the 17 universe coins (if the 17 coins' connection drops just before midnight while coin #200 receives a quote past midnight, the two definitions give different horizons!).
- **Outcome it could change:** Blows the 24-hour replay wall (hitting the 7-day timeout or starving `prodesk7`'s live `mmrec` recorder, risking **lost recordings**); causes capture hashing to overrun the hourly timer (Finding 1.2); or shifts horizon $T$ by a day if `last_quote` scope is ambiguous.
- **Classification:** **Design defect** (sizing and channel/coin reading architecture) and **text defect** (explicitly specify the channels in the forward identity and scope `last_quote` to the 17 universe coins).

### Direct answers to the three sizing questions

#### (a) Should a replay read only the channels and coins it uses?

- **Channels: Yes, unconditionally.** `mmrec` already separates channels into distinct directory trees (`RECORDER.md`). `allDexsAssetCtxs` (forbidden by V8), `events` (operational logs/acks/pongs), and `l2Book` (20-level books, unused by B5's 5-level forward run) should be excluded from both the forward identity and replay reads immediately. This requires zero extraction, introduces zero new trust assumptions, and preserves D205–D208 verbatim.
- **Coins: Yes, for closed hourly files—via a two-host-verified extract, not by modifying `mmrec` or blindly trusting a single-host extract.** Do **not** embed Study 2's 17-coin list into `mmrec` (which must remain a simple, universe-agnostic recorder where bugs cause irreplaceable lost recordings, and splitting ~350 coins into per-coin files multiplies open FDs and hurts zstd compression). Reading only the 17 coins from closed hours cuts Month-24 replay I/O and JSON parsing by ~10–20×.

#### (b) Could it read a per-coin extract made once and pinned by hashes to the original files, without weakening what D205 to D208 guarantee?

- **Made once on ONE host and trusted via hash pinning: No—that weakens D206 and A13.**
  - *Breaks D206:* A single-host transient fault (bit-flip, dropped print, corrupted line) during extraction is permanently baked into the extract and then fed identically to both replay hosts, turning a single-host fault into a common-mode failure that passes two-host byte-for-byte comparison.
  - *Breaks A13:* Anything that filters raw bytes between the record and the engine sits inside the verdict path (`4-study-2.md` step 6 wall 1); if the extractor is outside the registered Nix derivation, post-freeze changes can silently drop events without changing the build hash.
  - *Breaks D205/D208 on active files:* Open/unclosed files at capture time are still growing and must still be read raw to length $L$ via `mmrec cat`.
- **Made once per closed raw file by a frozen extractor inside the registered derivation (A13) and verified byte-for-byte by TWO distinct hosts (matching D206's exact trust model), with any open files at capture still read raw to $L$: Yes—this preserves D205–D208 with no weakening.**
  - Each closed raw file is streamed and extracted **once per host (twice total across the entire two-year study)** during the month it is recorded, rather than 48 times across 24 monthly two-host replays—reducing cumulative raw streaming from $O(M^2)$ to $O(M)$ while maintaining D206's exact two-host trust boundary (no single host's fault can corrupt an extract!).
  - Open files at capture time (at most 1–2 files per channel, ~150 MB) are never extracted; both hosts read them raw to length $L$ via `mmrec cat`.

#### (c) What would such an extract have to prove, and what could go wrong?

**Five invariants the extract must establish** (for raw file $F$ of length $L$ and universe $U$):
1. **Source & prefix immutability:** Derived from exact bytes `F[0..L)` matching `sha256(F[0..L))` via the frozen `mmrec cat` store path, and $F$ is provably closed/immutable (not merely older than the wall-clock hour).
2. **Soundness (no fabricated/mutated bytes):** Every line in the extract appears verbatim (byte-for-byte, zero JSON re-serialization) in `mmrec cat F L`.
3. **Completeness (no omitted universe or corrupt lines):** Every line in `mmrec cat F L` belonging to any coin in $U$—**and any malformed/unparseable line**—is present in the extract. *Crucial theoretical point:* pinning `(sha256(raw), sha256(extract))` in a manifest **cannot cryptographically prove completeness** without reading the raw file (a faulty extractor can omit a crash print and still record the true raw hash). Completeness is established only by running the frozen extractor independently on **two distinct hosts** and requiring byte-for-byte agreement on `(extract, cut_flag, total_lines, skipped_non_universe_lines)`.
4. **Order, multiplicity, and count preservation:** Extracted **1-to-1 per raw file** (never merging two runs' files of an hour), preserving exact relative line order (required by A18's source-position tie-breaker for same-millisecond events) and exact per-file cut/skipped counts, with **no deduplication and no horizon filtering** performed during extraction.
5. **Protected-window compliance (A13):** The extractor enforces the compiled protected-windows table and refuses any file/event in an unreleased window.

**Concrete failure modes (what could go wrong and what outcome each changes):**

| Failure mode | Mechanism | Outcome changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-host extraction fault** | Bit-flip/bug on one extractor drops a crash print or alters a quote; both replay hosts read the same extract and agree | False irreversible CS2.1 `"Outgrew"` or wrong CS2.2/CS2.3 verdict (breaks D206) |
| **Extracting an unclosed file** | `RECORDER.md` line 2846 closes hour `HH` only *"at the first frame of a new hour"*, not at wall-clock `HH+1:00`. Extracting after wall-clock `HH+1:00` during a websocket disconnect permanently freezes an incomplete prefix of `HH` | Silently drops quotes/prints (breaks D205/D208); alters CS2.1/CS2.2/CS2.3 |
| **Silent dropping of malformed/schema-drifted lines** | Extractor drops any line where `"coin"` fails to parse (assuming it is not in $U$), hiding a corrupt stream or venue schema change | Turns a mandatory `E_PARSE`/`R_MARKET` rejection into silent staleness, letting an invalid replay confirm |
| **Holdout leak before `2027-01-31`** | Extracting `[2026-11-01, 2027-01-31)` before release exposes per-coin file sizes and line counts (trading/quote activity and volatility of the 17 coins) prior to registration | **Leak** violating `CLAUDE.md` Holdout and A13 |
| **PVC exhaustion / deleting raw files** | Storing extracts on `prodesk7`'s already-constrained 300 GiB `ws` PVC (filling ~2027-05-20) accelerates disk exhaustion, or deleting raw files destroys auditability | **Lost recording** (ends Study 2 early) |
| **Extractor outside frozen derivation** | Post-freeze edits to extraction code do not change A13's build hash | Unaudited changes to inputs reaching verdicts (breaks A13) |
| **Raw-vs-extract `last_quote` mismatch** | If `last_quote` is defined over all ~350 coins rather than the 17 universe coins, a connection drop on the 17 coins just before midnight changes horizon $T$ | Shifts horizon $T$ by a day (breaks D207) |

## What I Accept

- **D205's core reversal of D200 ("one reading a month"):** Treating every post-capture computation failure (crash, kill, OOM, timeout, `E_FPENV`, hash mismatch, wrong output) uniformly on the same captured identity genuinely removes Argon's 1.2.1 seam and closes the post-capture kill-to-move-reading lever. Replacing GPT's round-5 killed-alike comparator with *"replayed once a month, computed right"* is the right design foundation once Findings 1.1–1.3 are closed.
- **D206's expansion of comparison scope and identity:** Moving from `"rows"` to byte-for-byte comparison of the full computed output (including `finish`'s summary, truncated rerun, `confirm`, `guard`, `held`, CS2.3 replay/matching, layer 2, counts, and report text) and requiring $\ge 3$ distinct x86-64 hosts closes Argon's 1.1.1–1.1.3 once Findings 3.1–3.2 are brought inside the boundary.
- **D207's deletion of the same-day rule and fixed pull timeout:** Capping the horizon at the pull's start (from hashed `start` bytes) rather than relying on *"completed on the UTC day it began"* eliminates Argon's 3.1.1 midnight-crossing race; adding a fixed 30-minute pull timeout closes hung-pull blocking (subject to adding a safety margin $M$ for clock skew/`00:00` retries and addressing Finding 2.2).
- **D208's message-only event rule and horizon filtering:** Computing events solely from message payloads (`known = time + lag` for funding, `time + δ` for quotes/trades) and filtering to `[w_start, w_end)` before `feed` closes Argon's 3.2.1–3.2.2; pinning `mmrec cat <file> <L>` by store path makes `mmrec cat` theoretically cut-deterministic once pipe/drain/edge-cut tests are enforced (Finding 4.1).
- **D209 (vault fill identity):** Keying fills by `(coin, tid, oid)` on parsed A18 placed-decimal read fields (ignoring envelopes, key order, and unread fields) and aligning `mmvault`'s counter to the same key cleanly closes Argon's 4.1. I find no defect in D209 itself.
- **D210's core statistical safety:** Against any committed-text procedure that keeps published days as shown, ending on a changed published prefix cannot add a false discovery for CS2.1, CS2.2, or CS2.3. The remaining issues (Findings 5.1–5.2) are wording and boundary precision, not statistical invalidity.
