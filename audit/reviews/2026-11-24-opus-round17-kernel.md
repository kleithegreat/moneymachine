## Findings

### 1. The laws: R1 does not order a millisecond, so a rule change can cancel the order its millisecond's arrival placed (design; built in part)

**What it breaks.** R1's claim to be the law of when rule changes happen, and that the whole machine's effects are placed by the inputs. R1 binds a `.rules` step to its listing's `(time, coin)` (`ruleChanges`), and an arrival's books to `marketAt` and to the machine's own effect prefix. No clause orders a step against an arrival, a step against the tick, or steps against the moves, inside one millisecond; the only clause naming both arrivals and steps is the one-owner cover. `KERNEL.md` A18, "The order of a millisecond", pins that order: the fed events in A3's order (listing class 1, funding 2, prints and quotes 3), then the tick, then the arrivals.

**Construction.** One coin, tick 1 s, `λ` of 1,000 ms (or 0), `δ` above 0, one active `alo` slot. A listing at 2,000 changes the coin's rules (any field: `ruleChanges` compares whole `Rules`); the wish sent at 1,000 arrives at 2,000. A machine that defers a listing's cancels to the end of its millisecond runs the arrival first: it cancels the slot's order (`replaced`) and places one on the new grid, since `marketAt … 2000` already holds the listing; then the `.rules` step cancels the order just placed. Every clause of the restated R1 holds: the arrival's books are `bookFrom` of `marketAt` and of the prefix, its slice is `arrivalEffectsOf`, `admissions` its `filterMap`; the step's slice is `stepForm (.rules c)` of the account it began at, and `(2000, c)` is `ruleChanges`; one owner; L1c's split and monotone times; L0; S1 (`.rules` ends nothing); L1a and C1 for a deterministic machine that buffers by millisecond. The honest run holds the new order from 2,000, this one none until the next arrival (`order/Order.lean`, on `b94e128`'s `Acct.apply`: both logs meet the `.rules` form at the account their step began at, and end holding `[oNew]` and `[]`; the arrival's clauses, which read only its own prefix, checked by hand). The same freedom lets a print stamped in the listing's millisecond fill the order on the old grid before its cancel, and a fill at a quote stamped `HH:00:00.000` precede that hour's `.funding` step, whose form reads the position at the step's start. U1's text names the right position ("before any fill at that time"), so step 7 could recompute it from the log; F0's says nothing of a millisecond's order.

**Outcome.** An order lost, or a fill or funding payment on the wrong state: CS2.1 to CS2.3, at those coincidences (the funding one recurs: settlements are stamped on the hour, and so are some prints and snapshots).

**Fix (design, small).** `CycleStep` names its event's index in `moves` (`ruleChanges` returning that index, so the field is the inputs', not the machine's word), and R1 requires the records of a run in A18's order: steps in their events' order, and in a millisecond its steps, then its tick, then its arrivals (`s.time ≤ a.time → s.effectEnd ≤ a.effectStart`, `s.time ≤ r.time → s.effectEnd ≤ r.effectCount`, `r.time ≤ a.time → r.effectCount ≤ a.effectStart`). Then F0, U1, M1 and M2 say which events have steps, and the order within one event (A7's for fills, then the check) is F0's and M1's. The tick clause also closes a smaller case: at `δ = 0` nothing now stops an arrival's placement being emitted before the tick that sent its wish, so that tick's view would hold it.

### 2. The boundary: Stage 2's names do not make the compiler's names unique; only the once-check does (text; built)

**What it breaks.** B2's derivation that the compiler makes names "only by extending a declaration's name with components of its own", and the addendum's "each guard closes every impostor built so far by itself". The specializer names a copy `f._at_.g.spec_k` (`Specialize.lean:315`, `appendCore`): `g`'s components are the caller's, and the library chooses them. Any library function with an instance argument is specialized (`SpecInfo.lean`, `.fixedInst`), so `f` can be the library's too.

**Construction, built** (`coll/`, tagged; `coll-untagged/`): `A [Add α]` and `«A».«#».«_at_».B [Mul α]`, called by `«B».«#».«_at_».C` and `C`, with exports proved 10 and 32. Tagged, both copies are named `A.«#»._at_.B.«#»._at_.C.«#».spec_0`; untagged (`A._at_.B`, `B._at_.C`), both `A._at_.B._at_.C.spec_0`. No library name meets a compiler name, the reach walk finds no opaque, no panic is raised, and the per-call check passes (neither copy is an environment constant). The prototype stops only at the once-check: `recorded twice: A.«#»._at_.B.«#»._at_.C.«#».spec_0._boxed` (each copy and its `_boxed` recorded by each caller's call).

**Outcome.** None: it fails closed. But the once-check is load-bearing, not a second guard beside the names, and must not go in a later cut.

**Fix (text).** B2 and the ledger say what each guard closes: the names close a library name taken for a compiler name, or looked up as one; the once-check closes the compiler's own names colliding, which library names can induce. Refusing a `#` component in a library name would make the tags parse uniquely and close it a second way; I see no need. The per-call check failed nothing in any construction (theirs or mine) that the once-check did not.

### 3. The boundary's stated trust: `Float` is not opaque to the kernel on 4.34.0 (text; built probes)

`kernel-v2.md` B2 ("What finite tests and review alone hold") says "`Float` is opaque to the kernel, so no law can say more of these than that they are `Float`'s". On 4.34.0 `Float.add`, `.toBits`, `.ofBits` and the rest are `@[extern]` definitions over `Float.Model`, IEEE arithmetic with NaNs canonical, which the kernel evaluates: `decide` proves `((0.0 : Float) / 0.0).toBits = 0x7FF8000000000000` and canonicalizes a signalling NaN's bits (`nan/Probe.lean`). I looked for a value kernel and binary give differently, a channel past every check: the runtime's `lean_float_to_bits` canonicalizes too (`0x7FF8000000000000` for `0/0`, whose x86 result is `0xFFF8…`, and for `0x7FF0000000000001`), big `Float.ofNat` rounds to nearest in both, and strings at a non-boundary position agree (`nan/Probe2.lean`, `Probe3.lean`, `RunNaN.lean`). So no outcome, but the residual is narrower than written and checkable: that each C operation equals its model. Likewise the walk's reason ("every other constant reached has a kernel definition, and that the toolchain compiles it to its value") should say that Init's 491 safe `@[extern]` or `@[implemented_by]` definitions are not compiled from their values; 11 have a stateful type, and each is value-faithful or reaches an `ST` opaque through its value (`ST.Prim.Ref.modify`), so restricting the list to opaques is right on 4.34.0 (`nan/ListExterns.lean`).
