## The revision states

1. **The schedule.** Information cutoff D + 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST the day before). Acquisition starts at the cutoff. The completion deadline and earliest session start are D + 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST). This is a choice from the recorded 2026-10-28 inventory, not a bound. Recorded behaviour (the unclosed candle returned in 514 of 524 scopes) costs 630 requests, 17,206 weight and 155 zero-latency packing minutes in both reviewed orders. The stress case, venue omits the unclosed candle in all 524 scopes, costs 1,144 requests and 28,000 weight: 252 minutes in the reviewed code order, 257 in the sorted histogram test. The latter bounds these two packing exercises only, not arbitrary orders or inventories. One explicit stress budget uses the tested 257 minutes + 1 minute of prewarm debt + four 8-minute mark allowances + 2 minutes for foreground resolutions + 6 minutes of latency = 298 minutes after the cutoff, projecting 05:03 UTC (00:03 EST), already past the next mark-prep cutoff at 04:59:05 UTC (23:59:05 EST) and the deadline. It therefore **does not fit**; the miss rule handles it. The same allowances with two mark pauses give recorded-behaviour completion at 03:05 UTC (22:05 EST), 1 h 55 min of margin. T3 explains the allowances and the code-order sensitivity. They are not bounds on retries, HTTP duration or unmeasured holes; no guarantee that the stress case finishes before the start remains.
2. **Misses and integrity.** A late or missing attested snapshot makes main and no-reading missed for the date, with that reason. There is no replacement from a shifted cutoff or another date. Only exact bytes already attested may be reused; an unattested file is reacquired, never certified merely because it exists. Changed or lost attested bytes fail closed. Non-Monday acquisition stops at 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST), releasing its hold and limiter wait; Monday acquisition may continue until 24:00 UTC (19:00 EST) for the benchmark only. A snapshot is on time only if attested strictly before 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST); the miss decision waits for an attestation in flight. Each date's `taken`, `acquired`, `listed_at`, `readiness_at` and request count are recorded in its attestation and listed in the C10.6 report. Changing the start after seeing misses is a new identity. The tolerated misses restate C10.5 (T3): at most 28 - 20/r main sessions, r the mean accepted theses per on-time main session. Exactly four Mondays and four executed benchmark batches are still required; a batch skipped for want of a snapshot does not count as executed.
3. **Acquisition is independent of the sessions and of model readiness.** It has its own loop. Sessions may wait for the model and the sandbox canary until the UTC date ends, and are then disclosed as missed. A crash consumes the started session; SIGTERM leaves unfinished work to the next start without backdating it.
4. **Day-fixed readiness and arm inputs.** At snapshot completion (`readiness_at`), save each listing's readiness: whether the funding scope has been acquired to within one page (500 hours) of that time. The attested snapshot's map is authoritative for the whole date, including after restart. Neither an arm's later clock nor another arm's fetch can change it. A false entry raises the failed-read disclosure and commits nothing. Registration fetches subsequent funding tails from `readiness_at` minus 500 hours, not a moving wall-clock lower bound that could leave an unacquired hour behind; existing positions still read from their earliest registration. Main, conditional ablations and no-reading share the identical attested daily snapshot and readiness. Their registration-time hourly bars, funding, equity and commit listings **differ by design**, as do their actual entry times; the identical-snapshot claim is not a claim of identical registration-time account inputs. Foreground limiter callers are FIFO (marks retain their sampling priority), so snapshot requests cannot repeatedly steal a due resolution's quota. Eligibility is unchanged.
5. **APPROVED Monday benchmark revision.** The benchmark reads the day's attested snapshot, not a second acquisition, with daily/hourly bars, funding and listing status fixed as of Monday 00:00 UTC (Sunday 19:00 EST). Listing status is the latest completed discovery observed by that instant; absent historical status stays missing, never backdated from a later live read. Its effective forecast time `t_r` is Monday midnight, its horizon is Monday + 21 days, and its reference holding window and base-rate window therefore never shorten when registration is delayed. The actual receipt time is separately recorded as `registered_at`. This is a deterministic paper reference, not a delayed executable trade at a historical price. It registers at the first whole hour at or after max(05:00 UTC (00:00 EST), attestation), except an attestation in [23:00, 24:00) UTC ([18:00, 19:00) EST) registers at attestation. Monday acquisition may continue after the agents' deadline for this purpose. A snapshot missing for the whole date leaves each market skipped "no attested snapshot", not an executed batch. These are approved changes for the new identity/window, never passes of the old midnight-registration rule.
6. **Listing observation times.** `listed_at` records the snapshot's current discovery. Acquisition may begin after its 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST) cutoff; listing metadata is explicitly labelled by its actual observation time. Agent registration re-reads listings at commit; the benchmark instead uses the archived midnight observation described above.
7. **What it changes.** `markets` shows the last 1h close as of 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST); main's entry is near 06:00 UTC (01:00 EST), against about 01:00 UTC (20:00 EST) in the original 00:10 UTC (19:10 EST) design. Daily bars and agent base-rate anchors are unchanged; snapshot age at start is 4 h 55 min on time and strictly under 23 h 55 min. The one-hour, 100-turn, two-registration limits, conditional ablations and no-reading arm are unchanged. This is a new identity and window, never a pass of the old schedule.

8. **APPROVED dex mark send-time revision.** At 120 weight per minute, discovery plus a cohort holds at most five dexes: the first five in lexical order at H + 5 s, later cohorts of five as the rolling minute admits them (about H + 65 s for dexes six to ten), every send time recorded. Cohort pagination consumes neither an error attempt nor error backoff; each failed dex has up to six attempts, subject to the sampling window. Give-up leaves the full five-minute window minus one 10 s timeout, not minus a further minute. Only samples both sent and received strictly before H + MARK_WINDOW may be sealed into H; either timestamp exactly on the seal is excluded. Timeout phases and quota waits do not guarantee timely receipt; late attempts remain archived. The old every-dex-at-H+5-s rule remains a known limitation of the running version, never a pass; the human approved this revision only for the new identity and window.
