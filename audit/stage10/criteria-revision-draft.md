## The revision states

1. **The schedule.** Information cutoff D + 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST the day before). Acquisition starts at the cutoff. The completion deadline and earliest session start are D + 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST). This is a choice from the recorded 2026-10-28 inventory, not a bound. A daily fetch never asks past the last row it can show. Recorded behaviour (the venue returning the unclosed hourly candle) costs 620 requests, 16,996 weight and 153 zero-latency packing minutes. The stress case, venue omits the unclosed candle in all 176 hourly scopes, costs 796 requests and 20,692 weight: 188 packing minutes, bounding that exercise only, not arbitrary orders or inventories. One explicit stress budget adds 1 minute of prewarm debt, 2 for foreground resolutions and 6 of latency, and an 8-minute mark allowance for each hourly preparation the work is still running at (01:00, 02:00 and 03:00 UTC (20:00, 21:00, 22:00 EST)): 221 minutes after the cutoff, completing at 03:46 UTC (22:46 EST), 1 h 14 min before the start and before the 04:59:05 UTC (23:59:05 EST) preparation. The recorded behaviour completes at 03:11 UTC (22:11 EST), 1 h 49 min before the start. T3 explains the allowances. They are not bounds on retries, HTTP duration or unmeasured holes; no guarantee of completion before the start remains.
2. **Misses and integrity.** A late or missing attested snapshot makes main and no-reading missed for the date, with that reason. There is no replacement from a shifted cutoff or another date. Only exact bytes already attested may be reused; an unattested file is reacquired, never certified merely because it exists. Changed or lost attested bytes fail closed. Non-Monday acquisition stops at 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST), releasing its hold and limiter wait; Monday acquisition may continue until 24:00 UTC (19:00 EST) for the benchmark only. A snapshot is on time only if attested strictly before 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST); the miss decision waits for an attestation in flight. Each date's `taken`, `acquired`, `listed_at`, `readiness_at` and request count are recorded in its attestation and listed in the C10.6 report. Changing the start after seeing misses is a new identity. The tolerated misses restate C10.5 (T3): at most 28 - 20/r main sessions (13 at r = 1.4), r the mean accepted theses per on-time main session. Exactly four Mondays and four executed benchmark batches are still required; a market skipped for a property of the market at Monday midnight (not listed, delisted, too few daily bars or no hourly close, no daily close) counts as benchmarked, but one skipped for an operational reason ("no attested snapshot", "no listing observation by Monday 00:00 UTC (Sunday 19:00 EST)", "funding not ready at the snapshot", "run missed") does not. A batch with any operational skip is not executed (a tightening of C10.5's counting only).
3. **Acquisition is independent of the sessions and of model readiness.** It has its own loop. Sessions may wait for the model and the sandbox canary until the UTC date ends, and are then disclosed as missed. A crash consumes the started session; SIGTERM leaves unfinished work to the next start without backdating it.
4. **Day-fixed readiness and arm inputs.** At snapshot completion (`readiness_at`), save each listing's readiness: whether the funding scope has been acquired to within one page (500 hours) of that time. The attested snapshot's map is authoritative for the whole date, including after restart. Neither an arm's later clock nor another arm's fetch can change it. A false entry raises the failed-read disclosure and commits nothing. Registration fetches subsequent funding tails from `readiness_at` minus 500 hours, not a moving wall-clock lower bound that could leave an unacquired hour behind; existing positions still read from their earliest registration. Main, conditional ablations and no-reading share the identical attested daily snapshot and readiness. Their registration-time hourly bars, funding, equity and commit listings **differ by design**, as do their actual entry times; the identical-snapshot claim is not a claim of identical registration-time account inputs. Foreground limiter callers are FIFO (marks retain their sampling priority), so snapshot requests cannot repeatedly steal a due resolution's quota. Eligibility is unchanged.
5. **APPROVED Monday benchmark revision.** The benchmark reads the day's attested snapshot, not a second acquisition, with daily/hourly bars, funding and listing status fixed as of Monday 00:00 UTC (Sunday 19:00 EST). Listing status is the latest completed discovery observed by that instant; absent historical status stays missing, never backdated from a later live read. Its effective forecast time `t_r` is Monday midnight, its horizon is Monday + 21 days, and its reference holding window and base-rate window therefore never shorten when registration is delayed. The actual receipt time is separately recorded as `registered_at`. This is a deterministic paper reference, not a delayed executable trade at a historical price. It registers at the first pass that finds the snapshot attested, with no rounding and at any time of day or after (the inputs are fixed at Monday midnight), so an attestation in the day's last minutes still registers; "no attested snapshot" is decided at or after the date's end, under the attestation lock, waiting for an attestation in flight; a batch still failing transiently at Wednesday 00:00 UTC (Tuesday 19:00 EST), two days after Monday midnight, is logged "run missed"; each thesis records the observation time of the listings it used (`listings_read`). A market not ready at the snapshot is skipped "funding not ready at the snapshot"; a Monday with no listing observation before its midnight is skipped "no listing observation by Monday 00:00 UTC (Sunday 19:00 EST)", and `freeze` refuses while the window's first Monday midnight has none before it (run `thesis-prewarm` on the new code first). The Monday snapshot widens its scope only by benchmark markets listed at midnight. Monday acquisition may continue after the agents' deadline for this purpose. A snapshot missing for the whole date leaves each market skipped "no attested snapshot". These are approved changes for the new identity/window, never passes of the old midnight-registration rule.
6. **Listing observation times.** `listed_at` records the snapshot's current discovery. Acquisition may begin after its 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST) cutoff; listing metadata is explicitly labelled by its actual observation time. Agent registration re-reads listings at commit; the benchmark instead uses the archived midnight observation described above.
7. **What it changes.** `markets` shows the last 1h close as of 00:05 UTC (19:05 EST); main's entry is near 06:00 UTC (01:00 EST), against about 01:00 UTC (20:00 EST) in the original 00:10 UTC (19:10 EST) design. Daily bars and agent base-rate anchors are unchanged; snapshot age at start is 4 h 55 min on time and strictly under 23 h 55 min. The one-hour, 100-turn, two-registration limits, conditional ablations and no-reading arm are unchanged. This is a new identity and window, never a pass of the old schedule.

8. **APPROVED dex mark send-time revision.** At 120 weight per minute, discovery plus a cohort holds at most five dexes: the first five in lexical order at H + 5 s, later cohorts of five as the rolling minute admits them (about H + 65 s for dexes six to ten), every send time recorded. Cohort pagination consumes neither an error attempt nor error backoff; each failed dex has up to six attempts, subject to the sampling window. Give-up leaves the full five-minute window minus one 10 s timeout, not minus a further minute. Only samples both sent and received strictly before H + MARK_WINDOW may be sealed into H; either timestamp exactly on the seal is excluded. Timeout phases and quota waits do not guarantee timely receipt; late attempts remain archived. The old every-dex-at-H+5-s rule remains a known limitation of the running version, never a pass; the human approved this revision only for the new identity and window.
