## Exact proposed wording

For (a), a failed read means an exception. A completed readiness check returning false is ordinary day-fixed cold status, not a retryable read error.

**(a) Item 2, operational skips, and item 4, readiness.** The operational reasons also include "market read failed at the snapshot". Each builder-dex listing, market read and readiness check is attempted once whenever its pass comes, subject only to the acquisition deadlines: strictly before D + 05:00 UTC (00:00 EST) for the agents, and on a Monday until D + 24:00 UTC (19:00 EST) for its benchmark only. A delisted or unlisted market needs neither. A failure of the core listing or of the builder-dex list that lasts to that deadline leaves the date with no attested snapshot. Later passes retry only what failed: market reads, readiness checks and builder-dex listings. No retry starts, and no network admission within a retry is made, at or after D + 04:45 UTC (23:45 EST the day before). The cutoff is checked within the pass and while waiting for quota. An in-flight request may finish, and its market's readiness is then checked. Before the cutoff, an outstanding failure prevents attestation. A failure is final for the snapshot if it remains at the cutoff, if it is first observed at or after it, or if its retry was left incomplete there. Final failures and their reasons are recorded in the attested snapshot and in its attestation record, and listed in C10.6. They make only the affected market, or every market of the failing builder dex, unavailable for that date: absent from the agent and benchmark market views and daily bars, and not ready. Main and no-reading still run on the identical attested snapshot, failures included. The Monday batch skips such a market operationally ("market read failed at the snapshot: <reason>"), so that batch is not executed. One `readiness_at`, fixed when the draft is created and saved with it after every pass, so a restart resumes the draft, is used by every readiness check of that draft. Registration of a newly used market reads funding from this `readiness_at` minus 500 hours through its own cutoff, and existing positions read from their earliest registration. A completed readiness check returning false is day-fixed cold status, not a read failure.

**(b) C10.2, replacing its last bullet ("Per session, every transcript client call maps to exactly one daemon call record. The transcript has no tool call outside Read, Write and `./thesis-agent`").** For the new track identity and window only, by the human's decision of 2026-11-09 17:01 UTC (12:01 EST) (`audit/decisions.md`: C10.2 re-derived on daemon-before-reply recording and the isolation canary, option B). It replaces the frozen transcript-to-record mapping; it is never a pass of the frozen bullet, and no old session is rejudged under it.

*Claim.* Every disclosure the agent received in a session is recorded in the ledger.

*Guarantee 1, record before reply.* The daemon commits each request's record (the request, the response hash, the response bytes it stores) to the ledger before any reply byte is written, so a process killed between the two leaves a record and no reply, never a reply without a record. Evidence: `thesis/tests/test_record_before_reply.py` (a real daemon on a real socket, SIGKILLed after its handler returns and before the reply: the asking client reads nothing, and the ledger holds the request and a stored response matching its hash) and `thesis/tests/test_daemon.py::test_no_bytes_are_released_if_the_record_cannot_be_written` (a failing ledger write releases nothing).

*Guarantee 2, the client is the agent's only channel to protected data.* The protected data (the snapshot, the market views, retrieved pages) is reachable only through the daemon's socket, and the sandbox gives the agent no other route to it: the repository, `data/`, the ledger and the host are not visible and the network goes only through the egress relay. Evidence: `thesis/tests/test_sandbox_view.py` (the thesis workspace shows neither the repository nor the data and has no direct route out) and `gate/tests/test_sandbox.py`, C3.1's OS-level tests on the same shared sandbox (`sandbox/session.py`), which differs between the tracks only in the client's name and spec: `test_sandbox_view`, `test_only_the_closure_of_the_store_and_no_host_environment`, `test_the_client_cannot_be_replaced_or_fed_modules`, `test_network_only_through_the_relay_to_its_hosts`. The canary (`sandbox/canary.py`; `sandbox/tests/test_canary.py` for its decisions, `test_the_real_cli` for the live run) is the sampled live check that the CLI's permissions hold as the tests assume: before every real session it must reach its daemon through `./<client>`, be denied a Write or Read outside `/work`, any tool outside `session.PERMITTED` and any other Bash command, and a session starts only after it passes (`thesis/runner.py`, `sandbox.canary.passes`). A canary that is Broken or unavailable starts no session. *Per-session condition:* a session whose transcript uses a tool outside `session.PERMITTED` (Read, Write, Bash) fails C10.2, and so does a session whose transcript cannot be audited. The transcript on disk is audited however the session ended (finished, error, kill, or a runner death closed by `close_interrupted`); a session whose CLI never started has none and nothing to audit. A tool such as a server-side WebSearch or WebFetch would be a disclosure channel that bypasses both the daemon and the sandbox's network, and the canary samples permissions only at a session's start. The check compares tool names, so it cannot fail an honest session as shell-text parsing did. Evidence: `thesis/tests/test_refreeze_round14.py::test_c10_2_*`, which run real `Track.session` and `close_interrupted` records through `thesis/report.py`, `c10_2` (read from each `session_end`'s `outside_permitted` and `audit_error`, recorded by `Track.audited`).

*What is not claimed.* The transcript is not mapped to the records: a command's arguments, FILE contents or refusals are not inferred from shell text or output, and a refusal the client makes before reaching the daemon discloses nothing and has no record. Each session's `session_end` records the daemon's call count as information, not a pass condition. The canary samples the CLI at the start of a session, not every call within it.
