## Part A. Semantics

### A1. One machine, two states

Version 2 is one event-driven machine: a program places priced orders, and an exact ledger keeps its account against recorded quotes and prints. It has two states, and round 2's contradictions came from writing laws about one "machine state" that mixed them:

- **The world:** the ledger, the orders, the wishes on their way to it (A6) and the book's latest quote. It acts on an event at the event's `time`.
- **The view:** what the program may read: the market as the world had it `δ` ago, and the account as its effects say. It holds an event from the event's `known` time, and an effect from the effect's time plus `δ`.

An **effect** is any change the world makes to the account or its orders: an order placed or refused, a fill with its fee, a cancellation with its cause, a funding payment, a liquidation, a bankruptcy with what was absorbed, a cycle's end. That is a definition, not a list to keep complete: nothing in the account or the orders changes without an effect, so the view's copy of them is the world's, `δ` late.

A **venue** is the record of what varies between runs: the fill rule (`optimistic` or `pessimistic`, A7), the order delay `λ`, the observation delay `δ`, the staleness length `G`, the order cap and the minimum order. There is no second engine and no switch inside a rule. A market of bars runs on the same machine, through a source that derives quotes and prints from bars under stated assumptions (A16).

### A2. Numbers

There are two sorts of number, and the ledger holds only one.

- **Exact** numbers are rationals. Prices, sizes, money and rates are exact. The ledger's state is cash and a position in each coin; equity is cash plus each position times its mark. **Nothing in the ledger rounds.**
- **Float** numbers are binary64, evaluated in stage 6's operation order (`reference/SEMANTICS.md` §1, §5) with two deliberate version-2 changes: (1) `+ − × ÷` and `sqrt` are IEEE 754's, correctly rounded, while `exp` and `log` are evaluated via pinned nixpkgs MPFR (`pkgs.mpfr`, rounded once to 53-bit binary64 with IEEE subnormalization via `mpfr_subnormalize` and IEEE 754 special cases; pinned by `X4` and verified bit-for-bit in step 5 test 12), rather than exact rational series or glibc `libm`; and (2) rolling `sum`, `mean` and `std` windows use a two-stack sliding-window monoid queue (SWAG) with Chan's parallel variance formula over `NumOps α` (`KERNEL.md` A17; round 8, text corrected round 9), removing stage 6's post-eviction subtraction drift (defect 1) and `sumsq/n − m²` cancellation/overflow (defect 2) while keeping `NumOps α` parametricity for `L2`, `L3`, and `N1`. Every stateless float operation's result is the one binary64 its arguments determine, and every stateful float window's result is the deterministic binary64 function of the node's input sequence from its first tick specified by A17, identical on every host under the floating-point environment A13 fixes.
- **Three crossings, all total.** `exact(f)` is the rational that the float `f` is. `float(e)` is the binary64 nearest the rational `e`, ties to even. A comparison of floats gives a bool. A float that is not finite makes whatever reads it undefined.
- **Roundings,** each of an exact value: a slot's price to the coin's price grid, away from the market (a buy down, a sell up); a slot's size to lots, toward zero; the report's three sums a coin, each term to a multiple of `2⁻¹²⁸`; a trade of the test ledger, to its own lot and toward zero; and the confirmer's bar, up, at 128 significant bits. The sums and the bar round toward the side that can only fail to confirm (A12, architecture 6.1).
- **A recorded number enters as an integer.** Each exact field has a scale `d`, from 0 to 12 (8 for a price, 6 for a size, 12 for a rate), and its value is the integer `n` of `n · 10⁻ᵈ`. No coin's rules set a scale (study 2's round 17, D337): a perp's lots are `10^−szDecimals` with `szDecimals` at most 6, so every lot is a whole multiple of `10⁻⁶`, and a size reads below `10⁹` units. The scale is the field's; a source declares one of two encodings (A18):
  - `string`: the venue's decimal string, whose digits the reader places with no arithmetic, and which the engine puts at the scale. A string with more decimals than the scale, or with `|n| ≥ 10¹⁵`, rejects the market (`E_DECIMAL`). The recorder's streams and the API are this.
  - `double`: a stored binary64, as the archive's Parquet holds. The engine reads it as the one `n` with `|n| < 10¹⁵` whose nearest binary64 it is, bit for bit, or rejects. Decimals of that range are at least four units in the last place apart, so at most one exists (law X2).
- **What a double cannot say.** The range is `|n| < 10¹⁵` at the declared scale, not "15 significant digits": a price of `10⁷` at scale 8 is rejected, and no recorded price is within a factor of fifty of it. A double that was never a decimal can still be the nearest of one; nothing that reads doubles can see that. What catches it is the coin's grid, which is therefore part of a `double` price's reading: a double that is the nearest binary64 of an `n` at scale 8 off its coin's grid is no reading, and, as any double that is no reading, rejects the market (C2): no reader reads a double, so only the engine can find one. A `string` price carries its digits, needs no witness, and is never read against a grid (round 17, adopting study 2's proposal with D337: the archive's doubles keep the one evidence they had, and no forward market event is read against a coin's rules). Float observations are never read as decimals.

### A3. Time and events

Times are integer milliseconds. A market has a horizon `[w_start, w_end)` and a finite list of events. Each has a coin, a `time` at which it happened and a `known` time, at or after it.

| Kind | Holds | Class |
|---|---|---|
| listing | the end of the coin's listing, which holds no price, the record holding no settlement price of the venue's (study 2's round 14): its position closes at its adverse price (A10, A12) | 1 |
| funding | the hour it settles, the settled rate, and the oracle price when the source has it; or, an unsettled hour, the hour alone, which the venue's replies show it settled none of (`KERNEL.md` A18) | 2 |
| trade | a print: its venue id, price, size, aggressor side | 3 |
| quote | a snapshot: up to 20 bid and 20 ask levels, each a price and a size, best first | 3 |
| observation | one value of a declared source (a name, a unit, a float; a `state` or a `flow`) | 3 |
| tick | the program's clock (A4); not in the market | 4 |

The machine consumes events in the order (time, class, coin, kind as listed, position in its source). A print comes before a quote of its own millisecond: a snapshot stamped `t` shows the book after what traded at `t`, and an order that arrives at that quote was not there for those prints. **Admission admits or rejects, and reads no market event against its coin's rules** (study 2's round 17, D337; C2). A coin's rules are the venue's for orders, fixed for a run by its header: no event changes them (round 20, `KERNEL.md` A18). The engine reads them where a wish becomes an order (A6), in its margin (A10), and in a `double` price's reading, as its one witness of having been a decimal (A2), and at no fill (A7). Admission rejects a market whose events are out of that order, whose trade repeats an id at one timestamp (a print is its coin, time and id; a source that writes a match once a side has its prints derived by its reader, which holds a match to once in the record by its two orders and not by its id, architecture 6.4), which holds a second settlement of one hour, any event of a coin after its listing's end in that order, a number A2 cannot read at its field's scale, a time in a protected window (A13), or a quote or a print the model has no price in: a quote crossed, one-sided or not strictly best first, or a price or a size not above zero. The reader counts each of these last, and a `string` number that fails A2, as a line that holds no item, and feeds none (`KERNEL.md` A18), so one fed is the reader's fault. Every other event is admitted. So a listed coin's price is above zero, a return has a divisor, and an ended coin has no event. An admitted quote or print off its coin's rules, a price off the grid or a size not in its lots, has an `off_rules` trace row (`KERNEL.md` A19): V1's and V3's witness, counted, which decides nothing. To reject it would end two years at the venue's first change of a coin's rules, and to drop it would choose the record by its content.

- **`known`.** A quote's and a trade's is `time + δ`, and a funding row's is its `time` plus its source's registered `lag`. An observation's is given by its source's certificate, and no listing's end's or observation's is sooner than its source's `lag` after its `time` (A18). A quote, a trade and a funding row take nothing from the envelope of the line that holds them, the time a frame or a pull was received or what the pull asked, and compare their decimals as A18 placed values (`"0.0001"` equals `"0.00010"`), so one row served by many pulls is one event; a listing's end and an unsettled hour are as `KERNEL.md` A18 says, which is the rule. An effect's is its time plus `δ`.
- **A timestamp is complete** only when an event with a later time has been fed, or at `finish`: until then the machine neither fires that time's tick nor closes its events, so a chunk may end anywhere (C1).
- **Observations** are how any other data enters: bars as signals, a forum count. A `state` source's value at a tick is its latest known observation. A `flow` source's is the sum, in order of delivery, of those that became known since the last tick, and undefined when none did. A quote is a state and a print is a flow; a declared source says which it is.
- **A coin's price** is the mid of its latest quote, or the price of a print since; the end of its listing leaves it there. It is the one valuation in the machine: a print is the newest fact about what the coin trades at, and a mid that a print has passed is an old one. A **price event** is a quote, a print, a funding event that settles a coin with a price, or a listing's settlement, whose return is zero: what moves the price or pays on a unit held. An unsettled hour is none, and neither is a funding event of a coin with no quote yet, which has no price, no holder and so nothing to pay. Rounds 3 and 4 both broke a report that valued at mids while orders filled on prints.
- **Staleness.** A coin is stale from its latest quote's `time + G` until its next quote, before its first quote, and after its last. It is causal: nothing later decides it. The next quote ends it for what follows that quote in the order above and for nothing before: a print in the millisecond of the first quote after a gap is still inside it (Argon's finding 5, round 5). While a coin is stale no order of it fills. **The view has its own staleness, the world's `δ` earlier:** at a tick `τ` a coin's price inputs are undefined exactly when the world held it stale at `τ − δ`, which known quotes alone decide. Round 3 showed that inputs read from the world's flag told the program a quote it had not yet seen had arrived. The trace marks both, and marks each print with the world's, so that nothing outside the engine decides staleness a second time (architecture 6.4); the report gives each coin's stale share and whether a position or an order was open across it.

### A4. The clock

The program declares `tick`, a whole number of seconds from 1 up, and `decide_every`, a whole number of ticks. Tick `k` is at `τ = k · tick`, for every `τ` in the horizon.
- At every tick the view takes every event and effect that has become known, and every expression is evaluated once. Windows count ticks.
- At every `decide_every`-th tick, counted from the epoch, the slots are read and reconciled (A6).
- A tick with nothing new reads what the tick before read. Only staleness makes a price input undefined.

Fills, funding, margin and the floor are not on the clock: they happen at their events.

### A5. The language

Strategies stay data: one JSON AST, type-checked, interpreted. Its grammar is A17 and the encoding of every event A18, both in the spec beside the code (`kernel2/KERNEL.md`).

**Header.** `version: 2`, `tick`, `decide_every`, `leverage` (a number for each coin or one for all, at most the coin's maximum), and `probes`, named expressions written to the trace at every tick.

**Slots.** A program has a fixed list of at most 16 named slots, the same for every coin. A slot is a standing wish, not a command:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `side` | `buy`, `sell` | |
| `when` | bool | the slot is active; undefined is false |
| `price` | exact, a price | every order has one |
| `size` | exact, a size or a notional; or `close` | the **target**: what this slot is to have filled in its cycle. `close` has no target: its order's remainder is what of the position it would reduce, a sell's the long and a buy's the short |
| `tif` | `gtc`, `alo`, `ioc` | `alo` is post-only |
| `reduce_only` | bool | its remainder is at most what of the position it would reduce (A6) |

There is no market order. An order that must fill now is an `ioc` at a price the program states, such as `ask × 1.01`; so every buy has a largest cost, known when it is placed. A notional becomes a size by exact division by the slot's price.

**A cycle** on a coin runs from one return to flat to the next: it begins flat, and ends at the fill that brings the position back to zero (A6). So the flat wait before an entry and the position it opens are one cycle. A slot's `filled` counts within the cycle. This is the whole of a ladder's memory.

**Inputs.** All are per coin, read from the view:

| Input | Sort, unit | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `bid`, `ask`, `mid` | exact, price | the latest known quote's best prices and their mean |
| `bid_size`, `ask_size` | exact, size | the best levels' sizes |
| `high`, `low` | exact, price | the highest and lowest mid and print price that became known since the last tick (at the first tick, since the horizon's start); the latest mid when nothing did |
| `traded` | exact, size | the size printed in the same interval; on a market with no trade source, `start` rejects a program that reads it (`E_UNKNOWN_INPUT`) |
| `oracle`, `funding` | exact, price and rate | the latest known funding row's; `oracle` is undefined when the row has none |
| `pos`, `entry` | exact | the signed position, and its cost over its size (undefined when flat); a fill that reduces the position leaves `entry` as it was |
| `first_price`, `first_size`, `age` | exact | the mean price and the total size of the fills of the order that opened the cycle, and the ticks since its first fill became known; undefined when flat |
| `filled(slot)`, `resting(slot)`, `resting_price(slot)` | exact | the slot's fills this cycle, and its live order's remainder and price |
| `equity`, `free_margin` | exact, money | equity at prices (A3); and equity at adverse prices less the initial margin of the positions and of what the live orders open, with their fees (the two sides of A6's first condition) |
| a declared source's name | float, its unit | A3 |

Own-state inputs are computed from the effects the view holds, so they lag the world by `δ`.

**Expressions.** Float expressions are stage 6's; where one of its operators names the hour, version 2 counts ticks. Exact expressions are decimal literals, exact inputs, `+ − × ÷`, `min`, `max`, negation and `exact(f)`; a division by zero is undefined. Comparisons of either sort give a bool. A float expression reads an exact one through `float(e)`.

**Typing.** Stage 6's unit checker (L2) gains the sort, the units size and money, and the rule that price times size is money. A literal in an exact expression is the decimal as written.

**What the ladder looks like.** An entry slot whose `when` is the trigger; five `alo` buy slots at `first_price × 0.9775`, and so on, with targets `first_size × 1`, `× 1.93`, …; one `reduce_only` sell slot of size `close` at `entry × 1.0056`. Every constant is a decimal. No kernel rule knows what a ladder is. A target weight is two `ioc` slots, the buy's target `filled(buy) + max(0, w × equity ÷ ask − pos)` and the sell's its mirror: both terms come from one view, so a fill in flight is subtracted once by the world (A6). A view `δ` old holds the weight `δ` late, as any controller of a delayed reading does.

### A6. Orders

**An order is a price and a target, and belongs to one cycle.** At every deciding tick `τ` each slot sends its **wish**: its exact price and target, or nothing when the slot is inactive, with the cycle the view stood in. The wish reaches the world at `τ + λ`, a slot's wishes in the order sent, and the world rounds it (A2) by the coin's rules. When one arrives:
- if its cycle has ended, or the account is bankrupt (A11), it does nothing;
- if it equals the slot's live order in price and target, nothing changes;
- otherwise the live order is cancelled, and the wished order is placed, if its remainder is not zero and it is admitted.

Until a wish arrives the old order can still fill. The world, not the view, computes what an order may still fill: its **remainder** is its target minus the slot's fills in the cycle, never below zero, and for `close` what of the position it would reduce; a `reduce_only` order's is at most that. So a `reduce_only` order with nothing to reduce (a sell while flat or short, a buy while flat or long) is not placed, one already placed takes nothing while it has nothing, and nothing is ever cut from a fill (A7). An order whose own fill took its remainder to zero is done and no longer live. So a fill between a wish and its arrival is never bought twice, a target below what has filled places nothing, a take-profit follows the position without being replaced, and a `close` wished while flat places nothing.

**A cycle's end cancels the coin's every order,** at the fill that ends it and before any other fill of that event. A target is a quantity of a cycle, so an order has no meaning outside its own: one that outlived its cycle regained its whole target when `filled` started again (round 3). A new cycle's orders therefore come only from wishes sent after the view saw the old one end, `δ + λ` later at the soonest. A liquidation ends every coin's cycle, a flat coin's too, so no wish on its way at one places an order (Argon's finding 4, round 4). A wish for a coin whose listing has ended places nothing.

**Cancel** is a slot going inactive, **expiry** a `when` that reads `age`, **replace** a wish whose rounded price or target has moved. There is one wish a slot a deciding tick, so nothing is scheduled twice and an `ioc` that found nothing is sent again.

Orders placed at one time are admitted one by one: first every `reduce_only` order, then the others, each group by (coin, slot) in the program's order. What an order **opens** is the amount by which filling its remainder would raise the position's absolute size. An order is admitted only if
- with it, the initial margin of the positions and of what every live order opens, at its limit price, plus each such order's taker fee, fits in equity at adverse prices (A10);
- its remainder's notional is at least the venue's minimum, 10 USD, unless it only reduces (it is `reduce_only`, or its size is `close`) and its remainder closes the position exactly;
- its notional is at most the venue's cap.

Otherwise it is refused, with a reason, and the next wish tries again. An order's requirement in the first condition is what it opens, at its limit price, times one over the leverage plus the taker rate in force: the fee is on what it opens, so a sell that only reduces needs nothing. What an order opens is its own, against the position at the check, and is not summed over its side (V11). The minimum and its exemption are the venue's (`venue/hyperliquid/rules.json`: reduce-only orders that exactly close a position are exempt). So a program closes dust with `close`, or with a `reduce_only` order whose target covers the position, since its remainder is then the position; an order that is neither is held to the minimum, whatever its fill would close (Argon's finding 5.1, the last pass's round 3: the text had said "opens nothing", which is wider than the venue's rule, and "with `close`", which is narrower). At a leverage of 1 the first condition says that live buys and their fees fit in cash when each is admitted. Funding can take that cash later; what answers it is the check of A10, and no law promises cash stays positive.

### A7. Fills

**A sell mirrors a buy throughout:** exchange bid and ask, buyer and seller, and reverse each inequality. Every rule and law below is written for a buy.

Let a buy at `p` be placed at `ℓ`. Its **arrival** is the coin's first quote with `time > ℓ`, with best bid `b` and ask `a`:
- **`a ≤ p`** (marketable): `alo` is refused; `gtc` and `ioc` take the ask levels priced at most `p`, best first; the rest of a `gtc` rests at `p`, the rest of an `ioc` is cancelled.
- **`a > p`:** it rests at `p`; an `ioc` is cancelled.

A fill at arrival is a taker fill at the levels' prices. Orders arriving at one quote take from it in admission order, each from what the ones before left. An order meets the first book recorded after it is placed, never one the program had seen.

**One fill an order an event.** At arrival and under both rules below, an order's fill at an event is the least of what the record offers it there and its remainder (A6), taken in the order offered: at arrival the ask levels priced at or within its own that the orders before it left, best first, at their prices; under a rule, at its own price. A recorded size is offered as recorded, in whole lots of the copied rules or not: no rule of a coin's is read at a fill. It is finer than the copied lots only where the venue's lots are, and there a fill of it is one the venue makes; study 2's round 17 rounded an offer to whole copied lots (D337), under which an hour of small prints fills no maker while the books fill takers, the asymmetry D337 removes, and a print under one lot qualifies and fills nothing (Neon's finding 5 on it). It is one fill however many prices it takes: one fee, one change of the position and of `filled`, one row, and one test of the cycle's end, so an order's own walk never ends its cycle part way. The rules differ in what is offered and when, and in nothing else (`briefs/3-kernel-v2.md`, step 5).

**A resting order fills on flow.** An `ioc` never rests, so no print fills one, before its arrival or after. A quote shows who is standing, and the same seller stands in the next one; a print is a sale that happened once. So a quote never fills a resting order under the judged rule. The two rules differ in one thing, the burden of proof:

| | Credits a resting buy at `p` | Fills |
|---|---|---|
| `pessimistic`: only what the record proves | after its arrival, a **sell-aggressor** print at `x < p`, size `s` | `s` lots, shared as below |
| `optimistic`: whatever the record does not exclude | after `ℓ`, any print at `x ≤ p`; after its arrival, a quote with `a ≤ p` | the whole remainder |

- Before its arrival the record neither proves the order rests nor excludes it, so `optimistic` fills there and `pessimistic` does not.
- Under `pessimistic` the order fills, as a maker at `p`, the lesser of its remainder and the lots allotted to it. A print's lots are allotted to the account's resting orders it qualifies for, best price first, then by slot, **each lot once**: what an order took is not allotted to the next.
- Under `optimistic` the fill is a maker's at `p`. Round 2's text had `b ≤ p` for the quote, which filled an order for joining the best bid (Argon's finding 4); what cannot be excluded is a sale at the order's price or below, and a bid standing under it is not one.

**Why these.** A seller who hit a bid below `p` would have hit this order first: his lots are offered through the price. A buyer lifting an ask priced under `p` proves only that someone stood there. `optimistic` is a fiction of unlimited size, named so, and the law about recorded lots (E1) is about `pessimistic` alone. A `pessimistic` venue needs a trade source: `start` rejects one on a market without it.

**What the rules do not bound.** Fills feed back: a fill starts a take-profit, ends a cycle, frees margin. Neither run's return bounds the other's or the truth's (Argon's two constructions, round 1). There is no law of whole runs. For one real order at its recorded price there is a statement that needs no assumption, and it is tested (architecture 6.4).

**What is assumed (V10).** Our orders move nobody: a taker fill does not thin the next snapshot, and every print would have happened as recorded. So two orders arriving at two snapshots can each take the same standing seller, and a seller taken at a quote can still sell in a later print (Argon's finding 4). No rule about one event removes that: it is a hypothesis about how the book refills, the venue's cap keeps an order inside the first levels, and the human accepts it as an assumption that both of study 2's claims name (architecture 6.8). The report counts an order's taker fills at a quote that came less than `G` after an earlier quote at which an order of the account took on the same coin and side; fills at one quote are not earlier than each other. That count is a diagnostic and not a certificate: two takes further apart than `G` can meet one seller who never moved, so a count of zero does not show the assumption unused (GPT, round 4), and a claim names V10 whatever the count.

**No fill beats the tape.** Every fill is at a price no better than its coin's price after the fill's event: a buy at or above it, a sale at or below. An arriving buy pays asks, which are above the quote's mid. A resting buy fills at its own price on a print at or below it, or on a quote whose ask is. A liquidation sells no higher than the price and buys back no lower (A10), and a settlement closes at the coin's adverse price, which is no better (A12). No fee is negative (A8). So a trade is never a gain on the books at the moment it happens, under either rule, and the test of a study rests on that alone (law T1, architecture 6.1).

### A8. Fees

A fill's fee is its notional times the rate of its role, maker or taker, in the fee schedule in force at the fill's time, exactly. The venue's own fee is that figure cut down to a whole millionth of a USDC by a double product (V19); the ledger cuts nothing, so its fee is never under the venue's and at most a millionth over it. The exact fee is the one fee that scales with a trade: the test ledger copies a fill at a scale and at its row's rate (A12, K1), and a cut of whole millionths is a function of a notional and no rate. It is a choice of model, said with every claim, and no equivalence to the venue's account: a millionth can carry a balance across a threshold (V19). The schedule is a market input: a list of dated tiers. The fill's trace row records the role, the tier and the rate. Today it has one tier, the venue's base (maker 0.015%, taker 0.045%), from 2026-04-03 (facts §7). The simulated account does not move between tiers (V4). A listing's settlement charges no fee. **Every rate is from 0 to 1:** `start` rejects a schedule that holds one outside, and so does the replay of given fills. A rebate is a fill better than the tape under another name, and T1 speaks of prices alone (GPT, round 5). A rate above 1 makes a sale cost its seller cash: a sale brings the price times one less the rate, and a dated tier of 2 took the test ledger's cash to −1 at an ordinary sale (GPT's finding 1, round 6).

### A9. Funding

A funding event names the hour it settles. At one, of a coin at `time` with rate `ρ`, cash falls by `q · O · ρ` exactly, `q` being the position the world holds at the event, after every event before it in A3's order and all each caused, and `O` the event's oracle price, or where the source has none the coin's price (before its first quote a coin has none, and no position to pay on: A3). Then the margin check of A10 runs. So a payment is on the position before any fill of its millisecond's prints and quotes, whose class is after funding's, and after a liquidation that an earlier funding event of its millisecond caused: a coin that liquidation closed is not held at its own payment, and pays nothing (GPT-6.2 on round 18, against "before any fill at that time", which a liquidation's closes are). An hour the venue's replies show it did not settle is fed as a funding event with no rate, which pays and moves nothing and names its hour (`KERNEL.md` A18). A coin held into an hour inside the horizon that no funding event names within the registered lateness of that hour aborts the run (`R_SETTLEMENT_MISSING`); `finish` checks the last hours.

### A10. Margin and liquidation

**Marks.** The account is valued at prices (A3) for reporting and at adverse prices for every check: for a long the lesser of its latest quote's bid and its price, for a short the greater of the ask and the price. A check has that one mark a coin: its equity and every notional in it, maintenance's and initial margin's, are at it.

**Cross margin, one balance.** Maintenance is the sum over coins of notional times `1 / (2 · maxLeverage)` of the tier holding that notional. Initial margin is notional over the header's leverage. **After every event that changes cash or a mark** (a quote or a print of a held coin after its fills, a funding payment, a listing's end): if equity at adverse prices is below maintenance, the account is **liquidated**. Every order is cancelled, every coin's cycle ends, and every position is closed against its coin's latest quote, level by level, paying the taker fee, a remainder beyond the levels closing at the last one's. **A long is sold into the bids, each level at the lesser of its price and the coin's price; a short is bought back from the asks, each at the greater.** A print under the bid has spent that bid, so nothing is sold above it, and a print over the ask has spent that ask. Round 4's text gave the long's rule for every position, and a short was bought back under the tape (both reviewers, round 5).

Fills come before the check because a resting bid fills as the price passes it. Isolated margin is not in version 2.

### A11. The floor

After a liquidation, if equity is not positive the account is **bankrupt**: cash is raised by the shortfall, which is recorded as `absorbed`; equity is zero, every later return is zero and no wish places an order.

**The floor is a gift, and no test receives it.** `absorbed` is money the venue paid. It has its own row in the report, and a test copies fills and funding, of which it is neither (A12, architecture 6.2).

### A12. One event, in order

1. **A listing's end:** the coin's orders are cancelled and its position is closed at its adverse price (A10), with no fee, then the check.
2. **Funding:** A9.
3. **Print or quote:** the coin's price moves; orders whose life starts or ends by now do so; then the fills, one at a time, arrivals before resting orders, each with its fee, each changing the position and `filled` before the next, and the one that brings a position to zero ending its cycle there; then the check and the floor. An observation changes nothing here.
4. **Tick:** deliver, evaluate, and at a deciding tick reconcile.

**A fill** is one of three kinds: an order's at an event (A7), a liquidation's close of a coin (A10), a listing's settlement. Each is one fill, with one row, however many prices it takes. The laws about orders speak of the first.

**The report is the world's own books,** written as the run goes. Equity is cash plus each position at its coin's price. Every fill, fee, funding payment and absorption is in cash at its own event and nowhere else. The report holds nothing back and normalizes nothing: three rounds of findings came from a report that kept a second account of when a fill counted and what was at stake.
- **A row for each cash flow:** every fill of the three kinds, every funding payment, every absorption. Its time, coin and kind; the coin's cycle; the units bought or sold; the cash paid or received, with the fee and beside it the fee alone; the position before it; and the coin's price after its event.
- **A row for each of a span's coins at each UTC day's end and at the span's end:** its price; its latest quote's best bid and that quote's time; its first price in the span; and since the span began, over the coin's price events, their count, the least of their returns and zero, the funding a unit paid, and three sums: of the returns, of their squares and of their cubes. A quote's or a print's return is the new price over the old, less one. A funding event's is minus what a unit paid over the price. A settlement, which leaves the price, is the coin's last return and zero. A coin has no return before its first quote, and a funding event before it enters none of these (A3). Each term of a sum is rounded to a multiple of `2⁻¹²⁸`, a return and a cube up and a square down, and the sums are then exact; the prices and the funding are exact. A **span** begins at the horizon's start and again when a universe coin's listing ends: at that event, after the settlement, the row of each of the ending span's coins is written, and the next span starts from the prices then. **A span's coins** are the universe coins still listed at its start. A coin whose listing ended has its last row at that span's end and is in no later span: its settlement turned every holding of it into cash, and admission refuses any event of it after (A3). Rows at days' ends alone lost the last hours of a span that ended at noon, the settlement among them (Argon's finding 4, round 5).
- **A row for the account at each UTC day's end:** equity; what was absorbed and what funding was paid so far; the long and short notional at prices; each coin's stale share; the count of A7.

**The trace** is the run in full, for whoever asks why: every effect; every listing's end of a universe coin, with the coin's price there, and every settlement of a universe coin, with what a unit paid at it, each whether or not the account held the coin; at every tick each probe's value and, for each coin, its price and the world's and the view's staleness; and every print, with the world's staleness at it. Study 2 reads three of these and decides none a second time: layer 2's join reads the prints' marks, and rung 2's guard reads the prices at ticks and the funding events. The guard does not run on the trace alone: a span's end and every coin's price there are the report's coin rows, and it reads them (B1; architecture 6.4, 6.1). Round 5's trace held a tick's total of funding and not the events. Whether a rule's cash runs out depends on which payment comes first, and a total does not say (GPT's finding 3, round 6). A listing's end that met no order and no position was no effect, so it had no row, and two runs that differed only in when a coin settled had one trace (GPT's read after round 6).

**The test ledger** is what a study's confirmer folds from the cash-flow rows (architecture 6.1 derives it). It is a second set of books, with its own cash, starting at 1, and its own units of each coin, starting at none and held in whole **test lots** of `10⁻³⁰` of a unit, defined for an account that is never short:
- **at a buy** of the account's, it buys at the same price and fee rate the least of a **scale** times the units and the most whose cost leaves its cash at least 1/24 of what it has then paid for the units it holds, fees included, a cost of `max(0, (24C − B)/25)` with `C` its cash and `B` what it has paid, then down to whole test lots (K1 writes the fold out; study 2's round 14, the wording). The cut is the funding's reserve (round 13, from study 2's half): without it, seventeen cycles opened together in a dip spend the cash their funding needs, in 7 of round 12's 12 pairs (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `round13_study2`). It is a cut of one buy and nothing set aside, so a payment that takes the cash below it cuts the next buys to nothing. A cycle's scale is fixed at its first buy: `φ` of the ledger's cash then, over `d`. The study registers both, `φ` at most 1 and `d` a sum in the account's money. The ledger follows the first `d` of the account's buys in the cycle, counted before fees, the buy that crosses `d` in part, and nothing after;
- **at a sale,** it sells the fraction of its units that the account sold of its position, at the same price and fee rate, down to whole test lots, and all of them when the account sold all of it or more; what it has paid for that coin's units falls by the fraction of its units it sold, after that cut (GPT-6.2, round 13), so that what it paid stays the cost of what it holds;
- **at a funding payment,** it pays or receives on its own units; one its cash cannot pay **ends** it, as does a fill that takes the account short, each counted first (architecture 6.1), and after its end nothing changes;
- an absorption is not its.

Its **wealth** `W` is its cash plus its units at prices. Nothing else moves it: no step, no stake set between fills, no cash set aside, no mark but the price.

Its **wealth at the bid** `Wᵇ` is its cash plus its units at each coin's **bid price**: the lesser of the coin's latest quote's best bid and its price, which is A10's adverse price of a long. It is what a study's rungs compare (architecture 6.1), and nothing in the books reads it: a scale reads the cash and what it has paid for its units, a trade its row, and the ledger's end a payment and the cash. So it is a valuation and no state. It moves only as `W` does, at a quote or a print of a coin held, and it is never above `W`, since units are never negative and a bid price is at most the price.

**Nothing in it rounds but the size of a trade,** and that is the ledger's to choose, since any size its cash pays for is a scale. **Its numbers are exact, on no fixed grid.** A fill at one price costs a unit a decimal. A fill at several, an order's walk at one event (A7) or a liquidation's close, costs a unit their mean: 10 lots at 1 and 20 at 3 are one fill at `7/3`, and a ledger that buys whole test lots of it has a 3 in its cash's denominator (GPT's finding 1, the last pass's round 2). Round 5 derived a grid, whole multiples of half of `10⁻⁵⁰`, from every price being a decimal; a mean is not one, and no proof rested on the grid. So a denominator grows at a fill of several prices, by at most the bits of its lots, and at nothing else, and since cash is a sum its denominator is the least common multiple of its terms', which holds each prime once: copying all 46 of the vault's such fills in seven months takes it from the grid's 168 bits to 381 (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `last_pass_r2`). Two years at that rate take it to about 900, and at the most a fill can add, 34 bits (an order under the cap at the grid's least price takes at most `10¹⁰` lots; more only in an hour whose recorded sizes are finer than the copied lots), to 5,540 (`last_pass_r3`). What that costs is held's variance and the bootstrap's products, each measured there: at a wealth of 900 bits, a variance of 378,000 bits in under a minute and a path of 48,000 bits in a tenth of a second, in Python's own integers (Argon's finding 4.2, the last pass's round 3). Numerators grow as any account's cash does, and exact arithmetic carries both. Keeping a grid would need the ledger to trade at a price other than the account's, which is not the copy CS2.1 judges, or to copy each level of a fill, a second record of it beside its cash (A19). Round 4 instead rounded every stored balance down to 128 bits and called the result a lower bound. It is another ledger: one that bought a little less before a fall, or paid funding on a little less, ends above the exact one and can outlive it (both reviewers, round 5).

### A13. Identity, the ledger and protected dates

A run's identity hashes the program, every file the run reads with its hash, the venue, the fee schedule, the coin rules, the sources' encodings and `known` rules, the protected windows and the engine's build. A forward replay's files are named by its capture's table, whose rows hash every file it reads and give each its `since`, so the identity hashes the registration, the start, the table, the build and the windows (study 2's rounds 18 and 19: the packed hours' pins, the prior settled state with its report and trace, the pinned `settled/events`, the hourly fee copy and the firing's pull events are deleted with what read them). It is of inputs, all known before the run, since the attempt row below holds it. What a reader dropped or deduplicated is a function of the files and of the reader, both hashed, so those counts are outputs: they are in the result row and the summary.

**Protected dates.** Version 1 refused holdout dates in the kernel (I8, I11); a build instruction not to read them is not that. The protected windows and their releases are a tracked file compiled into the executable, not an argument. It is data and no code: a list of windows, each its start, its end and whether it is released. `start` rejects a horizon that meets an unreleased window, and admission rejects any event whose `time` is in one (C2), so no report or trace can hold a value from it. There are two other entries. `mm2 inspect` is the integrity inspection of `CLAUDE.md`'s Holdout rule and prints what that rule lists: a file's size and hash, the count of its records, and its stale spans, never a value. The engine cannot hold what a person does with a line it printed, so the rest is that rule's: what `inspect` prints of a file dated in an unreleased window stays in the operator's log under `data/`, is in no tracked file, brief, prompt or review bundle before the release, and is no input to a registration (`briefs/4-study-2.md`, step 6). The replay of given fills through the ledger (study 2's layer 1) passes every fill through the same admission, which rejects a fill whose coin, trade id and order id an earlier fill holds, as it rejects a print id repeated at its time; a fill is the fields the replay reads, parsed, and nothing of its line's envelope, and its result through the same ledger rows. A release is a commit to that file, and changes the identity.

**The engine's build** is Nix's hash of the derivation that builds what a judged run executes: the Lean engine, `mm2` with its readers, and a study's own runner, `study2`, taken with the protected-windows file at a fixed stand-in. A derivation is everything Nix gives a build: the sources it compiles, its compilers and libraries, every program a run calls (each by its store path, none found on `PATH`), the build's script, its flags and environment, and the system. Nix builds it in a sandbox that sees nothing else and hashes all of it, as `harness_hash` takes `claude`'s store path. Each list of ours was short: the sources alone, to which the confirming read of study 2's criteria added the toolchain's store paths; three directories, which left the archive's reader out (GPT's finding 2, the last pass's round 1); and every source compiled with the toolchain, which leaves out the options they are compiled and linked with, under which a fast-math build flushes a subnormal product to zero and a trigger's comparison turns (GPT's finding 4, round 2). A list of what can change an executable is never known to be whole; the derivation is what the executable is made from. The build reads its windows file and this hash as data, so a release changes the windows and never the build, and a source, an option or a tool always changes it; a change of the lock changes it only through an input the derivation has. **Two premises of this are checked, since a hash names them and does not make them so** (Argon's finding 2.1, the last pass's round 3). The hash is of the stand-in derivation and the derivation built is the real one: they are one build only where the windows file and the hash enter as data and nothing else, so the suite puts the two back at their stand-ins in the real derivation and requires the stand-in's, byte for byte, which a function that read either as more than data fails. And a derivation fixes a build's inputs, not its output: a build that read outside its sandbox, or depended on how many cores built it, could give two outputs of one hash. So each judged executable is built on two hosts, each in its sandbox, and the outputs are one: evidence for the premise, which no check proves of every host, and which is assumed for the others as Nix's sandbox is (GPT, round 4). An identity records a run's inputs and not what was done with them, so whatever turns a record into a verdict is in the build, and whatever writes the record (the recorders and collectors) is not: their files are inputs. A study that registers a build and its windows before a release runs the same build after it (`briefs/4-study-2.md`, step 6).

**What a build cannot hold: the run's environment.** A derivation fixes what an executable is made of, not what it meets when it starts: the loader's variables (`LD_PRELOAD`, `LD_AUDIT`, `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`), glibc's tunables, the OCaml runtime's parameters, a temporary directory, and the floating-point control register (MXCSR on x86-64), whose rounding mode, flush to zero and denormals-are-zero each change every float operation, and which no probe of a few results can see (Argon's findings 2.2 and 3.2, the last pass's round 3). So:
- **A run reads no environment it inherits, never consults `/etc/localtime` or host CPU count, and its launch is trusted (revised round 9).** Each executable, at its start and before it reads anything, executes itself again by `/proc/self/exe` with an empty environment, unless its environment is already empty, and gives every program it calls an empty one (leaving POSIX `C` locale and clearing `TZ`, `LC_ALL`, `OCAMLRUNPARAM`, loader paths, and glibc tunables). Because glibc's `localtime()` still reads `/etc/localtime` when `TZ` is unset, `mm2` and `study2` never call `localtime()`/`tzset()` or read `/etc/localtime`: all report timestamps format via pure integer UTC calendar arithmetic from milliseconds since epoch. Single-run execution (`mm2`, `study2 forward`) is CPU-count-independent (single-threaded zstd and deterministic order regardless of host core count; any multi-core pool runs only independent full runs side by side). A process `execve` started with an empty environment is already what the re-execution makes, so it runs once and never loops (Argon, the last pass's round 5). Nothing it needs comes from a variable: a program it calls is named by its store path, a directory by its arguments. A new image starts with the control register at its default, so what the first process set is gone too. This removes what an honest launch can carry: a shell's loader paths, which override a Nix executable's own; glibc's tunables; the OCaml runtime's parameters; a temporary directory; host timezone and locale; and core-count differences. It cannot remove code that runs before it: a library the loader is told to preload runs its constructors before `main`, and could read a recording or replace the re-execution itself (GPT, the last pass's round 4). No process contains code loaded into it, so that is assumed, as the integrity of the recordings and of the build is: a judged run is launched by the operator's own command, with nothing preloaded. An operator who would inject code could as well rewrite a recording or run another build. What a person does with protected data is the Holdout rule's (`CLAUDE.md`): the engine's refusal of protected dates keeps them out of every report and trace, and keeps no one from reading a file.
- **The floating-point environment is the default, and is checked.** A process starts with the control register at its default, and nothing in the build sets it. The C stub that calls the engine reads it before and after every call and aborts the run (`E_FPENV`) unless its control bits are the default: round to nearest, no flush to zero, no denormals as zero, every exception masked. These are bits 6 to 15, `(MXCSR & 0xFFC0) == 0x1F80`. Bits 0 to 5 are the exception flags, which any inexact operation sets and which choose nothing, so they are not read (Argon, the last pass's round 5). No double the engine computes passes through x87, so its control word is not read. The engine's C is compiled with no contraction of a product and a sum (`-ffp-contract=off`, `leanc`'s default) and no fast-math option, both in the derivation.
- So `+ − × ÷` and `sqrt` are IEEE 754's correctly rounded operations on every host, and `exp` and `log` are evaluated via pinned nixpkgs MPFR (`pkgs.mpfr`, A2, X4): no float a judged number reads comes from glibc `libm` or from the CPU's choice of a library's path.

Round 2 instead refused at start a host whose `exp` and `log` gave other bits at a few arguments (`E_HOST`). One argument of each function tells two of glibc's paths apart and no more, and a probe cannot see the control register (Argon's findings 3.1 and 3.2): the probe was a list again, of hosts. Its cause is removed: glibc's `exp` and `log` are within a unit in the last place and not correctly rounded, so their bits depended on the path (2,446 of 4 million arguments of `exp` with FMA and without, `arithmetic.py.txt`, `last_pass_r2`), and the engine evaluates both through pinned MPFR (`pkgs.mpfr`, rounded once to binary64). The probe and `E_HOST` are deleted.

**The evaluation ledger** is SQLite whose schema rejects UPDATE, DELETE and REPLACE. **Nothing is published before it is recorded:**
1. an `attempt` row (identity, `session`, `agent`, `parents`) is committed before the engine starts;
2. the `result` row is committed and synced when it ends;
3. only then are the report and trace written and anything printed.

A session's count of attempts is the budget's meter, so a run killed anywhere is counted, and no result exists outside the ledger.

**What a harness needs** (`docs/research/2026-11-13-argon-multiagent-strategy-research.md`, part 3): the meter above; `parents`, the rows an author had seen, so that team topologies are views of one ledger; and an alias map, a bijection from source and coin names to opaque names, under which a program runs as its translation (law N1).

### A16. A market of bars

With `mm` gone, version 2 is the only kernel, and synthetic markets, where observations are free, and histories older than any recorded book are markets of bars. A **bar source** gives, for a coin and a span `[t, t + Δ)`, an open, high, low, close and volume. **A bar is known when it closes,** so the engine derives three events, all at the bar's end `t + Δ` and in this order, and the rest of the machine does not know they were derived:

| Derived event | What |
|---|---|
| two prints | the low as a sell-aggressor print and the high as a buy-aggressor print; the low first when the bar closed at or above its open, else the high first |
| a quote | bid `c × (1 − h)` rounded down to the grid, ask `c × (1 + h)` rounded up, one level a side |

They are a print's and a quote's in every way: known at `t + Δ + δ`, ordered as A3 orders any millisecond, prints first. A print's price is the rational its double is, rounded to the grid's nearest step. The bar's five values are observations at the same time. Round 3's three events sat a millisecond apart, and a tick could fall among their `known` times and read a bar in pieces (round 4, Argon's finding 6, GPT's 7).

**What a bar market assumes,** registered in the market's header and hashed:
- **B1, the spread:** a half-spread `h` above zero, one rate or one a coin. A bar shows none.
- **B2, depth:** every derived level and print holds a registered notional at its own price, in lots rounded up. A bar shows no depth, an order for more is filled in part as on any book, and a bar market says nothing of impact. Round 3 promised that every capped order was covered; a cap on notional at a limit price is no cap on units at another (GPT's finding 8).
- **B3, the path:** the two extremes in the order above, then the close, all at the bar's end; nothing trades between a close and the next bar's end.
- **B4, the extremes traded:** the low was sold into and the high bought.
- **B5, staleness:** `start` rejects a bar market unless `Δ < G ≤ 2Δ`. A bar's prints stand at its end before its quote, and a coin is stale from its latest quote's `time + G`: at `G ≤ Δ` every bar's prints would find it stale and no resting order would fill, and above `2Δ` a missing bar would not make its coin stale. Inside, a coin is stale when a bar is missing.

**What the machine then does, with no rule of its own for bars.** An order placed before a bar's end arrives at that bar's closing quote, and one placed at its end or later at the next bar's: an `ioc` or a marketable `gtc` fills at the ask if its limit allows, and pays the schedule's taker fee. A buy resting since an earlier close fills under `pessimistic` when a bar's low is strictly below its price. Under `optimistic` it fills when the low touches it, in the bar it was placed in too. Margin is checked at the extremes, because they are prints. A program learns everything of a bar, its fills included, at the first tick at or after its end plus `δ`.

Round 2's text claimed more: that these were the classical readings of a bar, with a fill at the next open. Round 3 showed the claim false, and it is withdrawn: an order placed on one bar's information trades at the next bar's close, which is the conservative convention and not the classical one. Nothing here is fitted, and no result of a bar market is compared with `mm`'s.

## Part B. The split, and the laws

### B1. What is Lean

Everything that decides a number: the program's parser and checker; both evaluators, with `exp` and `log` bound to pinned nixpkgs MPFR (`pkgs.mpfr`) via `@[extern]` (X4); slots and reconciliation; the fill rules; the ledger, fees, funding, margin and the floor; the reading of doubles as decimals; the derivation of events from bars; the admission of dates; the trace and the report; the replay of given fills; study 2's confirmer and its guard. One Lake library, `Engine`, in a new package `kernel2/lean`, with no executable and no `IO`. Exact numbers are core Lean's `Rat`.

- **The float evaluator is stage 6's semantics.** The build plan's step 4 takes the parked stage 7 files (`Ref/Source.lean`, `Ref/Resolve.lean`) if they stand alone, and otherwise writes it from `SEMANTICS.md` §1 to §5; B4 judges it either way.
- **The engine is written as its own specification:** plain, total, pure functions. A law is a theorem about them. Two things make it fast, and neither is a second machine (B5; the profile of `kernel2-build` `4616aed`):
  - **The machine keeps what it reads.** A reading is an integer at its field's scale, which is what `Exact.readBits` and `Exact.putPlaced` return. Admission reads each number of an event once and hands the readings on; nothing after admission reads a number, so nothing after it needs a default for a number that failed. Admission's checks are of integers at one scale: above zero, strictly best first, not crossed, and a `double` price on the grid (A2; no other market event is read against its coin's rules, study 2's round 17). The first three have no second form to be held equal to: two readings at one scale stand in the order of their integers. The grid is defined on rationals (A18), and X3 holds the two equal. A `Rat` is made of a reading only where arithmetic leaves its scale: a mid, a coin's price, a value the view holds, a row, the ledger. As first built, admission checked each number and threw the reading away: a quote's best prices were read three times, and every number was made a `Rat` by a division with two greatest common divisors, to be compared once.
  - **A numeric function that is still too slow has a twin,** proved equal to it on every input and attached by the compiler (Lean's `@[csimp]`, which takes exactly such an equality), so no downstream law, no proof and no caller names it:
    - **`Exact.readBits` and `Exact.onGrid` (law X3):** `Exact.readBits` computes on general rationals (5 µs a number); its `@[csimp]` twin `Exact.readBitsFast` computes on naturals and integers. `Exact.onGridAt` is `Exact.onGrid` of the scaled integer's rational, and admission calls it, on a `double` price to admit it and on a `string` price for its `off_rules` row; C2, whose statement names `onGrid` and the rational readings, is proved through that equality.
    - **`Exact.nearestBits`, `Exact.exp`, and `Exact.log` (Project Reset):** `Exact.nearestBits` keeps its proved X5 IEEE roundTiesToEven theorem. `Exact.exp` and `Exact.log` are bound directly to pinned nixpkgs MPFR (`pkgs.mpfr`) via `@[extern "mm2_mpfr_exp"]` and `@[extern "mm2_mpfr_log"]` in `kernel2/lean/c/mpfr_ops.c`. Exact-rational series formulas, incremental `@[csimp]` twins (`X6`), and `O26` are dropped.
    - Nothing else has a twin; a part of the machine too slow for B5 gets one via `@[csimp]` or a reviewed restatement tested differentially (step 5a).
- **The exported surface** is pure functions, `@[export]`ed to C:
  - `check : String → String`, a program's JSON to a verdict;
  - `start : String → String → Except String State`, a program and a market's header (horizon, venue, starting cash, coins with their rules, fee schedule, sources with their encodings and `known` rules, bar assumptions, alias map; A18);
  - `feed : State → Chunk → State × Rows`, a chunk being three flat arrays of events (A18: records of kind, coin, `time` and `known`; integers; doubles), and the rows being the lines of the report and of the trace for the timestamps it completed, as text (A19);
  - `finish`, which runs the clock to the horizon's end and gives the last rows and how the run ended, one typed outcome: completed with the summary's figures, or the fault that rejected or aborted it. The export returns it as a flag, which OCaml publishes by and every law that speaks of completion reads, beside the summary, its JSON (B2, L0; `KERNEL.md` A19);
  - `confirm`, study 2's confirmer, from the report's rows and a stake rule's constants to a result (architecture 6.1): at each day's end the ledger's wealth, its wealth at the bid, which the rungs compare, and the bar. It takes no multiplier: it computes the least of the bar's bound over them;
  - `guard`, from the trace's prices at ticks and its funding events, the report's coin rows, a clock and a mix for each span, to that rule's wealth at each day's end: the test ledger's books, folded over those rows in the machine's order (A3). The report's rows give each day's end and each span's end and the prices there, the trace the funding events and the ticks, and neither alone decides the fold. **A day's end is the report's** (`KERNEL.md` A19): the rule's wealth there is its books, as every event and tick before that midnight left them, at the prices of that day's `day` rows. At one timestamp that is a day's end, then a span's end, then funding, then the tick. So the rule and the bar are read at one instant, and a run cut back to a midnight gives that day the wealth the longer run gave it. A funding event is paid, as the ledger pays it, on the units held into it, one event at a time, and the first the cash cannot pay ends the rule. At the clock's ticks the rule is traded back to the mix, at prices, and its wealth is at prices too: it reads no bid (architecture 6.1). A mix weighs its span's coins and no other, and a coin with no price yet is not bought. At a span's end the rule sells what it holds at that row's prices and buys the next span's mix at them.

  The state holds no history: the ledger, the orders, the wishes on their way, the windows, the running sums and the ids of the current timestamp. Round 3 found two things that grew without bound in it, every trade id and the whole trace (GPT's finding 14).

### B3. The laws

★ marks a law a judged number depends on: proved in Lean on the running code, its statement frozen before its proof. The rest are property tests. A law is a theorem about the specification the engine runs (B2): a property a claim rests on, with a witness, or a characterization, an "exactly when" that determines a subject. What the machine does is its text, which no law duplicates: round 19 deletes the laws that did (R1's clauses binding the run's records to a second machine, F0, M1, M2 and L6's statement over the run's steps), round 20 restores the one of them the 5% rests on (U1's for the machine), and a do-nothing machine fails the tests that each step writes from part A (step 6: an engine that never fills fails tests 1 and 2). Round 2 showed four statements that the transitions contradicted; each is restated from the transition, not the reverse.

**Causality**
- **L1 ★ No future.** Two runs that complete, of one program and one header but for the horizon's end, whose markets agree on the events before `t`, `t` at or before both ends, write the same rows before `t`: every row of the report and of the trace of a time before `t`, and, where `t` is a midnight, its day's end's rows, which are the day's `coin` rows (`at` of `day`) and the `account` row (`KERNEL.md` A19). A run's `span` rows and summary at the end of its horizon, and any row of an event at `t`, are not among them (Argon's finding 5.2, the last pass's round 3). So a run cut back to a midnight writes, to that day's end, what the longer run wrote on the same events. CS2.1's monthly replays rest on it and on every replay reading one record of every time it runs, which the calendar fixes (`briefs/4-study-2.md`, step 7, wall 2): a law of the engine says nothing of a record that changed between two runs (GPT's finding 3, the last pass's round 2). A run that rejects or aborts publishes no row (C2), and this law compares none: as built, a run rejected at an event has not even made its rows before it, since admission is checked before the clock runs up to the event (Argon's finding 2.1, the last pass's round 1).
- **L1a ★ Delivery.** Two markets equal on the events with `known ≤ τ_k`, and on the events with `time ≤ τ_k − δ`, whose runs complete, give equal views, the view's account among them, evaluations and wishes at tick `k`, as `orderRun` records them (round 15: they were read off `snapshots`, a second fold no law tied to production; the events are those that survive the wire, as C1's). What the second premise holds: the view's account is the fold of the effects made before the tick with time at most `τ_k − δ`, which is every such effect unless `δ` is zero (A18, the order of a millisecond), and **an effect is made by the world's rules and the world's funding at its time,** whatever their `known`: an end settles, a payment pays; and the view's staleness is a function of known quotes (A3). The view holds what admission read and has no rules of its own: a quote's and a print's numbers are read at scales no rule sets (A2; round 17), so what it holds of one is a function of the event alone. **The law without its second premise is false,** and is not wanted: two markets that differ in an end timed at or before `τ_k − δ` and not yet known differ in the orders it cancelled and the position it closed, and the view holds those effects `δ` after its time, before it holds the end.
- **L1b ★ No fill on what was seen.** An order wished at `τ` has no fill on an event with `time ≤ τ + λ`.
- **L1c ★ The view's account is the world's, `δ` late** (round 15, strengthened round 16 after Neon finding 1; round 18, over the run's one list of records). At every tick `r`, the effects of the records before it, which are every effect the world made before the tick (R1: the records are one list in A18's order), split strictly at `l.time + δ ≤ r.time`: `r.account` is `initial.acct` folded over the effects with `l.time + δ ≤ r.time`, `r.queue` is the exact remaining effects with `r.time < l.time + δ`, and `r.world` is `initial.acct` folded over them all (which equals `r.queue` folded onto `r.account`), so an effect the world applied that no record holds, or that two hold, fails it (`HiddenEffect`, `SharedEffect`). L1a says the view reads nothing it may not know from the market; L1c that its own account holds every world effect known by `r.time` and none later. **The ledger**
- **A1 ★ Conservation.** Cash is the starting cash, minus buys, plus sells, minus fees, minus funding, plus `absorbed`, each exactly as A7 to A11 give it, and each fee is its fill's notional times its role's rate in the tier in force. Equity is cash plus positions at their coins' prices. `entry` is the cost of the open position over its size. No operation of the ledger rounds.
- **U1 ★ Funding.** At every settlement of a held coin the ledger's cash changes by exactly `−q · O · ρ`, `q` the position it is given, and at no other time for funding (the ledger's, step 2). **The machine gives it that position at every one and no other (step 7; round 20, restoring the half round 19 deleted):** over an observed run, a fed settlement of coin `c` at which the world's position in it is `q ≠ 0` (the effects of the records before its step) has one funding step, of that event, with the effect `paid c q (−q·O·ρ)` and one cash-flow row of that `before` and cash; one at which `q` is zero has none; and no step of any other event is a funding step. A restatement that skips a held coin's payment, its return recorded all the same, keeps every other law and hands the copied ledger carry its bar is charged for, which the 5% does not admit (Neon on round 19). A held coin's due hour with no funding record inside its lateness aborts the run (`KERNEL.md` A18).
- **L6 ★ The floor.** Equity is never negative; `absorbed` rises only at a bankruptcy, by exactly the shortfall; after one every return is zero.
- **P1 ★ The report.** Every row is the stated function of the world at its time. A run's fills as `orderRun` observes them are the report's fill rows, in order, so what S1 and the laws of fills say of the run's records holds of what the report holds (round 12). The cash-flow rows are all of A1's terms and each once: starting cash plus their cash is the world's cash at every event, and the units of a coin's rows sum to its position. A coin's row holds its price, its latest quote's best bid and that quote's time, its first price in the span and the funding a unit paid in it, exactly; its sums bracket the exact sums of its returns, squares and cubes over its price events, each within the count of events times `2⁻¹²⁸` on the side A12 names; its least return is exact. ### B5. Scale

A book market is slow and a bar market fast. A bar market has three derived events a bar, so a search at the pilot's scale runs on bars; a search over book markets is not a goal.

**What the wall is for.** A run is one account on one clock, so it is one thread: more hosts buy more runs at once and never a faster run. The wall is that **no run a study makes takes more than a day,** and admission alone, with no program, has half of it. Study 2 has two kinds of run, and they read numbers in two ways (A18):
- **The forward record,** two years of the recorder's streams: 17 coins at 0.55 s and five levels a side, about 1.95 billion quotes of 20 numbers. **Its numbers are decimal strings,** placed as integers by the reader, and no double is read. Admission alone within 12 hours is **45,000 quotes a second,** 1.1 µs a number; with its prints and a ladder, a day, 22,500.
- **The archive's window,** 16 coins over 212 days: 229 million quotes of 80 numbers, each a double. Admission alone within 12 hours is **5,300 quotes a second,** 2.4 µs a number; with trades and a ladder, a day, 2,650. Under 2 GB, the rows streamed and not counted.

The archive's window is measured once at each of the two slices, with the time and the peak memory written to disk. The suite holds the four rates: on the densest day on record, 2026-04-03's 2,487,176 quotes of 16 coins, and on a made-up day in the recorder's form (`briefs/3-kernel-v2.md`, step 3, test 6; step 6, test 11). The forward record is not read before its release, so its rate is held on made-up events alone.

**The earlier walls were wrong twice.** The 20 minutes and the hour were choices with no derivation. The 4 hours and 8 that replaced them (2026-11-16) scaled the forward record to the archive by its count of numbers, 2.1 of the window's, as if both were doubles. The forward record's numbers never pass through the reading of a double, so the window is bound by nothing but its own day, and the forward record by a rate no test then held. No judged number reads a time.

**What the profile showed, and what follows** (`kernel2-build` `4616aed`; BTC's day, 155,453 quotes of 80 numbers in 91.9 s, 590 µs a quote; callgrind's inclusive shares of `feed`): `readBits` 76%, at 5.3 µs a call; `onGrid` 13%, at 1.9 µs; the division that makes each reading a `Rat` 9%, at 0.65 µs; the chunk's decoding 1.4%; every comparison 1%. A quote's best prices are read three times. So:
- **the twins of the first answer were not enough for either run.** With both readings free the window's admission is 67 µs a quote where 4 hours asked 63; and the forward record, which `readBits` does not touch, was never costed: as built it is about 45 µs a quote of five levels, 24 hours for admission alone; of the 45, 17 are the `Rat` made of every number and 19 the grid on rationals (an estimate from the shares above; test 6 measures it);
- **the machine keeps what it reads, on integers (B1).** That removes the division and the grid on rationals from every run, and is what the forward record needs: about 12 µs a quote by the same shares, against the 22 its 12 hours allow;
- **`readBits` keeps its twin (X3),** which only the archive needs: without it the window's admission is 28 hours, 5.3 µs for each of 18.3 billion numbers. With a twin at a tenth of that, about 4 hours.

The last figure of each item is an estimate, and the first answer's estimate was wrong by more than two; the tests decide.
