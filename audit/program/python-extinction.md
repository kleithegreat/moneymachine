### 2.2 The datalayer recorder: keep, as `mmdata` in OCaml

- **What it records:** 642 feeds from 15 certified sources (the Fed's data download program, Treasury yields, the New York Fed, the Philadelphia Fed's real-time data, BLS, EIA, CFTC, ECB, BIS, BEA, Treasury, the SEC's filings feed, openFDA, weather alerts, GDELT): each response body whole, gzip-compressed under its sha256 (`raw/<sha256>.gz`), a day file of polls `{"feed","url","sent","recv","status","sha256","bytes"}`, and a day file of `repairs` when a stored body was found damaged. 803 MB and 6,168 bodies mirrored in 27 days, the largest 55 MB compressed.
- **Who would read it:** a reading-agent study, Q2 of the architecture, parked until a measured count of bets and a venue whose terms permit it (the paper). Its value is the point-in-time record after the models' cutoff: a frozen agent can be run on those dates as of each poll, with what had been published by then, which is the one way such a study need not wait out its whole calendar. Several of its sources keep no vintages. It is cheap: 100m and 256Mi, one 20Gi PVC.
- **Language and place:** OCaml, an executable `mmdata` in `recorder/` beside `mmrec` and `mmvault`: one dune project, one image. It stays a process and StatefulSet of its own, so its faults cannot stop `ws`.
- **One sequential loop.** It needs no Async: `Curl.perform` in the main thread, and a `SIGTERM` handler that sets a flag, acts between polls and wakes the wait.
- **What it reuses:**
  - the recorders' transport options (`RECORDER.md` on `rearchitecture-s2`, What it records, item 1): HTTPS only, HTTP/1.1, no redirects, the CA file, the peer and its name verified, no signals, `SIGPIPE` ignored. Not that client's acceptance rule, which keeps only a 200 under 16 MiB: the datalayer records every status with its body, bodies of tens of MB, and bodies that are not JSON;
  - the recorders' JSON reader, whose integers are read from their own bytes (`RECORDER.md`, item 1, step 3), for `datalayer.json` and the poll log;
  - of `recorder/`, its build, gates and image. `mmops` is the only other user of its prefix hashing and of the C stub (2.5);
  - `digestif` (sha256), `re` 1.12.0 for the one discovery pattern, `uri` 4.4.0, `camlzip` 1.11 or `decompress` 1.5.3 for gzip, `otoml` 1.0.5 or `toml` 7.1.0 for the registry: all in the pinned nixpkgs, so `flake.lock` does not move.
- **Six deliberate changes** from the Python, which its spec states (`recorder/DATALAYER.md`, step 5a):
  1. **A transport failure's stored text is the recorder's own.** Python stores `repr(e)` as the failed poll's body, which no other language can reproduce. The port stores a fixed word naming the stage (`connect`, `tls`, `timeout`, `body`), with `status` null as now.
  2. **A day file is read as bytes.** Python's `live.read` raises on a UTF-8 character cut by a kill and then cannot read the valid rows after it (the strict xfail in `test_recorder_spec.py`). The port skips the torn line and reads on.
  3. **A body's memory does not grow with its size.** Python holds a body two or three times over under the pod's 512Mi. A feed whose body outgrew that would kill the pod at every start, since it is due at every start, and the feeds listed after it would never be polled again. The port streams a body to a temporary file, hashing and compressing as it comes; a feed with a credential is redacted in the same pass, carrying the secret's length less one byte between pieces. A stored body is intact when its decompressed bytes hash to its name, which is streamed too.
  4. **Each row keeps the reply's two clocks and its age.** `date` is the final response's one IMF-fixdate field, by the recorders' rule for the venue's time, or null; `age` is the final response's `Age`, a non-negative integer of seconds, or null; `recv` is this host's clock. Study 2's round 20 (D361, `rearchitecture-s2`) places a reply by its two clocks and takes neither alone: if either is right, the reply was made after `min(date, recv)` less the longest an exchange lasts and before the later of `date` plus its whole second and `recv`, and it is in force from the later; so the hour's check it had is gone, and a clock that is off only delays. Here the longest exchange is the 240 s of change 5, where that client's is 30 s. A reader of these rows places them by that rule, which is its own; the recorder only keeps what it reads. `age` is kept because a cache's reply carries its origin's `Date`: without it such a row can agree with a host late by the cache's age. `ws`, on the same node, records a venue stamp beside `recv` on every frame, so the host's lateness has a third witness too. `adjtimex` is no substitute: it is the host's word about itself, which is why study 2 keeps no such record and `mmrec` gains none.
  5. **The schedule is by cadence, and a poll is bounded.** Python asks the due feeds once a pass, in declared order, 1 s apart, then waits 60 s. So a feed's interval is its cadence plus a pass plus the tick, and a pass is at least a second a due feed: 435 of the 642 have a weekly cadence, so a week's burst is over seven minutes before any reply. The port polls, at each step, of the feeds due (last `sent` plus `every_h` reached) the one of smallest `every_h`, declared order breaking ties; when none is due, it waits until the next one is. A poll ends within 240 s of its start, whatever it is doing. So a due feed waits for at most one poll under way and those of shorter cadence, and 240 s is under the pod's 300 s grace, so a stop always finds the poll finished.
  6. **A file an index names is asked until it answers 200.** GDELT's index names an export minutes before it can be fetched, and the Python asks each named file once and forgets it, so a large part of them is missed (the advisor's count and cause: `audit/decisions.md`, 2026-11-23 03:26 UTC (22:26 EST), whose decision this carries into the spec). Nothing is lost for good, so the missed files are asked again, known at the backfill's own time, while the index's rows keep when each was named. So a poll of a feed that declares `discover` asks the index, and then at most one file. That is the file the index names, unless the log holds a 200 for its URL; and otherwise the pending file longest unasked, where a URL is pending when the log holds it and no 200 for it, has not been asked for a day, and was first named no more than 30 days ago. The log is the only state, and the rule is also the backfill of the files missed before, whose rows are in the log: one a poll. A named file is asked again at every poll until its index names another, which is the decision's "until 200 (or the next index)", and then once a day for a month. A file that has answered 200 is not asked again. Each ask is a row with its own `recv`. D1 comes with it: GDELT's `every_h` is 1/12, so that a file is asked about three times while it is named (a publication a quarter of an hour apart, an interval of 5 minutes plus a poll), and the interval is under the 900 s publication (change 5).
- **The request each feed sends is fixed, not described.** The port sends, for each of the 642 feeds, what the Python sends now. `httpx` re-encodes a URL's query and adds the credential, and measured offline this changes 5 feeds' targets from what `datalayer.json` declares (the three CFTC feeds, openFDA and EIA's storage series, whose `$`, spaces, commas, colons or brackets it re-encodes). Those five are rewritten in `datalayer.json` as `httpx` sends them, which it leaves unchanged when asked again, so the Python's requests do not change. Then the rule is: the declared URL's bytes, with the credential's parameter appended where a feed has one, its value percent-encoded; exactly one `User-Agent`, the credential's where a feed declares a `User-Agent` credential and the file's otherwise; `Accept` as declared or `*/*`; `Accept-Encoding: gzip`, the content-coding undone so that the stored body is the entity the Python stored (`httpx` sends `gzip, deflate`; `ocurl`'s setting names one encoding, and the entity is the same); nothing else. A declared URL never carries its credential's parameter.
- **A fresh handle a request,** as the recorders' transport has, so that one request carries nothing of another's: a kept handle would be a place where a `User-Agent` credential set for one feed could reach the next feed's host. The Python reuses connections, and that buys nothing here: the pass and the bound of change 5 settle the cadence, a handshake is of the order of 100 ms, and the one weekly burst is one a second.
- **`.tmp` files** are bodies cut by a kill, which no row names and the mirror skips. At start, under the writer lock, `mmdata` deletes `raw/*.tmp`.

### 2.5 The mirror and its verifier: seal the closed volumes, port the rest to OCaml as `mmops`

- **The mirror's volumes today:** `hl` (closed 2026-11-22), `sources` (live until step 4), `datalayer` (live), `thesis` (the parked track's ledger and sessions), `agent` (the pilot's series). Three have no writer. `ws`, study 2's only copy, is not mirrored, by the human's choice.
- **Seal a closed volume,** the same way for each, in the order the facts allow. (1) Its writer is gone, and two of its manifests a day apart hold the same entries (path, size and sha256). (2) The volume leaves the copied volumes. (3) Its pin, the sha256 of its entries written one a line as `sha256 size path` and sorted by path bytes, is logged in `audit/decisions.md` and put in the table. The pin is of what the manifest lists and not of its bytes: the Python mirror rewrites every manifest (`taken`, `capacity`) each run, and going back to it must not break a pin. (4) The verifier is given the pin, and its first run over the volume is the seal's check: it compares sources the Python never did, so what it finds is examined before the pin is kept.
- **What the seal buys.** Only closed volumes hold SQLite ledgers and agent workspaces, so the port loses the `ledger` kind (SQLite's backup and integrity check) and the `work/` rule with them. A `.sqlite` file found in a copied volume is refused, naming it: copied whole while open it could be torn, and no live writer keeps one. The parked thesis track, if resumed, would need the ledger kind again: that is the reversal's cost.
- **Verify reads a sealed volume as closed.** The entries its manifest lists must hash to its pin, and every entry's mirror copy and, unless it is a `.sqlite` file (whose entry is a snapshot), its source must match, at once: nothing writes a sealed volume, so a mismatch has no newer manifest to wait for. The wait of up to 1,200 s stays for copied volumes, where a run can be publishing.
- **The manifest is ours at both ends, and its integers are exact.** A file's `mtime` is whole nanoseconds, about 1.8 × 10¹⁸, past the 2⁵³ at which a double, and `jsont`'s own integer decoder, lose a digit. `mmops` reads every integer by the recorders' rule, from the number's own bytes by `-?(0|[1-9][0-9]*)` in its field's range: `1.0` or `"1"` is not an integer, so the guard refuses it as it does now. It prints integers by their digits, which `yojson` does (as `mmrec` writes its events) and `jsont` does not above 2⁵³. Capacity counters lie in [0, 2⁶³ − 1], and the reserve test is `free ≤ total / 5` by integer division, which is `free × 5 ≤ total` without the product.
- **Day files are published by the manifest,** as now: an append goes into the copy in place, and a reader takes a day file to the length its manifest lists. Bytes past it are not published, and the next run truncates them. A whole file is published only complete, through a temporary name and a rename.
- **A listed volume that is not mounted is a fault:** the run refuses a missing source root, where the Python skipped it. The capacity-only and the sealed volumes are listed too.
- **A refusal withholds its own volume only.** The Python stops at its first refusal and takes its volumes in order, so one damaged volume ends the copying of every one after it. Each volume is a mirror of its own: a refused one keeps its manifest as it was, the others are copied, `RUN.json` names the refusal, and the run exits nonzero.
- **Language and place:** OCaml: `mmops mirror`, `mmops verify` and the guard (2.7), an executable of the `recorder/` project beside `mmrec`, `mmvault` and `mmdata`. It shares with `mmdata` the prefix hashing and one C stub, which a second project would hold twice. The project's store path then moves with any of its executables, which redeploys nothing: `apply` names only the workloads whose own program changed, and `ws` is applied only when `mmrec` is.
- **Reuses:** `digestif`, the recorders' JSON reader, the recorder's build, gates and image. What the libraries at the pin lack is one C stub, beside the recorder's zstd one: `statvfs`, and a file's modification time in whole nanoseconds.
- **The volumes are arguments,** from one table in `ops/workloads.nix` (2.6), to the mirror and the verifier: the copied, the sealed and the capacity-only ones. The guard reads none of it (2.7), so no list has two homes and no order of deployments follows from it.
- **Its spec, to write:** `recorder/MIRROR.md` (step 3a), from `CLUSTER.md` section 5, `ops/live.py`, `ops/diskguard.py` and the tests. `CLUSTER.md` section 5 then summarizes it and links to it.
- **One gap closes.** The guard saw prodesk7's disk only through the mirrored volumes' manifests, which happen to share `ws`'s filesystem, so the alarm for `ws`, study 2's only copy, hung on them by accident. `mmops mirror` takes `ws` for its capacity alone, mounted read-only, and reports it by name (2.7; step 3g).

### 2.6 The deployer: into the flake

- `push` and `apply` are glue around `nix build`, `skopeo` and `kubectl`; `WORKLOADS`, `pod_spec` and `manifest` are data. Each manifest is a pure function of the flake and one image digest.
- So the manifests become a flake output, built from a Nix file in `ops/` as `ops/units.nix` builds the timers, and `push` and `apply` become two shell apps of the flake, run on this PC as now. The boundary tests become a flake check over the built JSON.
- **The tag is the revision that was built.** The Python builds `REPO#image` from the working tree and tags it with `HEAD`'s short hash, so a dirty tree can carry another commit's tag. The app is built from the flake and carries its own revision, and refuses a tree that has none.
- **The way back from a cluster switch is the spec that ran.** Before a switch, `capture` saves each workload it changes as the cluster holds it, less what the server sets (`status`, and the metadata's `resourceVersion`, `uid`, `creationTimestamp`, `generation`, `managedFields`), and `kubectl diff` of the file must then show nothing; `restore` applies the file. The image it names is checked to be in the registry. A spec is a command, its mounts and a digest together, so to go back by digest alone is to start an old image under new mounts, or a new command in an old image. ### 2.7 The disk guard: OCaml, in `mmops`

`mmops diskguard`. A shell guard was weighed and refused: the tests' counters reach 2⁶⁰, and `jq` reads numbers as doubles.

- **It is told nothing about volumes.** At the end of a run, whatever it refused, `mmops mirror` writes `/mirror/RUN.json`: `taken`, and for each volume it was given (copied, or for its capacity alone) the source's capacity and the reason it was refused, or null. The guard reads that one file, and measures this PC's `data/` and the mirror's filesystem as now. It alarms when the file is absent, malformed, or its `taken` is not an integer in the last 30 minutes; when a volume was refused, naming it and why; when a capacity is missing, invalid, or at or below the reserve (2.5). A run that dies writes none, so the file goes stale. What to watch is thus what the mirror was run with, and there is no second list to keep in step with it.
- **Deploying it is the human's dotfiles switch, not a merge.** The home-manager module pins a revision of this flake, and the unit runs that revision's store path (checked: `systemctl --user cat mm-diskguard.service` shows a path, and no `OnFailure=`). So a merge changes nothing that runs, and a `git revert` rolls nothing back. The program is deployed by updating the dotfiles' lock and switching; the previous home-manager generation is the way back; and what proves either is the path the unit names (`systemctl --user show -p ExecStart`), not the checkout's build. Two other shapes were weighed. A program that resolves the checkout at each firing (`nix run`) makes a merge deploy, but a firing then needs the flake to evaluate and build, so an unfinished merge, a broken flake or a full Nix store silences the alarm, where a pinned path needs only to exist. A pointer that whoever merges repoints is a mutable thing nobody checks. The guard holds no list, so it changes seldom, and a rare act that is seen is the safer one.
- **Its own failure is an alarm.** The unit gets `SuccessExitStatus=1` and `OnFailure=` a second unit that notifies that the guard could not run. 0 is healthy; 1 is an alarm raised and delivered; any other status (a crash, a missing program, a notification that failed) starts the second unit. Today a guard that cannot start is silent, the Python one included. The notification's path is the same `notify-send`, so a dead session bus silences both, the limit `CLUSTER.md` records already, and a timer that never fires is seen by nobody.
- **`--test-alarm`** sends one marked synthetic and exits 1. It is run as the unit runs it, by the path the unit names, so that what is proven is what fires.

## 4. What must match, and how

A port equals the Python wherever a reader, or its own next run, reads what the Python wrote. What is hashed or compared as bytes matches byte for byte; what is parsed matches by value.

- **Byte for byte:**
  - **`mmdata`'s bodies:** the name `raw/<sha256 of the stored body>.gz`, after redaction, and the decompressed content. The gzip member's bytes are free: the Python's carry the time of writing. A damaged body is kept as `<name>.damaged-<sha256 of its bytes>`. Every temporary file's name ends in `.tmp`.
  - **The mirror's copies:** each, its source over the length its manifest lists.
  - **What is already on disk:** day files are appended to and never rewritten, so every byte the Python wrote stays.
- **By value,** parsed with exact integers, so that a 61-bit `mtime` is itself and `1` is not `1.0` (2.5); member order and spacing are free. Poll rows are ASCII, with every other character escaped `\uXXXX` (a surrogate pair above the BMP): the Python's reader, which a rollback uses, opens day files as text and dies on a character cut by a kill (its own strict xfail), and an ASCII row cut anywhere is a torn line it skips:
  - **`mmdata`'s rows:** the Python's members, and `date` and `age` beside them; the same for `repairs`. A line written to a file that does not end in `"\n"` is preceded by one.
  - **`MANIFEST.json`:** `files`, each path to its `size`, `mtime` (the whole nanoseconds of `st_mtim`) and `sha256`; and, until 3f, `taken` and `capacity`, which only the Python's mirror and guard read. **`RUN.json`** is new (2.7).
  - **The deployer's manifests** and the `List` they go in.
  - **The requests** of every feed (2.2, step 5d).
- **Free:** the guard's messages, since the Python's quote exception texts no other language prints; the tests name scope and resource, as the Python's do.
