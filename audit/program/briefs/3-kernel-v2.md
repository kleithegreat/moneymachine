## How every step runs

1. **Tests first, by a session that does not build the step,** from this brief and `kernel-v2.md`, without reading the code. Steps marked **◆** decide a judged number, so their tests go to **another model** than the builder's: GPT, or a fresh Opus session labeled as such when GPT is out.
2. **The builder (Sonnet) makes them pass without editing them,** in one commit. A test the builder believes wrong goes back to its author with the reason.
3. **A wall the brief did not foresee goes to the architect as a question.** No flag, tolerance, special case or second path (`CLAUDE.md` §1). Each step names the walls I can foresee.
4. **Opus checks the commit against its step,** and runs the step's checks itself.
5. **★ laws:** each step that proves one first commits its statement alone in `kernel2/lean/Engine/Laws.lean`. Another model reads the statements against part B3 before any proof, looking for one an empty or constant engine would satisfy, for one the transitions of part A contradict (round 2 found four, round 3 three more, round 4 three, round 5 T1 for a short), for one stated outside the records it holds on (round 5, K2), and for one written for buys or for order fills that is quantified over every fill. Then the proof; `checks.engine-proofs` prints each ★ law's axioms and admits only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

**Languages.** Lean 4.34.0 as the flake pins it, for everything that decides a number; core `Rat` for exact numbers. OCaml 5.4.1 with dune and Jane Street's stack (`core`, `async`, `ppx_jane`, `ppx_expect`, `base_quickcheck`, v0.17), `yojson`, `ocaml_sqlite3`, and hand-written C stubs. **Data a builder may read.** The archive before 2026-11-01 and the vault's fills before 2026-11-01. Nothing dated in [2026-11-01, 2027-01-31). From step 3 the executable enforces it (kernel A13); before that, and for every fixture of the recorder's format, data is synthetic.

### 1. ◆ Exact numbers and the two readings of a decimal (A2)

- **Builds:** `Engine.Exact`: grid rounding (a buy's price down, a sell's up, a size toward zero); the reading of a double's bits at a scale, and the nearest double of a rational, both by integer arithmetic. The putting of a placed decimal at a field's scale (A18). In OCaml, the placing of a decimal string's digits: the digits as one integer, and the count of those after the point, zeros at the end not counted.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. `10 × 0.9775` is `9.775` and rounds to itself as a buy on a grid of 0.001; `10 × 1.0056` to `10.056` as a sell; `1.93` coins are 193 lots and `0.29` are 29.
  2. For every scale from 0 to 12 and sampled `n` with `|n| < 10¹⁵`, the nearest double of `n · 10⁻ᵈ` reads back as `n`, and agrees with OCaml's `Float.of_string` on the written decimal.
  3. Rejected: `0.1 + 0.2` at every scale from 0 to 12; a NaN; an infinity; `10⁷` at scale 8 (its `n` is `10¹⁵`). Negative zero reads as zero.
  4. A string with more decimals than its scale is rejected by the engine, as is `"100.000000000000001"` at scale 8, which `Float.of_string` would have rounded to 100 (corrected by the advisor, 2026-11-16: the earlier `"100.00000000000001"` rounds to 100 + 2⁻⁴⁶, found by GPT) (Argon's finding 4). `"5.0"` at scale 0 is 5; `"5.01"` at scale 1 is rejected. The reader is given no scale.
  5. A value off the grid by any amount rounds in its direction and by less than one grid step.
  6. **The grid is the certificate's** (`venue/sources.toml`, "Price grid"; `KERNEL.md` A18), and this test is written from its quoted words and counts digits, where A18's step and its law count multiples. At five figures and one decimal: `123456` is on the grid and `12345.6` is not, a buy there rounding to 12,345 and a sell to 12,346; 99,999, 100,000 and 100,001 are on it and 100,000.5 is not, rounding to 100,000 and 100,001; a sell at 99,999.5 rounds to 100,000. For every price of `arithmetic.py.txt`'s `price_grid`, membership by the sentence equals membership by the step, a buy rounds to the greatest grid price at or below it, to zero when there is none, and a sell to the least at or above.
- **Laws ★:** X2, as a theorem on bits; the rounding bounds of test 5; that a price is a whole multiple of its step exactly when it is on the grid as A18's sentence says, significant figures being A18's multiples.
- **Foreseeable wall:** Lean's `Float` is opaque to proofs. X2 is about the integer functions on bits; `Float.toBits` stays in the trust boundary. The plain `readBits` and `onGrid` were built on `Rat` and met the wall of B5 at step 3, which specifies what answers it (law X3).

### 4. The float evaluator, the clock, the view and inputs (A1, A4, A5's float half, B4)

- **Builds:** the parser and checker for programs with no slots; the float evaluator over declared sources, `state` and `flow`; `probes`; ticks; the view, fed by events at their `known`. Take `Ref/Source.lean`, `Ref/Resolve.lean` and the evaluator from the parked `stage7` tree if they build alone in `kernel2/lean`; otherwise write the evaluator from `reference/SEMANTICS.md` §1 to §5. Say which in the commit.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. **B4:** every expression of the frozen C6.4 corpus, at a tick of one hour, its inputs as observations and its nodes as probes, gives streams bit-identical to the Lean reference's, which the test stores under a hash. The two event counts are `flow` sources: defined at the tick they arrive and undefined after (Argon's finding 7).
  2. A tick finer than the data's cadence reads the latest known value of a `state` source and no window goes undefined; a `flow` source read at two ticks is not counted twice.
  3. An event is never read before its `known`. At the first tick at which anything is known, `high`, `low` and `traded` cover the horizon's start to it; before it every input is undefined and no slot is active.
  4. **The view's staleness (GPT's finding 9, Argon's 5):** two markets that differ only in a quote inside `(τ − δ, τ]` give equal inputs at `τ`, though the world is stale in one and not the other. With `G ≤ δ` a quote is still read for `G` after it became known.
  5. A program reading `traded` is refused by `start`, not by `check`, on a market with no trade source.
  6. **A listing not yet known (the wall on L1a, 2026-11-16).** A coin at `sz_decimals` of 2; a listing at `t₀` that makes it 3, known long after; a quote at `t₁ > t₀` whose best bid's size is `1.234`. At a tick `τ` with `t₁ + δ ≤ τ` and before the listing is known, `bid_size` is `617/500` and `bid` the quote's price. The market without that listing rejects at the quote (`E_DECIMAL`): it is no market L1a compares. Two admitted markets that agree on the events known by `τ` and on those with `time ≤ τ − δ`, and differ in a listing after `τ − δ` not known by `τ` and in the quotes and prints after it, have equal ticks through `τ`.
  7. **A value does not depend on the scale it was read at** (a property, both encodings): for sampled `n` and scales `d₁ ≤ d₂ ≤ 12` with `|n| · 10^(d₂ − d₁) < 10¹⁵`, the number that reads as `n` at `d₁` reads as `n · 10^(d₂ − d₁)` at `d₂`. It is why a listing the view has not seen can change whether a quote is admitted and never what the view holds of it.
- **Laws:** L2, L3, L5, N1 restated over declared sources and proved; L1a ★ for the machine so far, its statement as it stands in `Laws.lean`.
- **The wall on L1a, and its answer** (the builder's stop at `kernel2-step4-wip` `536cc6c`; kernel L1a). The lemma sought, that events with `known > τ` can be dropped, is stronger than L1a and false of any machine that admits by the rules in force: without a listing not yet known, the next quote can fail admission. L1a has a second premise, and it holds that listing. Neither of the builder's options is taken: the view gets no rules of its own and reads nothing at release (a second clock for the rules, and a second reading of every number), and no law about two scales is needed (test 7 holds the fact as a property). The view holds what admission read (step 3's item 2). The proof, in order, each a lemma about the fold without admission (`advanceRec`), which an admitted run equals:
  1. **An admitted stream's times never fall** (`checkOrder`), so its events with `time ≤ τ − δ` are a prefix `A` and the rest a tail `B`. The two markets share `A` by the second premise, so both reach one state after it, with one list of ticks so far. By the first premise, `B₁` and `B₂` have the same events known by `τ`, in the same order.
  2. **In a tail, an event known by `τ` is no quote and no print:** admission gave those `known = time + δ`, above `τ`. So it is a listing, an end, a funding event or an observation, and what the view will take of it is a function of the event alone.
  3. **The view's part of a state at `τ`:** the header, the program, the next tick, the views, the evaluator's state, the pending events known by `τ`, and each coin's quote times at or before `τ − δ`. Pending is sorted by `known` in every reachable state.
  4. **Three steps keep two states equal in that part** while the next tick is at or before `τ`: a tick at or before `τ` taken by both, which also gives equal tick records; an event of item 2 applied to both; an event not known by `τ`, with `time > τ − δ`, applied to one alone. Ticks fire in the same number and at the same times in both whatever the part holds, since they read the next tick, the tick's length and an event's time.
  5. **Firing ticks up to a bound and then up to a later one is firing up to the later.** So, by induction on a tail, from any two states equal in that part: the tail's run and the run of its events known by `τ` give the same tick records through `τ`. An event not known is one side's step alone, after the ticks before its time, which the other side fires at its next event or at `finish`.
