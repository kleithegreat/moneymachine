## How every step runs

1. **Tests first, by a session that does not build the step,** from this brief and `kernel-v2.md`, without reading the code. Steps marked **◆** decide a judged number, so their tests go to **another model** than the builder's: GPT, or a fresh Opus session labeled as such when GPT is out.
2. **The builder (Sonnet) makes them pass without editing them,** in one commit. A test the builder believes wrong goes back to its author with the reason.
3. **A wall the brief did not foresee goes to the architect as a question.** No flag, tolerance, special case or second path (`CLAUDE.md` §1). Each step names the walls I can foresee.
4. **Opus checks the commit against its step,** and runs the step's checks itself.
5. **★ laws:** each step that proves one first commits its statement alone in `kernel2/lean/Engine/Laws.lean`. Another model reads the statements against part B3 before any proof, looking for one an empty or constant engine would satisfy, for one the transitions of part A contradict (round 2 found four, round 3 three more, round 4 three, round 5 T1 for a short), for one stated outside the records it holds on (round 5, K2), and for one written for buys or for order fills that is quantified over every fill. Then the proof; `checks.engine-proofs` prints each ★ law's axioms and admits only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

**Languages.** Lean 4.34.0 as the flake pins it, for everything that decides a number; core `Rat` for exact numbers. OCaml 5.4.1 with dune and Jane Street's stack (`core`, `async`, `ppx_jane`, `ppx_expect`, `base_quickcheck`, v0.17), `yojson`, `ocaml_sqlite3`, and hand-written C stubs. No Rust, no Python, no new flake input: every step's first test is that `flake.lock` is unchanged.

**Data a builder may read.** The archive before 2026-11-01 and the vault's fills before 2026-11-01. Nothing dated in [2026-11-01, 2027-01-31). From step 3 the executable enforces it (kernel A13); before that, and for every fixture of the recorder's format, data is synthetic.

**Layout.** `kernel2/lean` (Lake library `Engine`, no executable, no `IO`), `kernel2/ocaml` (dune project, executable `mm2`), `kernel2/KERNEL.md` (the spec beside the code: it holds A17, the program's grammar, A18, the market's encoding, and A19, the rows, and at step 0 part A of `kernel-v2.md` is moved there above them, with `kernel-v2.md` keeping parts B to D and linking to it).

### 1. ◆ Exact numbers and the two readings of a decimal (A2)

- **Builds:** `Engine.Exact`: grid rounding (a buy's price down, a sell's up, a size toward zero); the reading of a double's bits at a scale, and the nearest double of a rational, both by integer arithmetic. The putting of a placed decimal at a field's scale (A18). In OCaml, the placing of a decimal string's digits: the digits as one integer, and the count of those after the point, zeros at the end not counted.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. `10 × 0.9775` is `9.775` and rounds to itself as a buy on a grid of 0.001; `10 × 1.0056` to `10.056` as a sell; `1.93` coins are 193 lots and `0.29` are 29.
  2. For every scale from 0 to 12 and sampled `n` with `|n| < 10¹⁵`, the nearest double of `n · 10⁻ᵈ` reads back as `n`, and agrees with OCaml's `Float.of_string` on the written decimal.
  3. Rejected: `0.1 + 0.2` at every scale from 0 to 12; a NaN; an infinity; `10⁷` at scale 8 (its `n` is `10¹⁵`). Negative zero reads as zero.
  4. A string with more decimals than its scale is rejected by the engine, as is `"100.000000000000001"` at scale 8, which `Float.of_string` would have rounded to 100 (corrected by the advisor, 2026-11-16: the earlier `"100.00000000000001"` rounds to 100 + 2⁻⁴⁶, found by GPT) (Argon's finding 4). `"5.0"` at scale 0 is 5; `"5.01"` at scale 1 is rejected. The reader is given no scale.
  5. A value off the grid by any amount rounds in its direction and by less than one grid step.
  6. **The grid is the certificate's** (`venue/sources.toml`, "Price grid"; `KERNEL.md` A18), and this test is written from its quoted words and counts digits, where A18's step and its law count multiples. At five figures and one decimal: `123456` is on the grid and `12345.6` is not, a buy there rounding to 12,345 and a sell to 12,346; 99,999, 100,000 and 100,001 are on it and 100,000.5 is not, rounding to 100,000 and 100,001; a sell at 99,999.5 rounds to 100,000. For every price of `arithmetic.py.txt`'s `price_grid`, membership by the sentence equals membership by the step, a buy rounds to the greatest grid price at or below it, to zero when there is none, and a sell to the least at or above.
- **Laws ★:** X2, as a theorem on bits; the rounding bounds of test 5; that a price is a whole multiple of its step exactly when it is on the grid as A18's sentence says, significant figures being A18's multiples.
- **Foreseeable wall:** Lean's `Float` is opaque to proofs. X2 is about the integer functions on bits; `Float.toBits` stays in the trust boundary. The plain `readBits` and `onGrid` were built on `Rat` and met the wall of B5 at step 3, where their twins are specified (law X3).

### 2. ◆ The ledger, and the vault's fills through it (A8, A9, A11's arithmetic; study 2's layer 1)

- **Builds:** `Engine.Ledger`: cash, positions, cost; a fill, a fee, a funding payment, an absorption; the views `entry`, realized and unrealized profit, equity at given marks. An entry point that applies given fills with no venue. In OCaml, a reader of the fills file the advisor stores under `data/` (dated before 2026-11-01).
- **The tests must show:**
  1. One lot at 100 and two at 101, closed at 102, change cash by exactly 4 and never round; `entry` is `302/3`; a partial close leaves `entry` as it was; two opposite positions' funding sums to zero.
  2. Conservation (law A1) at every event of random sequences of fills, funding and one bankruptcy, as an equality of rationals with `absorbed` in it (GPT's finding 13).
  3. Every fee is its notional times its role's rate in the tier in force at its time, across a change of tier. A schedule that holds a rate below 0 or above 1 is refused, here by the replay and from step 3 by `start`; rates of 0 and of 1 are admitted (GPT, rounds 5 and 6).
  4. **Layer 1:** the vault's 5,806 fills, ordered by time and within a millisecond as the API's pages give them, are replayed by the ledger under the base tier, with each coin's `szDecimals` from `venue/hyperliquid/meta-2026-11-16.json`. The venue's printed figures are compared as whole numbers: sizes in lots of `10^−szDecimals`, prices in units of `10^(szDecimals − 6)`, money in millionths of a USDC. `cut(n, x)` is the venue's scaling of a whole number `n` by a double `x`: `Float.to_int (Float.of_int n *. x)`, the double product with its fraction dropped (facts §7; kernel V19, V20). Nothing is found on a sample, and no figure is rounded to be compared.
     - **Chain, grid, lots:** every `startPosition` is the ledger's position before the fill; every price is on its coin's grid (A18, at `figures` of 5 and `decimals` of `6 − szDecimals`) and every size a whole number of lots. This is the witness of V1 and V3, and a price off the grid means the certificate or the coin's rules are wrong.
     - **Fee:** with `N` the fill's lots times its price units and `r` the receipt's rate, the printed fee is `cut(N, r')`, `r'` the double nearest `r`; and the receipt's fee, in millionths, is at least the printed fee and at most one more. This is the witness of V4 and V19, and it is compatibility: a ledger that charged the printed fee would pass it. Test 3 and A1 hold the ledger's own fee (GPT's read, finding 3; kernel part G).
     - **`closedPnl`:** the test keeps the venue's entry notional `E` for each coin, zero when flat, from the fills alone. A buy adds `N`. A close of `s` lots from `p` at price `P` must print `s · (P − E div p)`, and then `E` becomes `cut(E, x)`, `x` the double nearest `(p − s) / p` as a ratio of whole lots, or zero when no lot is left. This is the witness of V20's arithmetic.
     - **The tie of `E` to the ledger is compatibility, asserted as arithmetic gives it.** Before every close, with `c` the ledger's `entry` times its position, in millionths, and `m` the partial closes since the coin was last flat: `E = c` when `m` is zero, and `c − 2m < E < c + m` otherwise. For an `E` under `2⁵¹` a double's product is within one of the exact product, so a cut leaves less than two under the exact remainder and less than one over it (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `layer1_read`). On this record every `E` is in the narrower `c − m ≤ E ≤ c`, and so every printed figure is at least the receipt's `closedPnl`: the test prints the count of each and asserts neither, because a product can round up (an `E` of 1,000,000,001 on 1,000,000,000 lots, one closed, leaves 1,000,000,000, above the exact cost). The tie does not show the ledger's cost right: a ledger that kept the venue's `E` would pass. Test 1 and A1 hold that.
     - **`cut`, on made-up numbers a rounding fails** (in the suite; the vault's fills are never fixtures): `cut(100000, 0.00015)` is 14, where the exact fee is 15; `cut(100000, 0.00045)` is 45. One lot at 100 and two at 101, at `szDecimals` of 0, closed at 102, print 4,000,002 millionths, where the ledger's `closedPnl` is 4. Eleven lots bought at 1,234 units have `E` of 13,574; closing 8 at 2,000 prints 6,128 and leaves `cut(13574, 3/11)`, which is 3,701 where the exact is 3,702; closing the last 3 at 2,000 prints 2,301, an entry of 1,233. A test oracle that scales `E` in whole numbers, or takes the exact fee's floor, fails these.
     - **Two wrong ledgers the ties accept and the exact assertions reject (GPT's read, finding 3),** written in the test as ledgers of their own: one charges the printed fee, the other keeps the venue's `E` as its cost after a partial close. On the made-up cases both pass the two ties, with 14 against a printed 14 and 3,701 against an `E` of 3,701. The engine's ledger must differ from each where it is wrong. Its fee on a notional of 100,000 millionths at the maker's rate is exactly 15 millionths, and a buy's cash falls by exactly 100,015. After 8 of 11 lots bought at 1,234 units are sold, its cost is exactly 3,702 and its `entry` 1,234; the last three, sold at 2,000, realize exactly 2,298, where the second wrong ledger gives 2,299 and the venue prints 2,301. A1's statement fails for each wrong ledger: the first's fee is not its notional times its rate, the second's `entry` is not the cost of the open position over its size.
     - The test prints every difference, and the counts the architect found on this record: 44 of 58 whole exact fees printed one under; 12 of 489 partial closes leaving `E` under its exact floor; 2,019 of 2,508 closes with `m` of zero; no `E` above `c`. The step is done at no difference.
  5. The replay is admitted like a run: a fill dated in an unreleased protected window rejects it, and its result is written through the same two ledger rows (from step 9; until then the test is marked as waiting on it).
- **Laws ★:** A1, U1.
- **Foreseeable walls:** a difference in layer 1 is a finding about V1, V3, V4, V19, V20 or the ledger. It goes to the architect with the fills that show it, and is never answered by a tolerance or by a rule refitted in the test. A difference in layer 1's funding (step 7, test 3) is a finding about V21, the rates or the fills on record, and goes the same way.

- **The tests must show:**
  1. C2: an event out of order, a crossed quote, a trade id repeated at one timestamp, a price off its coin's grid, a number out of range, a quote or a print with a price of zero, a settlement price below zero, an event of a coin after its listing's end (a funding event at the end's own millisecond included, since a listing comes first there), each put the state in a rejecting state that `finish` reports with no report and no trace.
  2. **Protected dates (GPT's finding 16):** a horizon meeting an unreleased window is refused by `start`; a well-formed event dated in one, fed under any horizon, rejects; `mm2 inspect` on such a file prints its size and hash, the count of its records and its stale spans, and no value of any record; no command-line argument or header field changes any of this; the tracked file is read as data, a list of windows each with its start, its end and whether it is released, and one that holds anything else does not build; a release in it changes the identity and not the engine's build, which hashes every source but that file and the store paths of the compilers and libraries, and a change of one byte of any other source, or of one of those paths, changes the build (A13; GPT's finding 10 and Argon's F3 on study 2's criteria).
  3. C1: any cutting of an event list into chunks gives the same final state and the same rows in the same order (property), a cut inside one timestamp that carries a tick included. The engine's state does not grow with the count of trades fed (GPT's finding 14). **Removed (architect, 2026-11-16):** the reader's refusal of a coin-day file in which a trade id repeats goes, with its test. A trade id is not a match's identity beyond its timestamp, where C2 holds it; a match is held to once in the record by its two orders, where prints are derived (step 6, test 10; architecture 6.4).
  4. Staleness is causal (GPT's finding 11): two markets equal through `t` are stale or not at `t` alike, whatever comes later; a coin is stale before its first quote and from `G` after its last. **The quote that ends a gap ends it for what follows it in A3's order (Argon's finding 5, round 5):** with `G` of a minute, a print at 60,010 ms that shares its millisecond with the first quote since 0 is stale, and a print at 60,020 is not.
  5. The reader equals `ingest/archive.py`, event for event, on 20 sampled coin-days across the three cadence periods, and its manifest check agrees with the archive's own record of short and missing hours (`missing.tsv`) on every sampled coin-day, flagging a short table built in the suite (corrected by the advisor, 2026-11-16: BTC's 2026-10-01 table, short when the brief was written, was rebuilt whole on 2026-11-12). A listing's grid is written from the coin's `szDecimals` alone, `figures` of 5 and `decimals` of `6 − szDecimals`, and a `szDecimals` above 6 stops the reader (A18).
  6. **The wall of B5 (kernel B5, as derived on 2026-11-16).** In the suite: the 16 coins of 2026-04-03, 2,487,176 quotes, are fed with no program in under 150 s on one core. Once, as a batch whose time and peak memory are written to disk and quoted in the step's report: the 16 coins over the 212 days in under 4 hours on one core, the process under 2 GB with the rows streamed out.
  7. **The fast readings change nothing a test can see:** every test of step 1 and tests 1 to 5 of this step pass unedited on the build that holds the twins, and `checks.engine-proofs` lists X3 with X2, C1 and C2 under the three admitted axioms.
- **Laws ★:** C1, C2, X3.
- **The wall was met, and this is its answer** (the builder's report at `kernel2-build` `4616aed`: 5.3 s for the reader alone on that day, 91 s for BTC's 155,453 quotes through `feed`, 1,700 quotes a second; kernel B1, B5). The steps, in order:
  1. **Profile first.** `perf` from the pinned nixpkgs joins the dev shell, the lock unmoved, and BTC's day is profiled to disk. That share is necessary and not enough, since at exactly nine tenths the twins would have to be 81 times faster (GPT, 2026-11-16); step 4's measurement decides.   2. **X3's statement, alone,** in `Laws.lean`: `@Exact.readBits = @Exact.readBitsFast` and `@Exact.onGrid = @Exact.onGridFast`. Its reader checks that each left side is the function X2 and C2 speak of, and that no law, instance or caller is restated on a twin.
  3. **The twins,** in `Engine.Exact`, by one builder in one commit: the same two computations on natural numbers and integers, with no `Rat`. A double is its mantissa and its exponent of two; a decimal its digits and its scale; a product or a comparison of two of them is carried on numerators over a denominator that is a power of two or ten, never reduced; the exponent is `Nat.log2`'s. `onGridFast` reads its rational's own numerator and denominator. Then the proofs, and `@[csimp]` on each equality, placed in `Engine.Exact` before any module that calls the two. **Nothing else changes:** no other definition, no statement, and no proof of X2, C1 or C2, which never mention a twin.

The two event counts are `flow` sources: defined at the tick they arrive and undefined after (Argon's finding 7).

### 5. Exact expressions, slots, and orders of one cycle (A5, A6)

- **Builds:** the exact sort and the three crossings in the checker and evaluator; slots; cycles; wishes and their arrival; effects, and own-state inputs as their fold `δ` late; admission, with margin's inputs given.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. A float literal where an exact one is required is a type error; `exact(f)` changes no value; a non-finite float makes its slot inactive. Two float evaluators that differ on a `when` place different orders, and two that agree on every `when` and every `exact(·)` argument place the same.
  2. The ladder of A5 as a program: given a first order filled at 10, its five wishes are at the published prices and targets exactly, on the grid. A first order that swept 0.1 at one level and 0.9 at the next gives `first_size = 1` and their mean price (Argon's finding 7).
  3. **A replacement does not buy twice:** a slot's target is 10; a price change at `τ` sends a new wish; the old order fills 6 before `τ + λ`; the new one fills at most 4, though the view still read `filled = 0` when it was wished.
  4. **`close` follows the position:** position 3 and a `close` take-profit that fills 1 has remainder 2; a level filling meanwhile raises the remainder with no replacement; a `close` wished while flat places nothing and is sent again; a buy slot of size `close` on a long places nothing (Argon's finding 2, round 4).
  5. A target below what has filled places nothing. A target weight written as two `ioc` slots tracks 10, 15, 8, then 12 without going flat; with a sale still in flight it reaches 12 at the first tick that sees the sale (Argon's finding 5, refuted as stranding).
  6. **One wish a slot a tick (GPT's finding 10):** with `λ` longer than two deciding ticks, wishes arrive in the order sent and no order is placed twice; an arriving wish equal to the live order changes nothing; an `ioc` that found nothing is sent again at the next tick; a refused order is tried again by the next wish.
  7. Orders placed together are admitted in A6's order, and together means arriving in one millisecond, in whatever order their slots are written (`KERNEL.md` A18; GPT's finding 14 on study 2's criteria); each refusal names which condition failed; a sell that only reduces a long needs no margin; an order's requirement holds its taker fee; a remainder under 10 USD is refused unless it closes the position exactly.
  8. **An order ends with its cycle (GPT's finding 3, Argon's 2).** GPT's construction: two units, a slot that has filled its one, its replacement on the way, a `close` at 40 that fills: the replacement places nothing, and the account loses 120, not 220. Argon's: a live buy of target 10 with 9 filled is cancelled at the fill that ends the cycle, and any number of later prints buy nothing. A new cycle's first order is placed no sooner than `δ + λ` after the old one's end. **A cycle ends at the fill (Argon's finding 4, round 4):** a `reduce_only` sell and a buy of one cycle arrive at one quote, the sell takes the position to zero, and the buy is cancelled before it fills, with the buy's slot written first in the program as with the sell's.
  9. **A wish has no grid of its own (GPT's finding 5, round 4):** a buy wished at 1.0001 while `szDecimals` is 2 arrives after a listing event made it 3: the live order was cancelled at the event, and the wish places an order at 1.000. The position was open throughout, so no cycle ended.
  10. **Effects are everything (GPT's finding 10, Argon's 5):** on random runs, the view's `pos`, `filled`, `resting`, `resting_price` and equity equal the world's `δ` earlier at every tick, across an order placed with no fill, an `ioc` remainder cancelled, a refusal, a cycle's end, a liquidation and a bankruptcy. **At `δ = 0` the view is the world as the tick finds it (Argon's finding 5 on study 2's criteria):** an order placed by a wish that arrives in a tick's own millisecond is in the view of the next tick and not of that one, and L1a holds there.
- **Laws ★:** X1, R1, S1.

### 6. ◆ Fills: arrivals on quotes, resting orders on prints (A7)

- **Builds:** arrival; the trade event; resting orders and the two rules; allotment; fees through the schedule; `high`, `low`, `traded`; the coin's price, which a print moves; the count of taker fills near an earlier one (V10's diagnostic); the reader of the recorder's streams through `mmrec cat`; the reader of the archive's fills by block (`node_fills_by_block`), which holds the stream to its form, derives prints as architecture 6.4 says, writes a table of them for each coin and day, and checks that no two prints of a coin share both orders over the days read.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. F0: for one order, each rule's fill is A7's, for every case of `b`, `a`, `p`, `tif`, remainder, print price and aggressor, **with the sells written out and not derived from the buys' fixtures:** a resting sell at 100 fills under `pessimistic` on a buy-aggressor print at 101 (GPT's finding 7).
  2. **One seller, once:** five levels and one seller standing 14.68 units below the lowest through five snapshots fill nothing under `pessimistic`. The same seller selling 14.68 in one sell-aggressor print fills 14.68 in all, best price first. A buyer lifting him fills nothing. A snapshot followed by a print of the same lots fills them once.
  3. **The burden of proof (Argon's finding 4):** a print between an order's placement and its arrival quote fills it under `optimistic` and not under `pessimistic`. A buy that joins the best bid, book unchanged at the next quote, fills under neither. A quote whose ask is at or below a resting buy fills it under `optimistic` only.
  4. Two orders arriving at one quote take a level once, best level first, in admission order. Two `ioc` buys arriving at two snapshots of one standing seller both fill, and the second is in the report's count (V10, named).
  5. A `pessimistic` venue on a market with no trade source is refused by `start`, and so is a venue whose `λ` or `δ` is below zero, whose staleness length is below 1, whose cap is not above zero or whose minimum is below zero. A `reduce_only` fill is cut to the position and its rest cancelled, under both rules.
  6. L1b: no fill on an event with `time ≤ τ + λ`; an order replaced at `τ` can still fill until `τ + λ`.

      - **Spans.** A mix with a negative weight, weights that sum above 1, or a weight in a coin that is not its span's is refused. A coin with no price yet is not bought.
