### 1. ◆ The verdicts, in Lean (CS2.1 to CS2.3, architecture 6.5)

- **Builds,** as pure functions of `Engine`, exported like `confirm`:
  - **`entries`,** from a report's rows and a span of time, to the fills that find their coin's position at zero: each `flow` row of a fill whose `before` is zero and whose `time` is in the span, as its coin and its time;
  - **`chained`,** from the vault's fills as the replay of given fills wrote them and each fill's `startPosition`, to "chained" or the first fill whose `startPosition` is not its row's `before` (wall 1);
  - **`matched`,** from two lists of entries and a window, to the size of the largest set of pairs, one entry of each list, of one coin and at most the window apart, no entry in two pairs;
  - **`held`,** from `confirm`'s results on the replay and on the forward run (each one's `W` at its days' ends and the day its ledger ended, if it did), to "held", "did not hold" or "not judged" with its reason, and the two variances. It reads the forward run's first 212 days and the replay's every day, and of a ledger's end only whether it fell on one of those days. It reads no `Wᵇ`. A day's return is its `W` over the `W` before, less one, the first from 1; a variance is of a list of returns about its own mean, divided by its count;
  - **`rules`,** from a report's coin rows at a day's end, to the guard's two mixes for each span: equal weights over the span's coins; and the bar's own mix, each coin's weight in [0, 1] where its term of the bound is greatest at the least multiplier, the lesser at a tie, all divided by their sum when that is above 1. Each weight is then multiplied by 24/25;
  - **`refused`,** from the wealth over the bar of each of the guard's rules at each day's end of the replay, to whether rung 2 is refused: one of them is 20 or more at some day's end, or its 730th power at the last day's end is at least 20 raised to the count of days.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. **An entry is a first fill:** a cycle's first fill, a partial one included, is an entry and its later fills are none; a fill at the span's first millisecond is in and one at its last is out; a liquidation's or a settlement's row is none.
  2. **A window includes its end:** entries of one coin at 0 and at 360,000 ms match under a window of six minutes, and at 360,001 they do not. Entries of two coins never match.
  3. **No entry is in two pairs:** one entry of the vault's at 0 and two of the replica's at five minutes before and after give 1.
  4. **The count is the largest there is:** the vault's at 0 and 7 minutes and the replica's at 5 and 13 give 2. A function that pairs nearest first gives 1, and fails. On random lists of up to eight entries a side the count equals that of a search over every set of pairs, and it does not depend on the order of either list.
  5. **`held` is of the variance, exactly (GPT's finding 2).** The two variances are rationals and are compared by cross-multiplying. Replay returns of +0.01 and −0.01 in turn against forward returns of +0.03 and +0.01 in turn hold, though their mean squares are 5 apart. A forward variance of exactly half the replay's and one of exactly twice it hold. One of twice it and `10⁻⁴⁰` more does not, and both variances are returned.
  6. **What is not judged (GPT's finding 3, Argon's 2):** a forward list of 211 days; a ledger that ended, or a wealth of zero, within either list; a replay of no variance, whether the forward variance is zero or not. Each names its reason. A replay with variance and a forward list that never moved "did not hold". Of a forward list of 300 days the first 212 alone are read: changing a later day changes nothing, and a ledger that ended on day 213 is judged as one that never ended (Argon's F1).
  7. **`rules`:** every weight is at least zero, a span's weights sum to at most 24/25, and a coin that is not its span's has none. On brief 3's market of pairs the bar's own mix is 12/25 in each coin. Two coins with one return of 3 each, where the bound is greatest at both ends of a coin's term at once, give the lesser weight. On random sums no multiplier of a fine grid gives a lower bound than the one the mix is taken at.
  8. **`refused` is exact (Argon's finding 1):** over 212 days a rule that ends at `239/100` refuses and one at `238/100` does not; a rule that stood at 25 on day 10 and has nothing at the end refuses; four rules of which one refuses, refuse.
  9. **Added by the last pass:** `chained` on three fills of one coin whose second's `startPosition` is a lot off names the second, and on the same fills in order is "chained"; two coins' chains do not mix. The replay of given fills writes one `flow` row a fill, its `price` undefined, and `entries` of those rows are the first fills (wall 1).
- **Laws ★:**
  - **M1** `matched` returns the size of a set of pairs that meets its three conditions, and no such set is larger.
  - **M2** `held` returns "held" exactly when both lists are judged and the forward variance is from half the replay's to twice it, and "not judged" exactly in the cases of test 6.
  - **M3** `refused` is true exactly when some rule meets one of its two conditions.
  - **M4** `chained` returns "chained" exactly when every fill's `startPosition` is the sum of its coin's fills before it.
- **Walls foreseen** (architect, 2026-11-16), each answered:
  1. **The vault's entries had no rows and its chain no judge.** CS2.3 reads the vault's entries "the same way" from its fills "replayed through the ledger", and is not judged when that replay fails, "a missing fill among them". Brief 3's replay of given fills returns receipts and writes no row, and the check of `startPosition` lives in layer 1's test, which is OCaml: a verdict would rest on a test's code. So the replay of given fills writes A19's `flow` rows, one a fill, with `price` undefined, since it has no market; and `chained` is Lean's. For CS2.3, `study2 forward` gives `chained` the vault's fills from its first on record through the 61st judged day's end, as `mmvault` and the copy before 2026-11-01 hold them at the judging replay's capture, in step 7, wall 3's order, and CS2.3 is "not judged" where it names a fill or where their replay rejects them (two versions of one fill, brief 3, step 2). Layer 1's test calls the same `chained`.
  2. **M1's second half is an optimality proof,** and the builder needs its lemmas. Entries of two coins never pair, so the count is a sum over coins, and for one coin both lists are sorted by time. With `a` and `b` the earliest of each: if they are within the window, some largest set holds the pair `(a, b)` (in a largest set one of them is paired, or the pair could be added; if `a` is paired with `b'` and `b` with `a'`, then `(a, b)` and `(a', b')` are within the window too, since `a ≤ a'` and `b ≤ b'`; if only one is paired, its partner is exchanged); if `a` is more than the window before `b`, it pairs with nothing and is dropped, and the same for `b`. So the count obeys one recursion on the two sorted lists, and M1 is an induction on their lengths. A set of pairs is a list of index pairs with no index twice. No Mathlib is needed.
  3. `refused` raises a ratio to the 730th power as a rational; its numerator has some hundred thousand bits, which exact arithmetic carries. No rounding is put there. **`held`'s variance** is of 212 returns, each over the wealth of the day before, so its denominator holds about 212 wealths' numerators, twice: 157,000 bits at a wealth of 381 bits and 378,000 at 900, about what two years at the vault's rate of fills of several prices reach (kernel A12), in proportion to a wealth's bits. That is under a minute in Python's own integers, and Lean's are GMP's (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `last_pass_r3`; Argon's finding 4.2, the last pass's round 3). No rounding is put there either: M2 compares exactly.

### 2. The ladder (architecture 6.1; `facts-kernel-v2.md`, section 7)

- **Builds:** `study2/replica.json` without its trigger: for each coin of the universe, a first buy of 100 USD, five resting buys below the first fill, and one take-profit above the cycle's average entry, the levels and the exit written over the two `let`s of wall 1. The header's leverage is 1 and its clock the table's.
- **Its constants** are read off the vault's fills before 2026-09-01: each level's price against the first fill's and its size in coins against the first buy's, and the take-profit's distance. The facts give them to three or four digits, over all seven months (levels at 2.25, 3.54, 5.04, 7.00 and 9.85% below; sizes of 1, 1.93, 3.80, 7.47 and 14.68 first buys; the exit 0.56% above). The builder fixes the digits that reproduce the most orders exactly. The anchor and the rounding are not the builder's (wall 2).
- **The tests must show:**
  1. On a synthetic market in which a first buy fills, the trace holds five resting buys at the constants' prices on the coin's grid, rounded down, and sizes in whole lots, rounded toward zero, and one sale of the whole position at the take-profit's price, rounded up.
  2. When a level fills, the take-profit moves to the new average entry's price and is one order for the whole position. When it fills, the cycle ends, every level is cancelled, and nothing of the old cycle fills after (kernel A6).
  3. **No order of a full ladder is refused (Argon's finding 4):** on a market where all 17 coins fall through every level together and then rise through their exits, the trace holds no `refused` row with the reason `margin` or `cap` and none of any reason for a level or the exit, every level and every exit fills, and the account's cash is never negative before funding (wall 5).
  4. **The counter of test 5 counts:** a synthetic vault cycle generated from the constants is counted as exact at every level and at its exit; with one level a grid step lower it is counted as that level's price differing, and with one size a lot more as that level's size.
  5. **The ladder given real first buys (D14), a measurement:** for each of the vault's cycles from 2026-09-01 to 10-31, a synthetic market in which the entry, set to the real first buy's size (wall 5), fills at its prices. It prints, by level, how many of the real level orders have the replica's price exactly and how many its size exactly, and the same for the exits. Its passing shows that every real cycle was counted, and nothing about fidelity: the table does.
- **A wall, not a criterion:** at each level and at the exit, more than half of the real orders have the replica's price exactly, and more than half its size. If not, the table goes to the architect before the trigger is fitted.
- **Not replicated, and counted:** levels beyond the fifth (9 cycles), the vault's exits other than the take-profit (97 fills), a first buy whose size is not the constant notional.
- **Walls foreseen** (architect, 2026-11-16, against `KERNEL.md` A17 and the facts' section 7), each answered:
  1. **Where the ladder ends and the trigger begins.** The levels and the exit read the first buy through two `let`s: `anchor`, a price, and `unit`, a size. Step 2 writes them as `first_price` and `first_size`, and writes the entry as one slot whose `when` is false. Step 3 writes the entry's slots and those two `let`s, and nothing else of the program. So what a first buy filled in part does (how long it stays, and whether the levels are sized on the opening order's fills or on the slot's, `filled(slot)`) is the fit's, said in the language, and the ladder's constants are not the fit's to move.
  2. **The anchor is `first_price`,** the opening order's cost over its size. A fill's row holds no limit price (facts §7, the fill fields), so "the first order's limit" can be read off no record, and the language has no input for it. The facts' medians were measured against that mean. **The rounding is the engine's** (A2: a buy down, a sell up, a size toward zero); a program cannot round. Test 5's table therefore counts, beside the exact, the orders one grid step or one lot off, by direction. Misses that are all one step in one direction mean the vault rounds otherwise: that is reported, the engine gains no rounding, and no constant is bent to hide it.
  3. **The levels and the exit are `gtc`.** The vault's level orders were takers in 0.018 of their fills and its take-profits in 0.010 (facts §7), and a post-only order never takes. A5's example writes `alo`; this ladder does not. A `gtc` level that arrives with the ask at or under it takes at the ask.
  4. **A slot refused at every tick writes a row at every tick** (A6: the next wish tries again; A19), 86,400 a day at this clock. No rule of the engine changes: a wish that would be refused is the program's not to send. Each level's `when` holds that its target at its price is at least the venue's minimum, a `param`. An entry's own remainder can still be refused once or twice: a notional over a price that fell leaves dust above what filled, in the `δ + λ` before the view sees the fill (A6: a remainder under the minimum is refused).
  5. **The tests enter without a trigger.** They set the entry's `when` to a declared `state` source that the test's market holds above zero for one tick, with a price of `ask` and `ioc`. Test 3 asserts what Argon's finding was about, the ladder's own orders, and not "no row of any reason", which wall 4's dust would break by design. Test 5's entry is an `ioc` of the real first buy's size in coins at its worst fill's price, on a book that holds the real fills' sizes at their prices, so that `first_size` and `first_price` are the real order's; a first buy of the constant notional cannot fill at another's size.

### 5. The bootstrap (architecture 6.3; D45)

It forecasts power and chooses `φ` and `d`. It judges nothing: every pair is valid (law K1), and CS2.2 is judged by `held`.

- **Builds,** in Lean but for the digests and the count (wall 1): from the replay's table, for each pair, 10,000 paths of 730 days made of the replay's days.
  - **A path** is 53 blocks of 14 consecutive days of the replay, the last cut to 2, each running past the replay's last day to its first. Block `j` of path `i`, both counted from 0, starts at the day whose place is the BLAKE2b-256 digest of the ASCII text `study2 bootstrap i j`, read as one whole number, most significant byte first, modulo the replay's days. No seed is registered, and the paths are the same for every pair.
  - **Why blocks of 14 days.** A choice: two weeks, and longer than the vault's longest cycle, 10.6 days, so that most cycles' days fall in one block. A block's edge can still cut a cycle, and a path does not keep the weekday. The forecast is also made at blocks of 7 and 28 days, reported beside.
  - **A day of the replay** gives a pair two numbers: its factor, `W` at its end over `W` at the end of the day before, the first from 1; and its share at the bid, `Wᵇ` over `W` at its end, which is from 0 to 1. A pair whose ledger ended on the replay, or was worth nothing at a day's end of it, has neither and no path.
  - **On a path,** `W` at a day's end is the product of the factors of its days so far, and `Wᵇ` is that `W` times that day's share at the bid. So a path's `Wᵇ` is never above its `W`, as on the record. A product of `Wᵇ`'s own factors is not that: a block that starts on the day after a bid was pulled takes the recovery without the fall, and on a book whose price never moved 0.197 of the paths reach 20 (GPT's R3, `arithmetic.py.txt`).
  - **A day's slice.** The report's coin rows hold running figures, since their span began (`KERNEL.md` A19): the count of events, the funding a unit paid and the three sums. A row adds its figure less that of the coin's row before it in the same span, or the whole figure where it is its span's first row. A day's slice of a figure is what the coin's rows after the day before's `day` row, up to its own, added. Each term of a sum was rounded before it was added (kernel A12), so a slice is exact. A day's price ratio is the coin's `price` in its `day` row over that in the day before's, and for the first day over the `first` of the coin's first row.
  - **The bar on a path** is `confirm`'s own, over one span of the coins that have a price in every day's rows of the replay; the forecast names any coin left out. Each such coin starts the path at its first price of the replay with figures of nothing. Each day of the path adds its slices of the count and of the three sums, moves the price by its ratio, and adds its slice of the funding times the path's price over the record's, which is one number at the day's start and at its end.
  - **The rungs on a path.** Rung 1 is reached where `Wᵇ` is 20 or more at a day's end, rung 2 where it is 20 times the bar or more. The bar is at least 1, so it is computed only where `Wᵇ` is 20 or more.
  - **The forecast:** each rung's share of the 10,000 paths, for each pair.
  - **The choice:** the pair with the greatest share at the top registered rung; ties to the greatest `Wᵇ` at the replay's end, then the smaller `φ`, then the smaller `d`. A pair with no path is never chosen.
- **The tests must show:**
  1. Two runs give the same paths. The digest of the empty text is the published one, and block 0 of path 0 starts where an independent computation of the digest says. Over 10,000 paths each day of a record of 212 starts a block within four standard errors of equally often.
  2. **Exact cases,** with every bid at its price. A record whose every day returns `r`: every path reaches 20 when `(1 + r)^730 ≥ 20` and none does below it. A record whose days all return nothing but one, whose `W` multiplies by 20: the share of paths that reach 20 is within three standard errors of `1 − (1 − 14/D)^52 (1 − 2/D)`, `D` the record's days.
  3. A record in which no coin's price moves and no funding is paid has a bar of 1 on every path, so the two rungs' shares are equal. One in which a coin doubles every day has no path at rung 2. A day's funding is scaled the same whether by the prices at its start or at its end.
  4. A coin with no price in some day's rows of the replay is left out of the bar and named.
  5. The choice, on tables built on each side of each tie; a pair whose ledger ended, and one worth nothing at a day's end, are passed over though their shares would be the greatest.
  6. **A day's slice (GPT's R2).** A coin that returns 0.01 on the first of 212 days and never moves again: its slices of the first sum are 0.01 and then nothing, and a path's first sum is 0.01 for each time it holds the first day, where the rows added as they stand give 7.3. A day with no event of a coin adds nothing to its count, its sums or its funding. Rows over three spans, one ending inside a day and one at a midnight, with first sums of 0.030, 0.007 and 0.001: the four days' slices are 0.010, 0.025, 0 and 0.003, and a span's row that stands after the last day's adds nothing (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `day_slices`).
  7. **A path's `Wᵇ` is never above its `W` (GPT's R3).** One coin held whole at a constant price, its bid a hundredth of a percent under it but for one midnight at a hundredth of the price: on every path `W` is 1 at every day's end, `Wᵇ` is at most 0.9999, and no path reaches a rung. A bootstrap that multiplies `Wᵇ`'s factors reaches 20 on about a fifth of the paths, and fails.
- **Walls foreseen** (architect, 2026-11-16), each answered:
  1. **A path's arithmetic is Lean's.** A slice is a difference of exact rationals, a path's `W` a product of 730 of them, and the bar `confirm`'s own. The pin gives OCaml no exact rationals (`flake.nix`: no `zarith` among `mm2`'s inputs), and no input is added. So `Engine.Study2` holds one more pure function, from the replay's rows, a pair and a list of 730 days to that path's `W`, `Wᵇ` and, where `Wᵇ` is 20 or more, bar at each day's end. OCaml computes the digests, calls it and counts. BLAKE2b-256 is `digestif`'s, which the pin has held since brief 3's step 3.
  2. **The replay's days are the record's, its holes included** (step 4, wall 1): the forecast is of that record, held and flat through its holes, and says so beside its shares, with the replay's stale share by coin. A forward period with fewer holes moves the wealth's volatility, and so the power the forecast gave; that is what CS2.2 measures.
- **Foreseeable walls:**
  - 10,000 paths through `confirm`'s bar. If they do not finish within a day on one host, report the time to the architect. The count of paths is not the builder's to lower. A day's factor carries the ledger's denominators, which grow at its fills of several prices (kernel A12), but a block's days telescope, so a path's `W` is a product of 53 ratios of two wealths: 20,000 bits at a wealth of 381 bits and 48,000 at 900, 24 and 100 ms a path in Python's own integers, 2 and 8 hours for the 30 pairs' 10,000 paths (`arithmetic.py.txt`, `last_pass_r3`; Argon's finding 4.2, the last pass's round 3). The step's report gives the largest factor's bits beside the time.
  - A replay with a return below minus one in any row has no bar from that day on (K2), and a slice does not say which day of a span held it. No path is made of such a replay: it goes to the architect.

- **Walls foreseen** (architect, 2026-11-16), each answered or reported:
  1. **A replay is no longer an hour.** Each month's replay runs from the first judged midnight. At B5's wall of 22,500 quotes a second the two years' 1.95 billion quotes are a day on one core, so a replay takes about an hour for each month run so far.
  2. **A change of a coin's `szDecimals` ends the test.** The listings copy is hourly, so the event that carries the new rules is timed up to an hour off, a recorded trade crosses it, and admission rejects the record there (brief 3, the recorders' table); CS2.1 then ends at the last day run.   3. **What a day's end showed stands only where every replay reads the same events before it** (CS2.1's definitions). L1 compares two runs whose markets agree before `t`, and a record can grow behind a day already run: an item written late, or a value restated. Four rounds tried to stop that by fixing what a day's record is: sources made complete, a deadline the writers stamped, a reading fixed 31 days late, and a dated revision of the criteria. It needs a replay never to publish a day's end other than the one shown, so:
     - **The record as it is.** A replay reads every file of the writers it reads as they were at its capture: the recorder's, the settled-funding collector's and the listings copy's, and, for the vault's fills, `mmvault`'s and the copy from before 2026-11-01. A file is only ever appended to (`CLAUDE.md`, Running things). Each file's length and hash at the capture are in the identity (kernel A13), and every run reads each file as its first that many bytes, a file cut there: whole lines only, a trailing partial frame or line not read and the file counted as cut (`mmrec cat`, `recorder/RECORDER.md`; Argon's finding 3.2.3, round 7). The replay reads the writers' own files, or those bytes streamed and checked against their hashes, never a copy that lags them.
     - **One event, one item.** An item is the event a line holds, as the engine or the replay of given fills reads it: a book, a trade of a frame, a row of a pull's response, a listing. **A quote, a trade, a settlement and a vault fill are made from the line's message alone, never from its envelope,** a frame's or a pull's `recv` and `request`; a listing, whose source states no time, is timed at the first copy that shows it (Argon's finding 3.2.1, round 7):
       - a quote's and a trade's `time` are the venue's stamp, and their `known` is `time + δ`;
       - a settlement is its coin, its `time` and its rate: its hour is its `time`'s floor and its `known` that time plus the header's lag (`KERNEL.md` A18). `premium` is read by nothing;
       - a vault fill is the fields the replay of given fills reads, parsed, its decimals as the values A18 places, so `"5.0"` is `"5"` (brief 3, step 2).
       - Only events with `w_start ≤ time < w_end` are fed. The writers' files hold events past the horizon's end, and the rest are counted (Argon's finding 3.2.2, round 7).

The engine rejects a second settlement of a coin's hour (A18) and a print id repeated at its time (C2), and the run ends there. The coin is in the key as it is in a print's and a settlement's; on the venue's data it adds nothing, since an order is of one coin (Argon's finding 4.1, round 7).

Each run computes it from the identity's bytes, the pull's start from that pull's own `start` event.

     - **The capture: one reading a month** (GPT's finding 2, the last pass's round 5; Argon's findings 1.2 and 3.1, round 7). A timer fires at 12:00 UTC (07:00 EST) on the first of each month, and then at each whole hour. No firing waits on a run. Each firing:

A pull that fails, is killed or is not complete captures nothing, and the next firing pulls again.
